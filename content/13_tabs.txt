## {story} කථා වස්තුව
### උජ්ඣාපනක වස්තුව
@ රජගහ නුවර · වේළුවනය කලන්දකනිවාපය
:::short
මෙත්තිය-භූමජක භික්ෂූන්ට හීන සෙනසුන් හා බත් ලැබුණු හෙයින්, ඔව්හු “දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත තෙරුන් කැමැත්තෙන් සෙනසුන් හා බත් බෙදති” යි ==භික්ෂූන් ලවා ඔහු ගැන අවමන් කරවූහ==. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක.
:::
:::long
එක් සමයෙක බුදුරජාණන් වහන්සේ රජගහ නුවර වේළුවනයෙහි කලන්දකනිවාපයෙහි වැඩ වසන සේක. එකල ආයුෂ්මත් **දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත** තෙරුන් සංඝයාට සෙනසුන් පනවති, බත් ද උදෙසති (බෙදා දෙති). එකල මෙත්තිය-භූමජක භික්ෂූහු නවකයෝ ද අල්ප පුණ්‍යවත්හු ද වූහ. සංඝයාගේ යම් හීන සෙනසුන් වේ ද, ඒවා ඔවුනට ලැබේ; හීන බත් ද ලැබේ.
ඔව්හු “දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත තෙරුන් ==කැමැත්තෙන් (පක්ෂපාතයෙන්) සෙනසුන් පනවයි, කැමැත්තෙන් බත් උදෙසයි==” යි කියමින් ආයුෂ්මත් දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත තෙරුන් ගැන භික්ෂූන් ලවා අවමන් කරවූහ (**උජ්ඣාපෙන්ති**).
අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු “මෙත්තිය-භූමජක භික්ෂූහු කෙසේ නම් ආයුෂ්මත් දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත තෙරුන් ගැන භික්ෂූන් ලවා අවමන් කරවන්නාහු ද?” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “මහණෙනි, ඔබ දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත ගැන භික්ෂූන් ලවා අවමන් කරවන බව සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත ගැන භික්ෂූන් ලවා අවමන් කරවන්නහු ද? මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක:
:::
!pn: මුල් පැනවීම
> උජ්ඣාපනකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
~ සම්මත භික්ෂුවක් ගැන අනුන් ලවා අවමන් කරවීමෙහි පචිති වේ.
### ඛිය්‍යනක වස්තුව
:::short
ඉන්පසු ඔව්හු භික්ෂූන් අසල ==ඔහු ගැන දොස් කීහ==. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ නැවත ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය සම්පූර්ණ කළ සේක.
:::
:::long
එකල මෙත්තිය-භූමජක භික්ෂූහු “භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ අවමන් කරවීම (**උජ්ඣාපනක**) පමණක් ප්‍රතික්ෂේප කළ සේක; මෙපමණකින් භික්ෂූහු (අප කියන දෙය) අසනු ඇත” යි සිතා, භික්ෂූන් ළඟදී ම ආයුෂ්මත් දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත තෙරුන් ගැන “කැමැත්තෙන් සෙනසුන් පනවයි, කැමැත්තෙන් බත් උදෙසයි” යි ==දොස් කීහ (**ඛිය්‍යන්ති**)==.
අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු “මෙත්තිය-භූමජක භික්ෂූහු කෙසේ නම් ආයුෂ්මත් දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත තෙරුන් ගැන දොස් කියන්නාහු ද?” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “මහණෙනි, ඔබ දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත ගැන දොස් කියන බව සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් දබ්බ මල්ලපුත්ත ගැන දොස් කියන්නහු ද? මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක:
:::
!pn: අනුපඤ්ඤත්තිය — අවසන් පැනවීම (සම්පූර්ණ ශික්ෂාපදය)
> උජ්ඣාපනකෙ ඛිය්‍යනකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
~ ලමු කොට සිත වීමෙහි නුගුණ කීමෙහි පචිති වේ.

# උමතු වූවහුට (**උම්මත්තකස්ස**);

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of Making Others Look Down (Ujjhāpanaka-vatthu)
@ Rājagaha · Veḷuvana, the Squirrels’ Feeding Place
:::short
Because the poorest lodgings and meals fell to them, the Mettiya and Bhūmajaka monks ==made the monks look down on Ven. Dabba Mallaputta==, saying he assigned lodgings and meals out of favouritism. The Blessed One rebuked them and laid down the rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Rājagaha, in the Bamboo Grove at the Squirrels’ Feeding Place. At that time Ven. **Dabba Mallaputta** was assigning lodgings for the Saṅgha and allotting meals. The Mettiya and Bhūmajaka monks were newly ordained and of little merit; the poorest lodgings of the Saṅgha and the poorest meals came to them.
They made the monks look down on Ven. Dabba Mallaputta (**ujjhāpenti**), saying: “==Dabba Mallaputta assigns lodgings out of favouritism, and allots meals out of favouritism.==”
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can the Mettiya and Bhūmajaka monks make the monks look down on Ven. Dabba Mallaputta?” The Blessed One asked: “Is it true, monks, that you make the monks look down on Dabba Mallaputta?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked them: “Foolish men, how can you make the monks look down on Dabba Mallaputta? Foolish men, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule:
:::
!pn: The original rule
> උජ්ඣාපනකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
~ For making others look down: Pācittiya.
### The Matter of Complaining (Khiyyanaka-vatthu)
:::short
They then ==complained about him near the monks==. The Blessed One rebuked them again and completed the rule.
:::
:::long
Then the Mettiya and Bhūmajaka monks, thinking, “The Blessed One has prohibited only making others look down; with just this the monks will hear us,” ==complained (**khiyyanti**) about Ven. Dabba Mallaputta near the monks==: “Dabba Mallaputta assigns lodgings out of favouritism, and allots meals out of favouritism.”
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can the Mettiya and Bhūmajaka monks complain about Ven. Dabba Mallaputta?” The Blessed One asked: “Is it true, monks, that you complain about Dabba Mallaputta?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked them: “Foolish men, how can you complain about Dabba Mallaputta? Foolish men, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule:
:::
!pn: Supplementary rule — the final rule (complete training rule)
> උජ්ඣාපනකෙ ඛිය්‍යනකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
~ For causing disparagement or complaint: Pācittiya.
## {story} Word analysis — ujjhāpanaka and khiyyanaka
%% About whom | About a fully ordained monk authorised by the Saṅgha (**saṅghena sammataṃ**) —
%% Office | an assigner of lodgings (**senāsanapaññāpaka**), an allotter of meals (**bhattuddesaka**), a distributor of gruel (**yāgubhājaka**), of fruit (**phalabhājaka**), of snacks (**khajjabhājaka**), or a giver of small items (**appamattakavissajjaka**) —
%% Intention | wishing to discredit him (**avaṇṇaṃ kattukāmo**), to bring him into disrepute (**ayasaṃ kattukāmo**), to put him to shame (**maṅkukattukāmo**),
%% Act | one who makes a fully ordained monk look down on him (**ujjhāpeti**) or complains (**khiyyati**),
%% Result | incurs a **pācittiya**.
## {story} Offences — by the person and the authorisation
|! About whom | To whom he speaks | Offence
| A fully ordained monk authorised by the Saṅgha | Makes a fully ordained monk look down / complains | PC
| A fully ordained monk authorised by the Saṅgha | Makes an unordained person look down / complains | DK
| A fully ordained monk not authorised by the Saṅgha (assigner of lodgings etc.) | To a fully ordained or an unordained person | DK
| An unordained person, authorised or not (assigner of lodgings etc.) | To a fully ordained or an unordained person | DK
## {story} Offences — by the act and by perception
|! Act of authorisation | Perception | Offence
| A legitimate act | Perceiving it as legitimate, he makes others look down / complains | PC
| A legitimate act | Doing so while in doubt | PC
| A legitimate act | Doing so perceiving it as illegitimate | PC
| An illegitimate act | Doing so perceiving it as legitimate | DK
| An illegitimate act | Doing so while in doubt | DK
| An illegitimate act | Doing so perceiving it as illegitimate | DK
## {story} No offence
# He makes others look down on, or complains about, one who by nature acts out of desire, hatred, delusion or fear;
# For one who is insane (**ummattakassa**);
# For the first offender (**ādikammikassa**).
## {att} Points on words in the origin story
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Ujjhāpenti** | Saying “Dabba Mallaputta out of favouritism …” and so on, they make those monks disregard that venerable (**avajānāpenti**), look at him with contempt, or think of him as low. There is also the reading “**ojjhāpenti**”, with the same meaning.
| **Chandāya** | Out of desire, out of partiality — the idea is: he assigns the best ones to his own friends and companions.
| **Khiyyanti** | They make it known, saying “Dabba Mallaputta out of favouritism …” and so on.
| **Ujjhāpanake khiyyanake** | The words by which they make others look down are ujjhāpanaka; the words by which they complain are khiyyanaka. **Pācittiya** — two pācittiyas are stated for the two matters.
## {att} How the words of the analysis connect
- The words “assigner of lodgings … giver of small items” connect with “**maṅkukattukāmo**” (wishing to put to shame).
- When connecting with “wishing to discredit, wishing to bring into disrepute”, “upasampannaṃ” and so on should be changed in case to “**upasampannassa**” (of the fully ordained one).
- Even if the mātikā word “khiyyanakaṃ nāma” were taken up separately, the same analysis given for “ujjhāpanakaṃ nāma” would have to be stated; there is no other distinction. So it should be understood that it was not taken up or analysed separately, and only the conclusion was made jointly.
## {att} “Dhammakamme dhammakammasaññī” — the meaning
%% Act | If the act of authorisation carried out for that fully ordained monk is a legitimate act,
%% Perception | and that monk, perceiving it as legitimate,
%% Act | makes others look down and complains,
%% Result | then for that making others look down and complaining he incurs a **pācittiya** — the remaining terms should be understood in the same way.
## {att} The terms unordained and unauthorised
|! Term | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Anupasampannaṃ ujjhāpeti vā khiyyati vā** | He makes some other unordained person look down on the ordained monk authorised by the Saṅgha, or complains about him in that unordained person’s presence.
| **An ordained monk not authorised by the Saṅgha** | One not authorised by a formal announcement: (1) one to whom the Saṅgha merely assigned the burden, “this is your burden”; (2) one who takes on that burden himself for the comfort of the monks; or (3) one who does such work where two or three monks live.
| **An authorised unordained person** | Although the thirteen authorisations may not be given to an unordained person, “authorised” refers to one who received the authorisation while ordained and later is in the state of an unordained person.
| **An unauthorised unordained person** | “Unauthorised” refers to a capable novice who has been given the burden, “you do this work”, by the Saṅgha or by an authorised monk.
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> උජ්ඣාපනකෙ, ඛිය්‍යනකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
This is **the third rule of the Bhūtagāma chapter** (**tatiye**). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explains the words thus:
|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Ujjhāpanaka** | Speech by which, saying “So-and-so does this out of favouritism” and so on, wishing disrepute for an ordained monk authorised by the Saṅgha as assigner of lodgings and so on, they make the monks disregard him, look at him with contempt, or think of him as low.
| **Khiyyanaka** | Speech by which, speaking in the same way, they complain and make his faults known everywhere.
| **Ujjhāpanake khiyyanake** | In that (**tasmiṃ**) making others look down and in that complaining.
| **Pācittiyaṃ** | ==Two pācittiyas for the two matters== are stated.
## {kkv} The full meaning
%% Intention | One who, wishing disrepute for an authorised monk,
%% To whom | speaking to a fully ordained monk,
%% Act | makes him look down on that monk or complains,
%% Result | incurs a **pācittiya**.

# For one who makes others look down on, or complains about, one who by nature acts out of desire and so on (the wrong courses);
# For the insane and so on.
## {kkv} Factors (= cha — six)
# Being authorised by a legitimate act;
# (He) is fully ordained;
# (He) does not go the wrong courses;
# Wishing to discredit him;
# The one in whose presence he speaks is fully ordained;
# Making others look down or complaining.

| **චම්මඛණ්ඩ** | සිංහ, ව්‍යාඝ්‍ර, දිවි, වලස් සම් ආදී යම් සමක්. අටුවාවල සෙනසුන් පරිභෝගයෙහි ප්‍රතික්ෂේප කළ සමක් නොපෙනේ; එබැවින් සිංහ සම් ආදිය **පරිහරණය කිරීම** (ගෙන යාම) පමණක් ප්‍රතික්ෂේප කළ බව දත යුතුය.
| **පාදපුඤ්ඡනී** | ලණුවලින් හෝ රෙදි කැබලිවලින් පා පිසීමට කළ දෙය.
| **ඵලකපීඨ** | ලෑලි පුටුව; නැතහොත් ලෑල්ල හා දැව පුටුව — මෙයින් සියලු දැව භාණ්ඩ ගැනේ.
- මහාපච්චරිය විස්තර වශයෙන් කියයි: පාත්‍ර ආධාරකය, පාත්‍ර පියන, පා උරච්චිය (**පාදකථලිකා**), තල් වටාපත, විජිනි පත, යම් දැව භාණ්ඩයක්, අන්තිමේදී පැන් හැන්දක් හෝ පැන් හක් ගෙඩියක් පවා එළිමහනෙහි තබා යන්නාට **දුක්කට** ය. (මහා අට්ඨකථාව මෙය දෙවන සේනාසන ශික්ෂාපදයේ දක්වයි.)
- එළිමහනෙහි සායම් පිස, සායම් බඳුන, සායම් හැන්ද, සායම් ඔරුව යන සියල්ල ගිනිහලෙහි තැන්පත් කළ යුතුය. ගිනිහලක් නැත්නම් වැසි නොතෙමෙන ගල් ලෙනක තැබිය යුතුය; එය ද නැත්නම් භික්ෂූන් බලන විට පෙනෙන තැනක තබා යෑම ද වටී.
- **අඤ්ඤස්ස පුග්ගලිකෙ** — විශ්වාසයෙන් ගැනීම නොපිහිටන කෙනෙකුගේ දෙයෙහි දුක්කට ය; විශ්වාසය පිහිටන කෙනෙකුගේ දෙය තමාගේ පුද්ගලික දෙයක් මෙන් ම වේ (මහාපච්චරිය ආදිය).

| **ආපදාසු** | ජීවිතයට හෝ බ්‍රහ්මචර්‍යාවට අන්තරා ඇති කල.
## {kkv} ශික්ෂාපදය හා පදාර්ථය
> යො පන භික්ඛු සඞ්ඝිකං මඤ්චං වා පීඨං වා භිසිං වා කොච්ඡං වා අජ්ඣොකාසෙ සන්ථරිත්වා වා සන්ථරාපෙත්වා වා තං පක්කමන්තො නෙව උද්ධරෙය්‍ය, න උද්ධරාපෙය්‍ය, අනාපුච්ඡං වා ගච්ඡෙය්‍ය, පාචිත්තියං.
මෙය **භූතගාම වග්ගයේ සතරවන ශික්ෂාපදයයි** (**චතුත්ථෙ**). කඞ්ඛාවිතරණිය පද මෙසේ විවරණය කරයි:
|! පදය | කඞ්ඛාවිතරණී විවරණය
| **සඞ්ඝිකං** | සංඝයාට අයත් දෙය.
| **මඤ්ච / පීඨ** | ඇඳක හැඩයට කළ ඕනෑම දෙයක් ඇඳ ම ය; පුටුවෙහි ද මේ ක්‍රමය ම ය.
| **භිසි** | යම් රෙද්දකින් හෝ කප්පිය සමකින් පිට ආවරණය කොට, මිනිස් කෙස් හා තාලීස පත් හැර, යම් ලොම්, කොළ, තණ, වැහැරි, රෙදි කැබලිවලින් පුරවා කළ සෙනසුන. එහි හිඳීම හා සැතපීම වටී; ප්‍රමාණ සීමාවක් ද නැත.
| **කොච්ඡ** | වැහැරි, සැවැන්දරා, මුඤ්ජ, පබ්බජ ආදී එකකින් කළ, ඇතුළට වෙළා බැඳි, යට හා උඩ පළල්, බෙර හැඩැති, මැද සිංහ සම් ආදියෙන් වට කළ ආසනය. මෙහි නොවටනා සමක් නැත; සෙනසුන රත්‍රන්මය වුව ද වටී.
| **අජ්ඣොකාසෙ** | විස්තරය පහත වගුවෙහි.
| **සන්ථරිත්වා** | එවැනි තැනක තමා උදෙසා හෝ අනුන් උදෙසා අතුරා. අනුන් උදෙසා අතුළ ද, ඔහු එහි නොහිඳින තාක්, හෝ “ඔබ යන්න” යි නොකියන තාක් අතුළ තැනැත්තාගේ ම භාරයයි.
| **සන්ථරාපෙත්වා** | අනුපසම්පන්නයෙකු ලවා අතුරවා — එය ඔහුට (අතුරවූවාට) වගකීමක් වේ. උපසම්පන්නයෙකු අතුළ දෙය, අණ කළ තැනැත්තා එහි නොහිඳින තාක් හෝ “ඔබ යන්න” යි නොකියන තාක් අතුළ තැනැත්තාගේ ම භාරයයි. තමා අතුරවූ හෝ ස්වභාවයෙන් අතුළ යම් සෙනසුනක උපසම්පන්නයෙක් හිඳියි ද, ඒ සියල්ල හුන් තැනැත්තාගේ ම භාරයයි.
| **නෙව උද්ධරෙය්‍ය, න උද්ධරාපෙය්‍ය** | තමා අකුලා සුදුසු තැනක නොතබයි නම්, අනුන් ලවා එසේ නොකරවයි නම්.
| **අනාපුච්ඡං වා ගච්ඡෙය්‍ය** | ලජ්ජී වූ, තමාගේ වගකීමක් සේ සලකන භික්ෂුවකට, සාමණේරයෙකුට හෝ ආරාමිකයෙකුට නොදන්වා, ඒ සෙනසුන ඔහුට නොපවරා, අපේක්ෂා රහිතව යයි නම්.

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of Lodging Left in the Open
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery
:::short
In the cold season monks set out furniture in the open to warm themselves and, at mealtime, ==left without putting it away or taking leave==; it was soaked by snow. The Blessed One rebuked them and laid down the rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Sāvatthī, in Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. At that time monks, in the cold season, set out lodging furniture in the open and warmed their bodies; when the time (for the meal) was announced, ==they left without putting it away, without having it put away, and without taking leave==. The furniture got soaked (by the snow).
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can monks set out furniture in the open and, on leaving, neither put it away nor have it put away, and go without taking leave? The furniture is soaked!” Having criticised them in many ways, they told the Blessed One. The Blessed One rebuked them and laid down the training rule:
:::
!pn: The training rule
> යො පන භික්ඛු සඞ්ඝිකං මඤ්චං වා පීඨං වා භිසිං වා කොච්ඡං වා අජ්ඣොකාසෙ සන්ථරිත්වා වා සන්ථරාපෙත්වා වා තං පක්කමන්තො නෙව උද්ධරෙය්‍ය න උද්ධරාපෙය්‍ය, අනාපුච්ඡං වා ගච්ඡෙය්‍ය, පාචිත්තියං.
~ Whatever monk, having spread out or had spread out in the open a Saṅgha-owned bed, bench, mattress or stool, on departing neither puts it away nor has it put away, or goes without informing anyone: Pācittiya.
### The Allowance for the Months Not Designated as Rainy
:::short
Seeing monks take furniture in too early, he allowed it to be kept ==in the eight months not designated as rainy== in a pavilion or under a tree where birds do not roost.
:::
:::long
Then monks who lived in the open (fearing the rule) took their furniture in far too early. The Blessed One, seeing them, gave a Dhamma talk on this occasion and said: “Monks, I allow you, ==during the eight months not designated as the rainy season (**avassikasaṅketa**)==, to keep furniture in a pavilion, or at the foot of a tree where crows or kites do not drop their droppings.”
:::
!src: This is an additional allowance, not a supplementary rule (anupaññatti); the original rule is the complete training rule.

## {att} The eight months not designated as rainy, and the foot of a tree
- **Avassikasaṅketa** — the eight months, the four of the cold season and the four of the hot season, that are not designated “the rainy months”.
- **Maṇḍape** — in a branch-pavilion or a plank-pavilion. **Rukkhamūle** — under any tree.
|! Situation | Ruling
| The foot of a tree where birds out foraging rest and go on | Allowed to keep it there
| The foot of a tree where crows, kites or other birds nest and live permanently | Must not be kept there
| Even in regions where it does not rain in the rainy season, the (rainy) four months | Not allowed (because “eight months” is said)
| In the cold season, where it rains in the cold season | Not allowed to keep it in the open (because “not designated as rainy” is said)
| The hot season (a clear, cloudless sky everywhere) | Allowed to keep a bed or bench in the open for some business
## {att} The duties of an open-air dweller
- If he has a personal bed, he should sleep on that.
- If he takes a Saṅgha bed, he should take one woven with cane or bark; if there is none, an old bed; if there is none, a newly woven or a covered one.
- Having taken it, he may not, thinking “I am a strict tree-root dweller, a strict open-air dweller”, set it out at the wrong time in the open or at the foot of a tree and lie down without even making a robe-hut (**cīvarakuṭi**).
- If even a hut made of a fourfold robe cannot keep it from getting wet, or a seven-day downpour and so on occurs, it is allowed, since (the furniture) follows the monk’s body.
## {att} Forest huts and brooms
- People pleased by the virtue of those living in forest leaf-huts give a new bed or bench, saying, “Use it as Saṅgha property.” When leaving after staying there, one should send it to like-minded monks in a nearby monastery; if there are none, leave it in a place where the rain does not reach; if there is no such place, hang it on a tree and go.
- Taking a broom from the cetiya courtyard, one may sweep the dining-hall courtyard, the uposatha-hall courtyard, a cell, a day-quarters, a fire-hall and so on, wash the broom, and put it back in the broom shed. The same method applies to one who takes it from the uposatha hall and so on and sweeps the rest.
- One who wishes to go while sweeping the alms-round path should, if there is a hall on the way, put it there. If not, noting that clouds have not risen, knowing “it will not rain until I leave the village”, he may put it down anywhere and, on returning, put it back in its usual place. **If he knowingly leaves it in the open when it will rain, it is a dukkaṭa** (Mahāpaccarī).
- If a broom has been kept at each place for sweeping that place, one may sweep it and put it back right there.
- The duty of one sweeping the sitting hall: the sand should be drawn from the middle towards the foot places; rubbish should be picked up by hand and thrown outside.
## {att} Kinds of bed and the koccha — the commentary’s explanation
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Masāraka** | Made by piercing the legs of the bed and inserting the frame-pieces into them.
| **Bundikābaddha** | Made by having the frame-pieces bite into the legs, in the manner of a couch.
| **Kuḷīrapādaka** | Made with legs like those of rams and the like; or any bed with crooked legs.
| **Āhaccapādaka** | Made by piercing the frame-pieces, inserting the tip of the leg there, and fixing a pin on top (since the Pāli itself says “made by piercing the frame”). The same method applies to the bench.
| **Koccha** | Bound in the shape of a drum (**paṇava**), wide below and above and narrow in the middle. They even surround it in the middle with lion or tiger skin; there is no unallowable skin here. Lodging furniture may be even of gold; so it is costly.
| **Leḍḍupāta** | The distance a clod is thrown by a man of average strength.
## {att} Responsibility (palibodha) — the elder and the junior
|! Situation | Ruling
| An elder, having eaten in the dining hall, orders a junior monk, “Go and set out a bed and bench in the day-quarters”; he does so and sits there; the elder, having wandered as he liked, goes there and puts down his bag or upper robe | From then on it is **the elder’s** responsibility; if he sits and then leaves without putting it away or having it put away, a pācittiya on passing the clod’s throw
| The elder, without putting down his bag etc., while walking up and down, says to the junior, “You go” | The junior should point out, “Venerable sir, here is the bed and bench”; an elder who knows the duties should say, “You go, I will put it back in order”
| The elder is a fool who does not know the duties and threatens, “Go, don’t stand here; I won’t let you sit or lie down” | The junior should take leave, saying “Venerable sir, sleep well”, pay respects and go; once he has gone, it is **the elder’s** responsibility
| At the very moment of the order, the junior says, “Venerable sir, I have some work such as washing my things”; the elder says, “Set it out and go”, and himself leaves the dining hall and goes elsewhere | He should be dealt with by **the lifting of the feet**; if he goes there and sits, an offence on passing the clod’s throw
| The elder orders a novice; the novice sets it out and sits there | If the elder goes from the dining hall elsewhere, by the lifting of the feet; having gone and sat, when leaving again, an offence on passing the clod’s throw
| He orders, “Set out the bed and bench and sit right there” | He may go wherever he wants and come back; if he leaves without putting it in order himself, a pācittiya on passing the clod’s throw
| At an intermediate gathering, those who set out beds and benches and sat | When leaving they should tell the monastery attendants, “Put this away”; for those who leave without saying so, an offence on passing the clod’s throw
## {att} A great Dhamma hearing and the Dhamma seat
- At a great Dhamma hearing they bring beds and benches from the uposatha hall and the dining hall and set them out; the responsibility is **the residents’**. If visitors take them, saying “this is for our preceptor, this for our teacher”, from then on it is **theirs**; for those who leave without putting them in order and pass the clod’s throw, an offence.
- The Mahāpaccarī says: as long as others do not sit, it is the burden of those who set it out; when others come and sit, it is the burden of those seated. If they leave without putting it away or having it put away, it is a **dukkaṭa** — because it was set out without a command.
- When the Dhamma seat has been set out, until the reciter or the Dhamma speaker comes, it is the responsibility of those who set it out; once he comes and sits, it is his.
- When there is Dhamma listening the whole day and night, and one gets up and another sits, it is the burden of whoever comes and sits. The one getting up should say, “This seat is your responsibility”, and go.
- If, before the other arrives, the one who sat first gets up and goes, and the other comes and sits while he is still within the precinct, the one who left is not to be charged with an offence. If he passes the clod’s throw before the other arrives, he is to be charged.
- Everywhere, the method “on passing the clod’s throw, **dukkaṭa at the first step, pācittiya at the second**” is stated in the Mahāpaccarī.
## {att} The floor-cover and other dukkaṭa objects
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Cimilikā** | Made to protect the colour of a floor finished with plaster and the like; it is spread underneath and a mat is spread on top.
| **Uttarattharaṇa** | A cover to be spread over beds and benches.
| **Bhūmattharaṇa** | Kinds of mat and the like to be spread on the ground.
| **Taṭṭikā** | A mat made of palm leaves or of bark.
| **Cammakhaṇḍa** | Any leather, even of lion, tiger, leopard or bear. In the commentaries no leather is found that is prohibited for use as lodging furniture; so it should be understood that only the **carrying about** of lion skins and the like is prohibited.
| **Pādapuñchanī** | Made of ropes or rags for wiping the feet.
| **Phalakapīṭha** | A plank-seat; or a plank and a wooden bench — this includes all wooden articles.
- The Mahāpaccarī says in detail: for one who leaves in the open a bowl-stand, a bowl lid, a foot-scraper (**pādakathalikā**), a palm-leaf fan, a fanning leaf, any wooden article, even a water ladle or a drinking conch, and goes, it is a **dukkaṭa**. (The Mahā-aṭṭhakathā shows this method in the second lodging rule.)
- Having boiled dye in the open, the dye pot, the dye ladle and the dye trough should all be put away in the fire-hall. If there is no fire-hall, they should be left under a rock overhang the rain does not reach; if there is none, one may also leave them where monks can see them when they look.
- **Aññassa puggalike** — with the property of someone with whom taking on trust is not valid, a dukkaṭa; the property of someone with whom trust is valid is like one’s own personal property (Mahāpaccarī and others).
## {att} Explanation of the no-offence terms
|! Term | The commentary’s explanation
| **Āpucchaṃ gacchati** | No offence for one who leaves after informing a conscientious monk, novice or monastery attendant who regards it as his own responsibility.
| **Otāpento gacchati** | No offence for one who leaves it airing in the sun, thinking, “I will come back and put it away.”
| **Kenaci palibuddhaṃ** | The lodging is obstructed by something: if a senior monk makes him get up and takes it, if a yakkha or a peta comes and sits on it, if some powerful person comes and takes it, if lions, tigers and the like come and stand in that area — even leaving without putting it away, no offence.
| **Āpadāsu** | When there are dangers to life or to the holy life.
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> යො පන භික්ඛු සඞ්ඝිකං මඤ්චං වා පීඨං වා භිසිං වා කොච්ඡං වා අජ්ඣොකාසෙ සන්ථරිත්වා වා සන්ථරාපෙත්වා වා තං පක්කමන්තො නෙව උද්ධරෙය්‍ය, න උද්ධරාපෙය්‍ය, අනාපුච්ඡං වා ගච්ඡෙය්‍ය, පාචිත්තියං.
This is **the fourth rule of the Bhūtagāma chapter** (**catutthe**). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explains the words thus:
|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Saṅghikaṃ** | Belonging to the Saṅgha.
| **Mañca / pīṭha** | Anything made in the form of a bed is a bed; the same method for the bench.
| **Bhisi** | Lodging furniture covered with any cloth or allowable leather and filled with any wool, leaves, grass, bark or cloth, except human hair and tālīsa leaves. One may sit and lie on it; there is no size limit.
| **Koccha** | A seat made of one of bark, usīra, muñja, pabbaja and so on, wound round inside and bound, wide below and above, drum-shaped, surrounded in the middle with lion skin and the like. There is no unallowable skin here; lodging furniture may be even of gold.
| **Ajjhokāse** | Explained in the table below.
| **Santharitvā** | Having spread it in such a place for himself or for another. Even what is spread for another remains the spreader’s burden as long as the other does not sit there, or does not say, “You go.”
| **Santharāpetvā** | Having had an unordained person spread it — that becomes his (the one who had it spread) responsibility. What an ordained monk has spread remains the spreader’s burden as long as the one who commanded does not sit there or say, “You go.” Whatever furniture, whether he had it spread or it was spread as usual, an ordained monk sits on — all that is the burden of the one seated.
| **Neva uddhareyya, na uddharāpeyya** | He neither puts it away himself in a suitable place, nor has another do so.
| **Anāpucchaṃ vā gaccheyya** | Without informing a conscientious monk, novice or monastery attendant who regards it as his own responsibility, without handing the furniture over to him, he goes without concern.
## {kkv} “Ajjhokāse” — which place, which time?
|! Place / time | Ruling
| The four months other than the eight not designated as rainy (the rains) | Even if it does not rain, not allowed to spread it in an ordinary open place or in a pavilion where rain comes in
| Places where it rains in the cold season | The other four months (the cold season) are not allowed either
| The hot season (a clear, cloudless sky everywhere) | Allowed if one goes away for some business
| The foot of a tree where crows and the like live permanently | Never allowed
!src: Thus wherever and whenever it is not allowed to spread it, all that is counted here as “**ajjhokāsa**” (the open).
## {kkv} The moment of the offence
%% Who | A monk who leaves without informing and without concern,
%% Distance | if he passes the clod’s throw of a man of average strength,
%% First step | incurs a **dukkaṭa** when one foot passes the clod’s throw,
%% Second step | and a **pācittiya** when the second foot passes.
!src: One who, standing in the dining hall, sends (another) saying, “Set it out in such-and-such day-quarters and go”, and then leaves from there and goes elsewhere, should be dealt with by the lifting of the feet.
## {kkv} The laying down of the rule and the command
%% Place | Laid down at Sāvatthī,
%% Person | concerning a number of monks,
%% Matter | **on the matter of leaving without putting away what was spread and without taking leave**, this rule
%% Kind of rule | is a **common rule** (**sādhāraṇa-paññatti**) — shared with the nuns;
%% Command | and it is **anāṇattika**.
## {kkv} Division of offences
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| A Saṅgha bed, bench, mattress or stool | Perceiving it as the Saṅgha’s, in doubt, or perceiving it as personal (the triad) | PC
| Personal property | The triad | DK
| A floor-cover, over-spread, ground-spread, mat, piece of leather, foot-wiper, plank-seat, or any other wooden or clay article — even a bowl-stand | Leaving it in an open place of the described kind and going | DK
- **A forest monk** — if there is no place the rain does not reach, he may hang everything on a tree, or arrange it so that termites do not eat it, and go.
- **An open-air dweller** — should protect it even by making a robe-hut.
## {kkv} No offence
# With one’s own property, or the personal property of a trusted person;
# When one goes after putting it away and so on;
# For one airing it in the sun, or going thinking, “I will come back and put it away”;
# When seniors make him get up, a non-human sits there, some powerful person takes it, lions and the like come and stand in that place — when the lodging is obstructed in this way;
# For one who goes in emergencies that endanger life or the holy life;
# For the insane and so on.
## {kkv} Factors (= cha — six)
# The bed and so on belong to the Saṅgha;
# It has been spread, or had spread, in a place of the described kind;
# There is no obstruction;
# There is no emergency;
# Being without concern (**nirapekkhatā**);
# Passing the clod’s throw.
## {kkv} Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
The origin and the rest are the same as in the **first Kaṭhina rule**. But this one is **kiriyākiriya** (by doing and not doing).

## {att} කාට දැන්විය යුතු ද? — කිසිවකු නැති කල කළ යුතු දෑ
- භික්ෂුවක් ඇත්නම් භික්ෂුවකට දැන්විය යුතුය; නැත්නම් සාමණේරයෙකුට; ඔහු ද නැත්නම් ආරාමිකයෙකුට; ඔහු ද නැත්නම් විහාරය කරවූ **විහාර හිමියාට** හෝ ඔහුගේ පවුලේ යමෙකුට දැන්විය යුතුය.
- ඔවුන් ද නැත්නම්: ගල් සතරක් මත ඇඳක් තබා, ඇඳ මත ඉතිරි ඇඳ පුටු තබා, උඩින් මෙට්ට ආදී සෙය්‍යා දහය ම රාශි කොට, දැව භාණ්ඩ හා මැටි භාණ්ඩ තැන්පත් කොට, දොර කවුළු වසා, **ගමික වත** සපුරා යා යුතුය.
- සෙනසුන වැසි තෙමේ නම් හා සෙවිලි කිරීමට තණ හෝ උළු ගෙනැවිත් ඇත්නම්, හැකි නම් සෙවිලි කළ යුතුය. නොහැකි නම් වැසි නොතෙමෙන තැනක ඇඳ පුටු ආදිය තබා යා යුතුය.
- සියල්ල ම වැසි තෙමේ නම්, හැකි නම් ගම ඇතුළත උපාසකයන්ගේ ගෙයක තැබිය යුතුය. ඔවුන් ද “ස්වාමීනි, සංඝික දේ බරපතල ය; ගිනි ගැනීම් ආදියට බිය වෙමු” යි නොපිළිගනිත් නම්, එළිමහනෙහි වුව ද ගල් මත ඇඳ තබා ඉතිරිය පෙර කී ක්‍රමයට තබා, තණ හා කොළවලින් වසා යෑම වටී. එහි අඟ මාත්‍රයක් හෝ ඉතිරි වේ නම් එය එහි එන අනෙක් භික්ෂූන්ට උපකාර වන හෙයිනි.

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of Bedding
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery
:::short
The group-of-seventeen monks, who always stayed and travelled together, spread bedding in a Saṅgha dwelling and ==left without putting it away or taking leave==; the lodging was **eaten by termites**. The Blessed One rebuked them and laid down the rule.

At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Sāvatthī, in Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. At that time the **group-of-seventeen** monks were friends; when staying, they stayed together, and when leaving, they left together. They spread bedding in a certain Saṅgha dwelling and, on leaving, ==went without putting it away, without having it put away, and without taking leave==. The lodging was **eaten by termites**.
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can the group-of-seventeen monks spread bedding in a Saṅgha dwelling and, on leaving, go without putting it away, without having it put away, and without taking leave? The lodging has been eaten by termites!” Having criticised them in many ways, they told the Blessed One. The Blessed One asked, “Is it true, monks, that the group-of-seventeen … and the lodging was eaten by termites?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked them: “Monks, how can those foolish men …? Monks, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule:

!pn: The training rule
> යො පන භික්ඛු සඞ්ඝිකෙ විහාරෙ සෙය්‍යං සන්ථරිත්වා වා සන්ථරාපෙත්වා වා තං පක්කමන්තො නෙව උද්ධරෙය්‍ය න උද්ධරාපෙය්‍ය, අනාපුච්ඡං වා ගච්ඡෙය්‍ය, පාචිත්තියං.
~ Whatever monk, having spread out or had spread out bedding in a Saṅgha-owned dwelling, on departing neither puts it away nor has it put away, or goes without informing anyone: Pācittiya.
!src: This rule has no supplementary rule (anupaññatti); the original rule is the complete training rule.
## {story} Word analysis
|! Word | Meaning in the text
| **Saṅghiko vihāro** | A dwelling given to, made over to the Saṅgha.
| **Seyyaṃ** | Ten kinds of bedding — a mattress (**bhisi**), a floor-cover, an over-spread, a ground-spread, a mat (**taṭṭikā**), a piece of leather, a sitting cloth, a covering cloth (**paccattharaṇa**), a grass spread, a leaf spread.
| **Santharitvā** | Having spread it himself.
| **Santharāpetvā** | Having had another spread it.
| **Neva uddhareyya** | He does not put it away himself.
| **Na uddharāpeyya** | He does not have another put it away.
| **Anāpucchaṃ vā gaccheyya** | Without informing a monk, a novice or a monastery attendant, ==on passing the enclosure (**parikkhepa**) of an enclosed monastery, or the precinct of an unenclosed one==, there is a pācittiya.
## {story} Offences by perception
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| A Saṅgha dwelling | Perceiving it as the Saṅgha’s, in doubt, or perceiving it as personal, having spread bedding / had it spread, he leaves without putting it away / having it put away, or without taking leave | PC
| In the precinct of the dwelling, in the attendance hall, in a pavilion or at the foot of a tree | Spreading bedding / having it spread and leaving like that | DK
| In the dwelling, its precinct, the attendance hall, a pavilion or at the foot of a tree | Spreading a bed or bench / having it spread and leaving like that | DK
| A personal dwelling | Perceiving it as the Saṅgha’s | DK
| A personal dwelling | While in doubt | DK
| Another’s personal dwelling | Perceiving it as personal | DK
| One’s own personal dwelling | — | N
## {story} No offence
# He leaves after putting it away;
# He leaves after having it put away;
# He leaves after taking leave;
# It is obstructed by something;
# Having gone with intent to return (**sāpekkha**), he informs while standing there;
# He (himself) is obstructed by something;
# In emergencies;
# For the insane; for the first offender.
## {att} Kinds of bedding and the precinct
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Bhisi** | A bed mattress or a bench mattress.
| **Nisīdana** | To be understood as a sitting cloth with a border (**sadasa**).
| **Paccattharaṇa** | Only a cloak (pāvāra) and a woollen rug (kojava) are meant.
| **Tiṇasanthāra / paṇṇasanthāra** | A spread of any kind of grass; the same method for a spread of leaves.
| **Parikkhepaṃ atikkamantassa** | For one who passes the first foot beyond it, a **dukkaṭa**; on passing the second, a **pācittiya**.
| **The precinct of an unenclosed one** | The area of two clod’s throws (**leḍḍupāta**) from the lodging.
## {att} Whom to inform — and what to do when there is no one
- If there is a monk, the monk should be informed; if not, a novice; if not, a monastery attendant; if not, **the owner of the dwelling** who had it built, or anyone in his family.
- If there is none of them: set a bed on four stones, put the remaining beds and benches on the bed, pile the ten kinds of bedding such as mattresses on top, put away the wooden and clay articles, close the doors and windows, fulfil **the duties of one departing**, and go.
- If the lodging leaks and grass or bricks have been brought for roofing, one should roof it if able. If not able, one should put the beds, benches and so on in a place the rain does not reach, and go.
- If it all leaks, one should, if able, leave them in the house of lay followers within the village. If they will not accept them, saying, “Venerable sir, Saṅgha property is a heavy burden; we fear fire and the like”, it is allowed even in the open to set the bed on stones, put away the rest in the way stated, cover it with grass and leaves, and go. For whatever even a part of it remains will be of help to other monks who come there.
## {att} Dwelling and precinct — why pācittiya in one and dukkaṭa in the other?
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Vihārassa upacāra, upaṭṭhānasālā, maṇḍapa, rukkhamūla** | In the Kurundī commentary: the cell compound, the compound’s dining hall, the compound’s pavilion, the compound’s tree foot. Though so stated, “dwelling” should be understood as the inner chamber, or any other protected lodging enclosed on all sides.
| **Vihārassa upacāre** | In the open space close outside it.
| **Upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ** | In the dining hall.
| **Maṇḍape** | In a pavilion, unenclosed or even enclosed, where many gather. There is nothing to say about the tree foot.
|! Situation | Offence | Reason
| Setting out the ten kinds of bedding in a protected place such as an inner chamber and leaving | PC | Both the bedding and the lodging decay through termites and become just a termite mound
| Setting them out outside, in the attendance hall and so on, and leaving | DK | Because the place is unprotected, only the bedding would be lost, not the lodging
| Spreading a bed or bench even in the dwelling itself and leaving | DK | Termites cannot quickly eat a bed or bench
| A bed or bench in the dwelling’s precinct | DK | Those walking about on a round of the dwellings will see it and put it away
## {att} Putting it away — how it should be done
- One who puts it away before leaving should remove all the bed and bench planks (**kavāṭa**), gather them up, hang them on the robe-pole, and go. A monk who later comes and lives there should do the same when he leaves after setting out a bed or bench and sleeping.
- One who takes bedding from inside the wall and sets it out by the outer wall should, when leaving, put it away in the very place he took it from. The same for one who brings it down from an upper storey and lives on the lower storey.
- Even when beds and benches are set out at night-quarters or day-quarters, when leaving they should be put back in the place they were taken from.
## {att} Lodgings where one must inform, and where one need not
|! Lodging | Ruling
| A long hall or leaf hut built on the ground, or a house built on wooden posts, a place where termites rise | One must leave only after informing — if it is not looked after for a few days, termite mounds form
| A lodging built on a rock surface or on stone pillars, a rock cave, a plastered lodging — where there is no fear of termites | Allowed to go whether one informs or not; but informing is the duty
| Even in such a lodging, if termites climb up on one side | One must leave only after informing
## {att} Responsibility — visitors and groups
- If a visiting monk, deferring to a monk living there who has taken a Saṅgha lodging, lives there without taking a lodging of his own, as long as he does not take it the lodging is **the earlier monk’s** responsibility. From when he takes the lodging and lives there under his own authority, it is **the visitor’s**. If both divide and take it, it is **both’s**.
- The Mahāpaccarī: if two or three set it out together, all of them must inform when leaving. If the first of them to leave goes, having in mind “the last will look after it”, that is allowed; the last has no release by such a thought.
- If many send one to have it spread, then when leaving either all should inform, or they should send one to inform.
- Even if one brings beds and benches from elsewhere and lives in another place, when leaving they must be taken back there. If, while one lives with things brought from another residence, a senior monk arrives, he is not to be turned away; one should say, “Venerable sir, I brought this from another residence; please return it to its usual place.” When he agrees, “I will do so”, the other may go.
|! Use | When lost, worn out or stolen
| Something taken elsewhere and **used as Saṅgha property** | No liability to make good
| Something **used as personal property** | Liable to make good
| Another’s bed or bench, used as Saṅgha or personal property | If lost, liable to make good in either case
## {att} Explanation of the no-offence terms
|! Term | The commentary’s explanation
| **Kenaci palibuddhaṃ hoti** | The lodging is obstructed by anything such as senior monks, powerful people, yakkhas, lions, fierce beasts, black snakes and so on.
| **Sāpekkho gantvā tattha ṭhito āpucchati** | Intending to return — “I will come back today and look after it” — he goes across a river or to another village and, standing right where the thought of going on arose, sends someone to inform.
| **Kenaci palibuddho hoti** | He is obstructed by something such as a flood, kings, thieves and the like and cannot come back — for such a one too there is no offence.
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> යො පන භික්ඛු සඞ්ඝිකෙ විහාරෙ සෙය්‍යං සන්ථරිත්වා වා සන්ථරාපෙත්වා වා තං පක්කමන්තො නෙව උද්ධරෙය්‍ය, න උද්ධරාපෙය්‍ය, අනාපුච්ඡං වා ගච්ඡෙය්‍ය, පාචිත්තියං.
This is **the fifth rule of the Bhūtagāma chapter** (**pañcame**). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explains the words thus:
|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Vihāre** | In an inner chamber, or in any other lodging of the kind described, enclosed on all sides.
| **Seyyaṃ** | The ten: mattress, floor-cover, over-spread, ground-spread, mat, piece of leather, sitting cloth, covering cloth, grass spread, leaf spread.
| **Cimilikā** | Made to protect the colour of a finished floor.
| **Uttarattharaṇa** | A cover suitable for spreading over beds, benches and so on.
| **Bhūmattharaṇa** | Kinds of mat and the like to be spread over a floor-cover if there is one, or on the bare floor if not.
| **Taṭṭikā** | A mat made of palm leaves and the like.
| **Cammakkhaṇḍa** | Any leather. Only carrying about lion skin and the like is prohibited; there is no unallowable leather for use as lodging furniture.
| **Paccattharaṇa** | Only a cloak and a woollen rug. The rest is clear.
## {kkv} The full meaning
%% Bedding | A monk who, taking even one of these ten kinds of bedding by his own seniority,
%% Place | has spread it or had it spread in a dwelling of the described kind,
%% Who | and, setting off in some direction (**disaṃgamika**),
%% Not putting away | neither puts it away nor has it put away — in the sense of placing it so that termites do not eat it as it lies —
%% Not informing | or goes without informing (a conscientious monk and so on) in the way stated in the previous rule,
%% Boundary | on passing the enclosure of an enclosed monastery, or the precinct of an unenclosed one,
%% Result | incurs a **dukkaṭa** at the first step and a **pācittiya** at the second.
!src: From a place where there is no fear of termites one may go even without informing; but informing is the duty.
## {kkv} The laying down of the rule and the command
%% Place | Laid down at Sāvatthī,
%% Person | concerning the group-of-seventeen monks,
%% Matter | **on the matter of spreading bedding in a Saṅgha dwelling and leaving without putting it away and without taking leave**, this rule
%% Kind of rule | is a **common rule** (**sādhāraṇa-paññatti**) — shared with the nuns;
%% Command | and it is **anāṇattika**.
## {kkv} Division of offences
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| A Saṅgha dwelling | The triad (perceiving it as the Saṅgha’s, in doubt, perceiving it as personal) | PC
| A personal dwelling | The triad | DK
| In the precinct of a dwelling of the described kind — close outside — in the attendance hall, in an unenclosed pavilion, or in an enclosed one where many gather, or at the foot of a tree | Spreading bedding / having it spread and leaving without putting it away and so on | DK
| In the dwelling, or in its precinct of the kind described | Spreading a bed or bench / having it spread and leaving without putting it away and so on | DK
## {kkv} No offence
# With one’s own property, or the personal property of a trusted person;
# When one goes after putting it away and so on;
# When one leaves a lodging that is obstructed, in the way stated before;
# For one who, intending to return — “I will come back today and look after it” — goes across a river or to another village and, standing right where the thought of going on arose, sends someone to inform;
# For one who is obstructed by a flood, kings, thieves and the like and cannot come back;
# In emergencies;
# For the insane and so on.
## {kkv} Factors (= satta — seven)
# Bedding of the described kind;
# It belongs to the Saṅgha;
# It has been spread, or had spread, in a dwelling of the described kind;
# There is no obstruction;
# There is no emergency;
# Setting off in some direction without concern;
# Passing the boundary of the precinct.
## {kkv} Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
The origin and the rest follow the method stated in **the immediately preceding rule**.

අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු “ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු කෙසේ නම් ස්ථවිර භික්ෂූන්ගේ උපචාරයට ඇතුළු වී සෙය්‍යාව සකසන්නාහු ද?” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ; නොයෙක් අයුරින් ගරහා භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට සැල කළහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “මහණෙනි, ඔබ ස්ථවිර භික්ෂූන්ගේ උපචාරයට ඇතුළු වී සෙය්‍යාව සකසන බව සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් …? මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක:

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of Encroaching
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery
:::short
At Sāvatthī, the group-of-six monks had taken possession of the best lodgings, but the elder monks, by seniority, made them get up. Wanting to spend the rains right there, the group-of-six thought, “==Whoever is crowded will leave==,” and lay down after encroaching on the elders. The monks of few wishes criticised this and told the Blessed One, who rebuked the group-of-six and laid down the rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Sāvatthī, in Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. At that time the group-of-six monks took possession of the best lodgings (**palibundhenti**); but the elder monks made them get up (by seniority). Then the group-of-six thought: “By what means can we spend the rains right here?” So the group-of-six, thinking, “==Whoever is crowded (**sambādha**) will leave==,” lay down after encroaching on the elder monks.
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can the group-of-six monks lie down after encroaching on the elder monks?” Having criticised them in many ways, they told the Blessed One. The Blessed One asked: “Is it true, monks, that you lie down after encroaching on the elder monks?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked them: “Foolish men, how can you …? Foolish men, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule:
:::
!pn: The training rule
> යො පන භික්ඛු සඞ්ඝිකෙ විහාරෙ ජානං පුබ්බුපගතං භික්ඛුං අනුපඛජ්ජ සෙය්‍යං කප්පෙය්‍ය – ‘යස්ස සම්බාධො භවිස්සති සො පක්කමිස්සතී’ති, එතදෙව පච්චයං කරිත්වා අනඤ්ඤං, පාචිත්තියං.
~ Whatever monk, in a Saṅgha-owned dwelling, knowing that a monk arrived earlier is there, should lie down after intruding on him, thinking, "Whoever is crowded will leave," for just this reason and no other: Pācittiya.
!src: This rule has no supplementary rule (anupaññatti); the original rule is the complete training rule.
## {story} Word analysis
|! Word | Meaning in the text
| **Saṅghiko vihāro** | A dwelling given to, made over to the Saṅgha.
| **Jānāti** | He knows “he is senior”, knows “he is ill”, knows “he has been given (the lodging) by the Saṅgha”.
| **Anupakhajja** | Having entered.
| **Seyyaṃ kappeyya** | If he spreads bedding, or has it spread, within the precinct of a bed or bench, or of (the monk) entering or leaving, a **dukkaṭa**; if he sits or lies down (there), a **pācittiya**.
| **Etadeva paccayaṃ karitvā anaññaṃ** | There is no other reason for lying down after encroaching.
## {story} Offences by perception and place
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| A Saṅgha dwelling | Perceiving it as the Saṅgha’s, in doubt, or perceiving it as personal, he lies down after encroaching (sits / lies down) | PC
| Leaving aside the precinct of the bed or bench, or of the monk entering or leaving | He spreads bedding / has it spread | DK
| Likewise, leaving aside the precinct | He sits / lies down | DK
| In the precinct of the dwelling, in the attendance hall, in a pavilion, at the foot of a tree or in the open | He spreads bedding / has it spread | DK
| In those places | He sits / lies down | DK
| A personal dwelling | Perceiving it as the Saṅgha’s, or in doubt | DK
| Another’s personal dwelling | Perceiving it as personal | DK
| One’s own personal dwelling | — | N
## {story} No offence
# He enters because he is ill;
# He enters because he is oppressed by cold or heat;
# In emergencies;
# For the insane; for the first offender.
## {att} Points on words in the origin story
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Palibundhenti** | They go there first, carry in their bowls and robes, and stand blocking (the lodgings).
| **Therā bhikkhū vuṭṭhāpenti** | Taking them by seniority, saying “Friends, it falls to us”, they make them get up.
| **Anupakhajja seyyaṃ kappenti** | Saying, “Venerable sir, only the place for your bed falls to you, not the whole dwelling; this place now falls to us”, they enter, set out beds and benches, sit, lie down, and even do their recitation.
## {att} “Jānaṃ” — who cannot be made to get up?
“Jānaṃ” means knowing, “**This one cannot be made to get up** (**anuṭṭhāpanīya**)”; that is why the analysis says “he knows he is senior” and so on.
|! Person | Why he cannot be made to get up
| **Vuḍḍha** (senior) | Because of his seniority
| **Gilāna** (ill) | Because of his illness
| **Saṅghena dinna** (given by the Saṅgha) | Considering the great service and special qualities of a storekeeper, of Dhamma speakers, Vinaya experts and the like, or of a teacher of a group, the Saṅgha authorises and gives a dwelling for permanent residence
!src: Although the Saṅgha itself gives a suitable lodging to an ill monk too, the ill one is mentioned separately to show that “even an ill monk to whom the Saṅgha has not given a lodging by consultation should not be harassed, but treated with compassion”.
## {att} The precinct and the number of pācittiyas
|! Precinct | Extent
| Of beds and benches in a large dwelling | One and a half cubits (**diyaḍḍha hattha**) all around
| In a small dwelling | One and a half cubits from wherever is sufficient
| Of one entering after washing his feet, and of one going out to urinate | A path one and a half cubits wide, from the foot-washing stone placed at the door, and from the place for urinating, up to the bed or bench
- In the precinct of a monk standing in that precinct, or of a monk entering or leaving, one who, wishing to lie down after encroaching, spreads bedding or has it spread incurs a **dukkaṭa**.
- By merely sitting, or merely lying down, a **pācittiya**. If he does both, **two pācittiyas**. If he gets up again and again and sits or lies down, **a pācittiya for each effort**.
- When he spreads it leaving aside the precinct, and in the dukkaṭa sections beginning “in the precinct of the dwelling”, the **division of dukkaṭas** should be understood just as the division of pācittiyas was stated — for merely sitting, merely lying down, doing both, and by each effort. There is no use in living with such an incompatible person in one dwelling or one compound; so his living there is prevented everywhere.
- **Aññassa puggalike** — here too, the personal property of a trusted person is like one’s own personal property; there is no offence there.
- **Āpadāsu** — if one living outside faces a danger to life or the holy life, there is no offence for one who enters in such emergencies.
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> යො පන භික්ඛු සඞ්ඝිකෙ විහාරෙ ජානං පුබ්බුපගතං භික්ඛුං අනුපඛජ්ජ සෙය්‍යං කප්පෙය්‍ය ‘යස්ස සම්බාධො භවිස්සති, සො පක්කමිස්සතී’ති එතදෙව පච්චයං කරිත්වා අනඤ්ඤං, පාචිත්තියං.
This is **the sixth rule of the Bhūtagāma chapter** (**chaṭṭhe**). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explains the words thus:
|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Jānaṃ** | Knowing, “This one cannot be made to get up.” That is why the word analysis says, “he knows he is senior, he knows he is ill, he knows he was given it by the Saṅgha.” A senior cannot be made to get up because of his seniority, an ill monk because of his illness; and the Saṅgha, considering the great service and special qualities of a storekeeper, or of Dhamma speakers, Vinaya experts and teachers of groups, authorises and gives a dwelling for permanent residence; so one to whom the Saṅgha has given it also cannot be made to get up.
| **Pubbupagataṃ** | Who arrived first.
| **Anupakhajja** | Entering the precinct of the beds and benches, or of that monk entering or leaving.
| **The precinct** | Of beds and benches in a large dwelling, one and a half cubits all around; in a small one, one and a half cubits from wherever is sufficient. For one entering, from the foot-washing stone up to the bed or bench; for one leaving, from the bed or bench up to the place for urinating.
| **Seyyaṃ kappeyya** | (explained below)
## {kkv} The full meaning
%% Intention | Wishing to crowd him,
%% Place | in that precinct,
%% Spreading | for one who spreads, or has spread, even one of the ten kinds of bedding, a **dukkaṭa**;
%% Sitting | for one who sits or lies down there, a **pācittiya**;
%% Both | for one who does both, **two pācittiyas**;
%% Repeatedly | for one who does it again and again, **pācittiyas by the number of efforts**.
## {kkv} The laying down of the rule and the command
%% Place | Laid down at Sāvatthī,
%% Person | concerning the group-of-six monks,
%% Matter | **on the matter of lying down after encroaching**, this rule
%% Kind of rule | is a **common rule** (**sādhāraṇa-paññatti**) — shared with the nuns;
%% Command | and it is **anāṇattika**.
## {kkv} Division of offences
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| A Saṅgha dwelling | The triad — sitting / lying down in the precinct | PC
| A personal dwelling | The triad | DK
| Outside the stated precinct, in the attendance hall and so on, or in the precinct of the dwelling | Spreading, having spread, sitting, lying down | DK
## {kkv} No offence
# For one who spreads it in his own dwelling or that of a trusted person;
# For one who enters because he is ill, or oppressed by cold or heat;
# In emergencies;
# For the insane and so on.
## {kkv} Factors (= cattāri — four)
# It is a Saṅgha dwelling;
# Knowing that he cannot be made to get up;
# Wishing to crowd him;
# Sitting or lying down in the precinct.

The origin and the rest are the same as in the **first pārājika rule**. But this one has **painful feeling** only.

එක් සමයෙක බුදුරජාණන් වහන්සේ සැවැත් නුවර අනාථපිණ්ඩිකයාගේ ආරාමය වූ ජේතවනයෙහි වැඩ වසන සේක. එකල සත්තරසවග්ගිය භික්ෂූහු “අපි මෙහි වස් වසන්නෙමු” යි එක්තරා **ප්‍රත්‍යන්ත මහා විහාරයක්** ප්‍රතිසංස්කරණය කරති. ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු ඔවුන් විහාරය ප්‍රතිසංස්කරණය කරනු දැක, “ඇවැත්නි, මේ සත්තරසවග්ගිය භික්ෂූහු විහාරය ප්‍රතිසංස්කරණය කරති; එව, ඔවුන් නැගිටුවමු” යි කීහ. ඇතැමෙක් “ඇවැත්නි, ඔවුන් ප්‍රතිසංස්කරණය කරන තුරු ඉවසව්; ප්‍රතිසංස්කරණය කළ පසු නැගිටුවමු” යි කීහ.
ඉක්බිති ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු සත්තරසවග්ගිය භික්ෂූන්ට “ඇවැත්නි, නැගිටිව්; විහාරය අපට ලැබේ” යි කීහ. “ඇවැත්නි, කලින් ම කිව යුතු නොවේ ද? අපි වෙනත් එකක් ප්‍රතිසංස්කරණය කරන්නෙමු.” “ඇවැත්නි, මෙය සංඝික විහාරයක් නොවේ ද?” “එසේ ය ඇවැත්නි, සංඝික විහාරයකි.” “ඇවැත්නි, නැගිටිව්; විහාරය අපට ලැබේ.” “ඇවැත්නි, විහාරය විශාල ය; ඔබ ද වසව්, අපි ද වසන්නෙමු.” “ඇවැත්නි, නැගිටිව්; විහාරය අපට ලැබේ” යි ==කිපී, නොසතුටු සිතින් ගෙළින් අල්ලා ඇද දැමූහ==. ඇද දමනු ලබන්නෝ හැඬූහ. භික්ෂූහු “ඇවැත්නි, ඔබ කුමට හඬන්නහු ද?” යි ඇසූහ. “ඇවැත්නි, මේ ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු කිපී, නොසතුටු සිතින් අප සංඝික විහාරයෙන් ඇද දමති.”
අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු “ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු කෙසේ නම් කිපී, නොසතුටු සිතින් භික්ෂූන් සංඝික විහාරයෙන් ඇද දමන්නාහු ද?” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ; භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට සැල කළහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “මහණෙනි, ඔබ කිපී … සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් …? මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක:

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of Evicting
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery
:::short
The group-of-seventeen monks were repairing a large borderland dwelling to spend the rains there. Seeing this, the group-of-six decided to wait until the repairs were finished and then make them get up. When it was done, they said, “Get up, friends, the dwelling falls to us.” The seventeen replied, “Shouldn’t you have said so beforehand? The dwelling is large; you stay, and we will stay too.” But ==angry and displeased, the group-of-six seized them by the neck and dragged them out==. The monks, finding them weeping, told the Blessed One, who rebuked the group-of-six and laid down the rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Sāvatthī, in Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. At that time the group-of-seventeen monks were repairing a certain **large dwelling in the borderlands**, thinking, “We will spend the rains here.” The group-of-six monks saw them repairing it and said, “Friends, these group-of-seventeen monks are repairing a dwelling. Come, let’s make them get up!” Some said, “Wait, friends, while they repair it; when it is repaired, we will make them get up.”
Then the group-of-six said to the group-of-seventeen: “Get up, friends, the dwelling falls to us.” “Friends, shouldn’t you have said so beforehand? We would have repaired another.” “Friends, isn’t it a Saṅgha dwelling?” “Yes, friends, it is a Saṅgha dwelling.” “Get up, friends, the dwelling falls to us.” “Friends, the dwelling is large. You stay, and we will stay too.” “Get up, friends, the dwelling falls to us” — and, ==angry and displeased, they seized them by the neck and dragged them out==. Being dragged out, they wept. The monks asked, “Why are you weeping, friends?” “Friends, these group-of-six monks, angry and displeased, are dragging us out of a Saṅgha dwelling.”
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can the group-of-six monks, angry and displeased, drag monks out of a Saṅgha dwelling?” — and told the Blessed One. The Blessed One asked, “Is it true, monks, that you, angry … ?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked them: “Foolish men, how can you …? Foolish men, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule:
:::
!pn: The training rule
> යො පන භික්ඛු භික්ඛුං කුපිතො අනත්තමනො සඞ්ඝිකා විහාරා නික්කඩ්ඪෙය්‍ය වා නික්කඩ්ඪාපෙය්‍ය වා, පාචිත්තියං.
~ Whatever monk, angry and displeased, should expel or have expelled a monk from a Saṅgha-owned dwelling: Pācittiya.
!src: This rule has no supplementary rule (anupaññatti); the original rule is the complete training rule.
## {story} Word analysis
|! Word | Meaning in the text
| **Bhikkhuṃ** | Another monk.
| **Kupito anattamano** | Dissatisfied, with a mind struck (by anger), hardened (**khilajāta**).
| **Saṅghiko vihāro** | A dwelling given to, made over to the Saṅgha.
| **Nikkaḍḍheyya** | Seizing him in the room he drags him out to the porch (**pamukha**) — pācittiya; seizing him on the porch he drags him outside — pācittiya; ==with a single effort he makes him pass many doors — (one) pācittiya==.
| **Nikkaḍḍhāpeyya** | He commands another — pācittiya; commanded once, (the other) makes him pass many doors — (one) pācittiya.
## {story} Offences by perception and place
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| A Saṅgha dwelling | Perceiving it as the Saṅgha’s, in doubt, or perceiving it as personal, angry and displeased, he evicts (the monk) / has him evicted | PC
| (From a Saṅgha dwelling) his requisites | He removes them / has them removed | DK
| From the precinct of the dwelling, the attendance hall, a pavilion, the foot of a tree or the open | He evicts (the monk) / has him evicted | DK
| His requisites from those places | He removes them / has them removed | DK
| An unordained person — from the dwelling or from the precinct, attendance hall, pavilion, tree foot or the open | He evicts him / has him evicted | DK
| An unordained person’s requisites | He removes them / has them removed | DK
| A personal dwelling | Perceiving it as the Saṅgha’s, or in doubt | DK
| Another’s personal dwelling | Perceiving it as personal | DK
| One’s own personal dwelling | — | N
## {story} No offence
# He evicts, or has evicted, a shameless one or his requisites;
# He evicts, or has evicted, an insane one or his requisites;
# He evicts, or has evicted, one who makes disputes, quarrels, arguments, idle talk (**bhassa**) and legal issues in the Saṅgha, or his requisites;
# He evicts, or has evicted, a pupil or co-resident who does not behave properly, or his requisites;
# For the insane; for the first offender.
## {att} The number of doors and commanding
|! Situation | Offence
| In lodgings such as mansions of four or five storeys or four-sided halls with six or seven rooms, seizing him by the hands or the neck and making him pass through **with a single effort**, without stopping in between | One pācittiya
| Making him pass through **with several efforts**, stopping again and again | Pācittiyas by the number of doors
| Not touching him with the hand but evicting him **by speech**, saying “Get out” | The same method applies
| **Merely commanding**, “Evict him” | Dukkaṭa
| Commanded once, the other makes him pass many doors | (For the commander) one pācittiya
| Commanding **with a specification**, “Evict him through so many doors” or “Evict him as far as the main gate” | Pācittiyas by the number of doors
## {att} Requisites and the no-offence terms
- **Tassa parikkhāraṃ** — for one who removes, or has removed, anything of his — bowl, robe, water strainer, water pot, bed, bench, mattress, pillow (**bimbohana**) and so on, even a dye-bark chip — **dukkaṭas by the number of articles**. But for things tightly tied together, there is only one offence, says the Mahāpaccarī.
- **Aññassa puggalike** — here too, the personal property of a trusted person is like one’s own personal property; and as here, so everywhere.
|! One who may be evicted | Extent to which he may be evicted
| One who makes disputes and quarrels | **From the whole Saṅgha monastery** — for, gaining a faction, he might even split the Saṅgha
| The shameless and the like | **Only from one’s own living place**; not allowed to evict them from the whole Saṅgha monastery
- **Ummattakassa** — no offence for one who is himself insane.
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> යො පන භික්ඛු භික්ඛුං කුපිතො අනත්තමනො සඞ්ඝිකා විහාරා නික්කඩ්ඪෙය්‍ය වා නික්කඩ්ඪාපෙය්‍ය වා, පාචිත්තියං.

|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Kupito** | Angry.
| **Anattamano** | With a mind not content.
| **Nikkaḍḍheyya vā nikkaḍḍhāpeyya vā pācittiyaṃ** | (explained below)
## {kkv} The full meaning
|! Situation | Offence
| In lodgings such as many-storeyed mansions or four-sided halls with many rooms, for one who seizes him and makes him pass with a single effort, without stopping in between | One pācittiya
| For one who makes him pass with several efforts, stopping again and again | Pācittiyas by the number of doors
| For one who evicts by speech, not touching him with the hand, saying “Get out” | The same method applies
| For one who has him evicted, merely on commanding “Evict him” | Dukkaṭa
| When commanded once, even if he goes out through many doors | For the other (the commander) just one pācittiya
| When the command specifies “Evict him through so many doors” or “as far as the main gate” | Pācittiyas by the number of doors
## {kkv} The laying down of the rule and the command
%% Kind of rule | This rule, as laid down, is a **common rule** (**sādhāraṇa-paññatti**) — shared with the nuns;
%% Command | and it is **sāṇattika** — the offence also arises by getting another to do it.
!src: Here the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī text does not mention the place and the person (it says only “paññattaṃ”). According to the Pāli, it was laid down at Sāvatthī concerning the group-of-six monks.
## {kkv} Division of offences
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| A Saṅgha dwelling | The triad | PC
| A personal dwelling | The triad | DK
| His requisites | Removing them | DK
| From the attendance hall and so on, or from the precinct of the dwelling | Removing him or his requisites | DK
| An unordained person or an unordained person’s requisites — from the dwelling or from its precinct | Evicting, having evicted | DK
!src: (For requisites) the dukkaṭas should be understood by the number of requisites, when they are not tied together.
## {kkv} No offence
# In evicting from one’s own dwelling or that of a trusted person;
# In evicting, or having evicted, a troublemaker or his requisites even from the whole Saṅgha monastery;
# In evicting from one’s own living place a shameless one, an insane one, pupils and co-residents who do not behave properly, or their requisites;
# For one who is himself insane and so on.
## {kkv} Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# It is a Saṅgha dwelling;
# The ordained monk is free from being a troublemaker and so on;
# Evicting him, or having him evicted, out of anger.
## {kkv} Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
The origin and the rest are the same as in the rule on taking what is not given (**Adinnādāna-sikkhāpada**). But this one has **painful feeling**.

## {story} කථා වස්තුව
### වෙහාසකුටි වස්තුව
@ සැවැත් නුවර · ජේතවනය අනාථපිණ්ඩිකාරාමය
:::short
සංඝික විහාරයක උඩුමහල් කුටියක උඩ සිටි භික්ෂුවක් ==ආහච්චපාදක ඇඳක වහා හිඳගත්තේ== ය. ඇඳ පාදය ගිලිහී යට සිටි භික්ෂුවගේ හිස මත වැටුණෙන් ඔහු හඬ නැගුවේ ය. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක.

:::long
එක් සමයෙක බුදුරජාණන් වහන්සේ සැවැත් නුවර අනාථපිණ්ඩිකයාගේ ආරාමය වූ ජේතවනයෙහි වැඩ වසන සේක. එකල භික්ෂූන් දෙනමක් සංඝික විහාරයක **උඩුමහල් කුටියක** (**උපරිවෙහාසකුටි**) වසති; එක් අයෙක් යට වසයි, එක් අයෙක් උඩ. උඩ සිටි භික්ෂුව ==ආහච්චපාදක (ගලවිය හැකි පා ඇති) ඇඳක වහා (බර දමා) හිඳගත්තේ ය==. ඇඳ පාදය ගිලිහී යට සිටි භික්ෂුවගේ හිස මත වැටිණ. ඒ භික්ෂුව විරූප හඬක් නැගුවේ ය.
භික්ෂූහු දිව අවුත් “ඇවැත්නි, ඔබ කුමට හඬ නැගුවෙහි ද?” යි ඇසූහ. ඒ භික්ෂුව භික්ෂූන්ට ඒ කරුණ කීවේ ය. අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු “භික්ෂුවක් කෙසේ නම් සංඝික විහාරයක උඩුමහල් කුටියක ආහච්චපාදක ඇඳක වහා හිඳගන්නේ ද?” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ; භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට සැල කළහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “භික්ෂුව, ඔබ … සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂය, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් …? මෝඝපුරුෂය, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක:

## {story} අනාපත්ති
# වෙහාසකුටියක් නොවූ කුටියෙහි (**අවෙහාසකුටියා**);
# හිස ගැටෙන (උසැති) කුටියෙහි (**සීසඝට්ටාය**);
# යට කොටස පරිභෝග නොකරන්නක් නම් (**හෙට්ඨා අපරිභොගං**);
# (උඩු තලය) ලෑලිවලින් ඝනව ඇතිරූවක් නම් (**පදරසඤ්චිතං**);
# (ඇඳ පාදවලට) ඇණ ගසා ඇත්නම් (**පටාණි දින්නා**);
# එහි සිට යමක් ගනියි නම් හෝ එල්ලයි නම්;
# උමතු වූවහුට; ආදිකම්මිකයාට.
## {att} කථා වස්තුවේ පදවලට අදාළ විශේෂ කරුණු
|! පදය | සමන්තපාසාදිකා විවරණය
| **උපරිවෙහාසකුටියා** | උඩින් තලය නොවැසූ (**අච්ඡන්නතලා**) දෙමහල් කුටියක හෝ තෙමහල් ආදී කුටියක.
| **මඤ්චං සහසා අභිනිසීදි** | ඇඳ වහා යටපත් කොට, (බර) ඔබා හිඳගත්තේ ය. නැතහොත් මෙය සප්තමී අර්ථයෙහි කර්ම වචනයකි — “ඇඳෙහි හිඳගත්තේ ය” යන අර්ථයයි. “**අභි**” යනු පදයේ ශෝභනය පිණිස වූ උපසර්ගයක් පමණි.
| **නිප්පතිත්වා** | ගිලිහී වැටී, හෝ (අටනියෙන්) ඉවත් වී. එහි උඩින් ඇණයක් ද ගසා නොතිබිණ; එබැවින් ඉවත් විය.
| **විස්සරමකාසි** | විරූප වූ, ආතුර හඬක් නැගුවේ ය.
## {att} “මධ්‍යම පුරුෂයෙකුගේ හිස නොගැටෙන” — අර්ථය
- ප්‍රමාණයෙන් මධ්‍යම පුරුෂයෙකුගේ හිස සියල්ලට ම යටින් ඇති තුලා (බාල්ක) වල නොගැටේ ද, මෙයින් **මෙහි අදහස් කළ** වෙහාසකුටිය දක්වන ලදී; වෙහාසකුටියේ (සාමාන්‍ය) ලක්ෂණය නොවේ.
- උඩින් තලය නොවැසූ ඕනෑම දෙමහල් හෝ තෙමහල් ආදී කුටියක් “**වෙහාසකුටි**” යැයි කියනු ලැබේ; මෙහි අදහස් කළේ හිස නොගැටෙන එක පමණි.
- හිඳීම ආදියෙහි ප්‍රයෝග වශයෙන් ඇවැත් භේදය — හිඳීම පමණෙහි, සැතපීම පමණෙහි, දෙක ම කිරීමෙහි, නැවත නැවත කිරීමෙහි ප්‍රයෝග ගණනින් — පෙර කී ක්‍රමයට ම දත යුතුය.

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of the Raised Hut
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery
:::short
A monk on the upper floor of a raised hut in a Saṅgha dwelling ==sat down abruptly on a bed with detachable legs==. A leg came out and fell on the head of the monk below, who cried out. The Blessed One rebuked him and laid down the rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Sāvatthī, in Jetavana, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. At that time two monks were living in a **raised hut** (**uparivehāsakuṭi**) in a Saṅgha dwelling, one below and one above. The monk above ==sat down abruptly on a bed with detachable legs (āhaccapādaka)==. The leg of the bed came out and fell on the head of the monk below. That monk cried out in pain.
The monks ran up and asked, “Friend, why did you cry out?” That monk told the monks the matter. The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can a monk sit down abruptly on a bed with detachable legs in a raised hut of a Saṅgha dwelling?” — and told the Blessed One. The Blessed One asked, “Is it true, monk, that you …?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked him: “Foolish man, how can you …? Foolish man, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule:
:::
!pn: The training rule
> යො පන භික්ඛු සඞ්ඝිකෙ විහාරෙ උපරිවෙහාසකුටියා ආහච්චපාදකං මඤ්චං වා පීඨං වා අභිනිසීදෙය්‍ය වා අභිනිපජ්ජෙය්‍ය වා, පාචිත්තියං.
~ Whatever monk, in a Saṅgha-owned dwelling, should abruptly sit or lie down on a bed or bench with detachable legs in an upper-storey room: Pācittiya.
!src: This rule has no supplementary rule (anupaññatti); the original rule is the complete training rule.

## {story} No offence
# In a hut that is not raised (**avehāsakuṭiyā**);
# In one where the head touches (**sīsaghaṭṭāya**);
# When the space below is not in use (**heṭṭhā aparibhogaṃ**);
# When (the upper floor) is closely laid with planks (**padarasañcitaṃ**);
# When pegs have been fitted (to the legs) (**paṭāṇi dinnā**);
# Standing on it, he takes something or hangs something up;
# For the insane; for the first offender.
## {att} Points on words in the origin story
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Uparivehāsakuṭiyā** | In a two-storeyed hut whose upper floor is not covered over (**acchannatalā**), or in a three-storeyed hut and so on.
| **Mañcaṃ sahasā abhinisīdi** | He sat down abruptly, overpowering and pressing down on the bed. Or this is an accusative used in the sense of location — the meaning is “he sat on the bed”. “**Abhi**” is merely a prefix for the beauty of the word.
| **Nippatitvā** | Having fallen out, or come out. No pin had been fitted above it; so it came out.
| **Vissaramakāsi** | He made an ugly, distressed cry.
## {att} “Where the head of a man of average height does not touch” — the meaning
- One where the head of a man of average size does not touch the lowest beams of all: by this, **the raised hut intended here** is shown, not the (general) definition of a raised hut.
- Any two-storeyed or three-storeyed hut whose upper floor is not covered over is called a “**vehāsakuṭi**”; but here only one where the head does not touch is intended.
- In sitting and so on, the division of offences by effort — merely sitting, merely lying down, doing both, and doing it again and again by the number of efforts — should be understood in the way stated before.
## {att} Explanation of the no-offence terms
|! Term | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Avehāsakuṭiyā** | No offence in leaf huts and the like built on the ground — for one cannot harm another there.
| **Sīsaghaṭṭāya** | No offence in one where the head touches either — since one cannot walk about upright on the lower floor there, it is not a place for moving about, so there will be no harm to others.
| **Heṭṭhā aparibhogaṃ** | No offence either in one whose lower part is not in use because timber and the like are stored there.
| **Padarasañcitaṃ** | No offence either in one whose upper floor is closely laid with wooden planks, or finished with plaster and the like.
| **Paṭāṇi dinnā** | Pins have been fitted into the tips of the legs of the bed or bench, so that they do not fall out even when one sits; no offence for one who sits on such a bed or bench.
| **Tasmiṃ ṭhito** | Standing on a bed or bench with detachable legs, he takes a robe or something hung on a peg (**nāgadantaka**) above, or hangs up something else — no offence for him either.

## {kkv} No offence
# In one’s own dwelling or that of a trusted person;
# In a hut that is not raised; in one where the head touches;
# For one who sits on a bed with detachable legs where the space below is not in use because timber and the like are stored there, or where the upper floor is closely laid with planks and finished with plaster and the like;
# For one who, standing on a bed with detachable legs in that raised place, takes or hangs something;
# For one who sits on one fitted with pegs (**paṭāṇi dinnā**), pins inserted above the tips of the legs;
# For the insane and so on.
## {kkv} Factors (= cattāri — four)
# It is a Saṅgha dwelling;
# It is a raised hut where the head does not touch;
# The space below is in use;
# Sitting or lying down on a bed or bench with detachable legs not fitted with pegs.
## {kkv} Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
The origin and the rest are the same as in the **Eḷakaloma rule**.

## {story} කථා වස්තුව
### මහල්ලකවිහාර වස්තුව
@ කොසඹෑ නුවර · ඝෝසිතාරාමය
:::short
ඡන්නයන්ට මහාමාත්‍යයෙක් කරවූ විහාරය නිම වූ පසුත් ඔව්හු ==නැවත නැවත සෙවිලි කරවා, බදාම කරවූහ==; අධික බරින් විහාරය කඩා වැටිණ. තණ හා දර ඇදීමේදී බ්‍රාහ්මණයෙකුගේ යව කෙතක් ද නැසිණ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක.

එක් සමයෙක බුදුරජාණන් වහන්සේ කොසඹෑ නුවර ඝෝසිතාරාමයෙහි වැඩ වසන සේක. එකල ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයන්ගේ උපස්ථායක වූ මහාමාත්‍යයෙක් ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයන්ට විහාරයක් කරවයි. ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයෝ ==කර නිම කළ විහාරය නැවත නැවත සෙවිලි කරවති, නැවත නැවත බදාම කරවති==. අධික බර වූ විහාරය කඩා වැටිණ.
ඉක්බිති ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයෝ තණ ද දර ද ඇදගෙන යමින් එක්තරා **බ්‍රාහ්මණයෙකුගේ යව කෙතක්** නැසූහ. ඒ බ්‍රාහ්මණයා “භවත්හු කෙසේ නම් අපගේ යව කෙත නසන්නාහු ද?” යි අවමන් කළේ ය, දොස් කීවේ ය, නින්දා කළේ ය. භික්ෂූහු ඒ බ්‍රාහ්මණයාගේ නින්දාව ඇසූහ.
අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු “ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයෝ කෙසේ නම් කර නිම කළ විහාරය නැවත නැවත සෙවිලි කරවන්නාහු ද, නැවත නැවත බදාම කරවන්නාහු ද? අධික බර වූ විහාරය කඩා වැටිණ” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ; භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට සැල කළහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “ඡන්නය, ඔබ … සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂය, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් …? මෝඝපුරුෂය, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක:

| **තතො චෙ උත්තරි** | මාර්ග තුනට හෝ වට තුනට උඩින් සතරවන මාර්ගයෙහි හෝ වටයෙහි.

## {kkv} ශික්ෂාපදය හා පදාර්ථය
> මහල්ලකං පන භික්ඛුනා විහාරං කාරයමානෙන යාව ද්වාරකොසා අග්ගළට්ඨපනාය ආලොකසන්ධිපරිකම්මාය ද්වත්තිච්ඡදනස්ස පරියායං අප්පහරිතෙ ඨිතෙන අධිට්ඨාතබ්බං, තතො චෙ උත්තරි අප්පහරිතෙපි ඨිතො අධිට්ඨහෙය්‍ය, පාචිත්තියං.
මෙය **භූතගාම වග්ගයේ නවවන ශික්ෂාපදයයි** (**නවමෙ**). කඞ්ඛාවිතරණිය පද මෙසේ විවරණය කරයි:
|! පදය | කඞ්ඛාවිතරණී විවරණය
| **මහල්ලකං** | හිමිකරුවෙකු ඇති (**සස්සාමික**).
| **විහාරං** | ඇතුළතින් හා පිටතින් බදාම කළ.
| **යාව ද්වාරකොසා** | ද්වාරකෝෂය නම් උපරිම සීමාවෙන් දොර බාහුවේ අවට **රියන් දෙකහමාරක** ප්‍රදේශයයි.
| **අග්ගළට්ඨපනාය** | දොර පියන සහිත දොර රාමුව ස්ථිර කිරීම පිණිස. සැහැල්ලුවෙන් කැරකෙන දොර පියන විවෘත කරන කල බිත්තියේ ද, වසන කල දොර රාමුවේ ද ගැටේ; ඒ ගැටීමෙන් බිත්තිය සෙලවේ, එයින් මැටි සෙලවී ලිහිල් වේ හෝ වැටේ. “මෙය කළ යුතුය” යි මාතිකාවෙහි හෝ පදභාජනයෙහි නොකීව ද, කථා වස්තුවේ අධිකාරය අනුව දොර බාහුව දක්වා දොර ස්ථිර කිරීම පිණිස නැවත නැවත බදාම කළ යුතුය, හෝ කරවිය යුතුය යන අර්ථය දත යුතුය.
| **ආලොකසන්ධිපරිකම්මාය** | ආලෝක සන්ධි නම් කවුළු පියන් ය; ඒවා විවෘත කරන කල වියතක් පමණ හෝ ඊට වැඩි බිත්ති ප්‍රදේශයක ගැටේ; උපචාරය සියලු දිශාවල ලැබේ. එබැවින් සියලු දිශාවල කවුළු පියනේ පළල තරම් අවකාශය කවුළු පිරියම් පිණිස බදාම කළ යුතුය, හෝ කරවිය යුතුය.
| **ද්වත්තිච්ඡදනස්ස පරියායං** | සෙවිල්ලෙහි වට දෙක තුනක් — “පරියාය” නම් වටයයි; වට දෙකක් හෝ වට තුනක් විධාන කළ යුතුය යන අර්ථයයි.
| **අප්පහරිතෙ ඨිතෙන** | හරිත නැති තැනක සිට. **හරිත** — සත් වැදෑරුම් ධාන්‍ය ආදී පූර්වාන්නය ද, මුං, මෑ, තල, කොල්ලු, ලබු, කොමඩු ආදී අපරාන්නය ද අදහස් කෙරේ. යම් කෙතක වපුළ දෙය තවම නොහටගෙන, වැසි වැටුණු කල හටගනී නම්, එය ද හරිත ගණයට ම යයි; එහි සිට විධාන කරන්නා **දුක්කටයට** පැමිණේ.

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of the Large Dwelling
@ Kosambī · Ghositārāma
:::short
After a minister finished a dwelling for Ven. Channa, he ==had it roofed and plastered again and again== until, overloaded, it collapsed; gathering grass and wood, he also damaged a brahmin’s barley field. The Blessed One rebuked him and laid down the rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Kosambī, in the Ghositārāma. At that time a high minister, a supporter of Ven. Channa, was having a dwelling built for Ven. Channa. Ven. Channa ==had the finished dwelling roofed again and again, and plastered again and again==. Overloaded, the dwelling collapsed.
Then Ven. Channa, gathering grass and wood, damaged a certain **brahmin’s barley field**. That brahmin complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can the venerable ones damage our barley field?” The monks heard the brahmin complaining.
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can Ven. Channa have a finished dwelling roofed again and again and plastered again and again? Overloaded, the dwelling has collapsed!” — and told the Blessed One. The Blessed One asked, “Is it true, Channa, that you …?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked him: “Foolish man, how can you …? Foolish man, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule:
:::
!pn: The training rule
> මහල්ලකං පන භික්ඛුනා විහාරං කාරයමානෙන යාවද්වාරකොසා අග්ගළට්ඨපනාය ආලොකසන්ධිපරිකම්මාය ද්වත්තිච්ඡදනස්ස පරියායං අප්පහරිතෙ ඨිතෙන අධිට්ඨාතබ්බං. තතො චෙ උත්තරිං අප්පහරිතෙපි ඨිතො අධිට්ඨහෙය්‍ය, පාචිත්තියං.
~ When a monk is having a large dwelling built, he may, standing where there are no crops, give instructions two or three times about the covering of the roof, as far as the door-frame, the fittings for the bolt, and the window openings. If he gives instructions beyond that, even standing where there are no crops: Pācittiya.
!src: This rule has no supplementary rule (anupaññatti); the original rule is the complete training rule.
## {story} Word analysis
|! Word | Meaning in the text
| **Mahallako vihāro** | A dwelling that has an owner (**sassāmika**) is meant.
| **Vihāro** | Plastered inside, plastered outside, or plastered inside and outside.
| **Kārayamānena** | Building it himself or having it built.
| **Yāva dvārakosā** | Within arm’s reach (**hatthapāsa**) all around the door-post (**piṭṭhasaṅghāṭa**).
| **Aggaḷaṭṭhapanāya** | For fixing the door.
| **Ālokasandhiparikammāya** | For finishing the windows — white colour, black colour, red-ochre work, garland work, creeper work, makara-tooth work, five-strip (**pañcapaṭika**) work.
| **Haritaṃ** | The first crops (**pubbaṇṇa**) and the later crops (**aparaṇṇa**). If he directs while standing among crops, a **dukkaṭa**.
| **Dvatticchadanassa pariyāyaṃ** | One roofing in courses (**maggena**) should direct two courses, give instructions for the third, and leave. One roofing in layers (**pariyāyena**) should direct two layers, give instructions for the third, and leave.
| **Tato ce uttari** | For one roofing with bricks, a pācittiya **for each brick**; with stones, **for each stone**; with plaster, **for each lump**; with grass, **for each handful of grass**; with leaves, **for each leaf**.

## {att} “Yāva dvārakosā” — the area around the door-post
|! Source | Extent of the dvārakosa
| Samantapāsādikā | An area the width of the door panel all around the door-post (**piṭṭhasaṅghāṭa**)
| Mahāpaccarī | One and a half cubits from the door-post
| Kurundī | The size of the door panel on both sides of the door
| Mahā-aṭṭhakathā (**well stated**) | “A door panel may be one and a half, two, or two and a half cubits” — it is with reference to this that the Blessed One gave the maximum, “within arm’s reach all around the door-post”
- **Aggaḷaṭṭhapanāya** — for fixing the door-frame together with its panel; the word analysis “for fixing the door” was spoken with this same meaning in mind.
- **The idea** — a lightly swinging door panel strikes the wall when opened and the frame when closed. By that striking the wall shakes; then the clay moves, and having moved it becomes loose or falls. Therefore the Blessed One said, “yāva dvārakosā aggaḷaṭṭhapanāya”.
- Although “this is to be done” is said neither in the mātikā nor in the word analysis, by the context in the origin story — “he had it roofed again and again, plastered again and again” — the meaning should be seen as: **up to the door-post, for fixing the door, it may be plastered, or have plaster applied, again and again**.
## {att} The surrounding area — doors and windows
- For a dwelling with a door in the middle and a high wall above, the surrounding area is arm’s reach all around in three directions; for a small dwelling, in two directions. Even there, whatever wall the opened panel strikes may have an incomplete surrounding area. By the maximum limit, plastering is allowed for arm’s reach all around in three directions to fix the door. If there is also room for plaster below the door, it is allowed to plaster that too.
- **Ālokasandhi** — the window panels; these too, when opened, strike a span or more of wall. Here the surrounding area is obtained in all directions; so in all directions an area the width of the window panel may be plastered, or have plaster applied, for finishing the windows.
- **Setavaṇṇa** (white colour) and so on are not the analysis of a mātikā word. Since these put no load on the dwelling, they were allowed in the word analysis itself; so all of this may be done as one likes.
## {att} “Harita” and where to stand when directing
- **Harita** — here the first crops of the seven kinds of grain, and the later crops such as mung beans, black gram, sesame, horse gram, gourds and pumpkins, are meant.
- In a field where the seed sown has not yet come up, but will come up when the rain falls, that too counts as crops. So one should not direct while standing in such a field either; one should direct only while standing where there are no crops.
%% Limit | A man seated at the side of the ridge-pole (**piṭṭhivaṃsa**), the pinnacle of a peaked roof, or the finial above,
%% Looking | looking from the edge of the eaves, sees (the monk) standing on some piece of ground,
%% Looking back | and (the monk) standing on that ground sees the one seated above —
%% Ruling | he should direct from that place; inside it, even where there are no crops, he may not stand and direct —
%% Reason | for when the dwelling falls, this is the space where it falls.
## {att} Roofing in courses and in layers
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Maggena chādentassa** | Roofing straight on, without going round — this is done with bricks, stones and plaster.
| **Dve magge adhiṭṭhahitvā** | If the two courses are badly roofed, one may remove them and roof again and again; so he should direct two courses as he wishes, instruct for the third, “now roof it like this”, and leave.
| **Pariyāyena** | In layers — such roofing is done with grass and leaves. Here too he should direct two layers as he wishes, instruct for the third, and leave. If he does not leave, he should stand silent.
| **Chadanūpari** | All this roofing should be understood as roofing on top of roofing — they roof like this thinking that a dwelling roofed layer upon layer will not leak for a long time.
| **Tato ce uttari** | In a fourth course or layer, above the three courses or layers.
| **Karaḷe karaḷe** | For each handful of grass.
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> මහල්ලකං පන භික්ඛුනා විහාරං කාරයමානෙන යාව ද්වාරකොසා අග්ගළට්ඨපනාය ආලොකසන්ධිපරිකම්මාය ද්වත්තිච්ඡදනස්ස පරියායං අප්පහරිතෙ ඨිතෙන අධිට්ඨාතබ්බං, තතො චෙ උත්තරි අප්පහරිතෙපි ඨිතො අධිට්ඨහෙය්‍ය, පාචිත්තියං.
This is **the ninth rule of the Bhūtagāma chapter** (**navame**). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explains the words thus:
|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Mahallakaṃ** | Having an owner (**sassāmika**).
| **Vihāraṃ** | Plastered inside and outside.
| **Yāva dvārakosā** | The dvārakosa is, by the maximum limit, an area of **two and a half cubits** around the door-post.
| **Aggaḷaṭṭhapanāya** | For fixing the door-frame with its panel. A lightly swinging door panel strikes the wall when opened and the frame when closed; by that striking the wall shakes, then the clay moves and becomes loose or falls. Although “this is to be done” is said neither in the mātikā nor in the word analysis, by the context of the origin story the meaning should be seen as: up to the door-post, for fixing the door, it may be plastered, or have plaster applied, again and again.
| **Ālokasandhiparikammāya** | Ālokasandhi are the window panels; when opened they strike a span or more of wall; the surrounding area is obtained in all directions. So in all directions an area the width of the window panel may be plastered, or have plaster applied, for finishing the windows.
| **Dvatticchadanassa pariyāyaṃ** | Two or three layers of roofing — “pariyāya” means a layer; the meaning is that two or three layers may be directed.
| **Appaharite ṭhitena** | Standing where there are no crops. **Harita** — the first crops of the seven kinds of grain and so on, and the later crops such as mung beans, black gram, sesame, horse gram, gourds and pumpkins, are meant. What has been sown in a field and has not yet come up, but will come up when rain falls, also counts as crops; one who directs standing there incurs a **dukkaṭa**.

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of Sprinkling
@ Āḷavī · Aggāḷava shrine
:::short
Doing building work, the Āḷavaka monks ==knowingly sprinkled water containing living beings on grass and clay==. The Blessed One rebuked them and laid down the rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Āḷavī, at the Aggāḷava shrine. At that time the Āḷavaka monks, doing building work, ==knowing that the water contained living beings, sprinkled it on grass and on clay, and had it sprinkled==.
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can the Āḷavaka monks, knowing that water contains living beings, sprinkle it on grass and on clay, and have it sprinkled?” Having criticised them in many ways, they told the Blessed One. The Blessed One asked, “Is it true, monks, that you …?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked them: “Foolish men, how can you …? Foolish men, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule:
:::
!pn: The training rule
> යො පන භික්ඛු ජානං සප්පාණකං උදකං තිණං වා මත්තිකං වා සිඤ්චෙය්‍ය වා සිඤ්චාපෙය්‍ය වා, පාචිත්තියං.
~ Whatever monk, knowing that water contains living beings, should sprinkle it on grass or clay, or cause it to be sprinkled: Pācittiya.
!src: This rule has no supplementary rule (anupaññatti); the original rule is the complete training rule.
## {story} Word analysis
|! Word | Meaning in the text
| **Jānāti** | He knows it himself, or others inform him.
| **Siñceyya** | He sprinkles it himself — pācittiya.
| **Siñcāpeyya** | He commands another — pācittiya; ==commanded once, (the other) sprinkles even a great deal — (one) pācittiya==.

## {story} No offence
# Unintentionally (**asañcicca**);
# Without mindfulness (**assatiyā**);
# For one who does not know (**ajānantassa**);
# For one who is insane (**ummattakassa**);
# For the first offender (**ādikammikassa**).
## {att} “Jānaṃ sappāṇakaṃ” and the number of offences
- **Jānaṃ sappāṇakaṃ** — knowing in some way or other, “this has living beings in it”.
- **Siñceyya vā siñcāpeyya vā** — he sprinkles with that water himself, or commands another and has it sprinkled.
|! Act | Number of offences
| Sprinkling without breaking the stream — from one water pot | One offence (the same method for all vessels)
| Sprinkling while breaking the stream | An offence for each effort
| He opens a channel (**mātikā**) towards (the field) — even if it flows the whole day | One offence
| He blocks it here and there and leads it from one place to another | An offence for each effort
| He throws even a cartload of grass into the water with one effort | One offence
| He throws in grass or leaves one by one | An offence for each effort
| With clay, wood, cow dung and the like | The same method
!src: This was not said with reference to a great body of water; it should be understood as said with reference to water that is used up (dries out), or becomes muddied, when grass or clay is thrown in — water in which the beings die.
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> යො පන භික්ඛු ජානං සප්පාණකං උදකං තිණං වා මත්තිකං වා සිඤ්චෙය්‍ය වා සිඤ්චාපෙය්‍ය වා, පාචිත්තියං.
This is **the tenth (last) rule of the Bhūtagāma chapter** (**dasame**). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explains the words thus:
|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Jānaṃ sappāṇakaṃ** | Knowing in any way, by seeing or hearing, “this has living beings in it”.
| **Siñceyya vā siñcāpeyya vā** | He sprinkles with that water himself, or commands another and has it sprinkled.
## {kkv} The full meaning
|! Act | Number of offences
| Sprinkling without breaking the stream — from one pot | One offence
| Breaking the stream | Offences by the number of efforts
| He opens a channel towards (the field) — even if it flows the whole day | One offence
| He blocks it here and there and leads it elsewhere | Offences by the number of efforts
| He throws much grass, leaves, branches and the like into the water with one effort | One offence
| He throws them in one by one | Offences by the number of efforts
| In having it sprinkled, on the command | DK
| On one command, even if a great deal is sprinkled | Just one pācittiya for the one who commands
!src: This is said with reference to water that, when things are thrown in like this, is used up or becomes muddied so that the beings die — not a great body of water.

## {kkv} Division of offences
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| Water with living beings | Perceiving it as having beings, he sprinkles / has it sprinkled | PC
| Water without living beings | For one who perceives it as having beings | DK
| In both cases (with / without beings) | For one in doubt | DK
## {kkv} No offence
# For one who perceives it as without beings;
# For one who sprinkles unintentionally or without mindfulness;
# For one who does not know;
# For the insane and so on.
## {kkv} Factors (= cattāri — four)
# The water contains living beings;
# Knowing, “the beings will die by sprinkling”;
# The water is in fact like that;
# Sprinkling grass and so on for some purpose, without the intention to kill.
## {kkv} Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
The origin and the rest are the same as in the rule on taking what is not given (**Adinnādāna-sikkhāpada**). But this one is **paṇṇattivajja**, **ticitta** and **tivedana**.
