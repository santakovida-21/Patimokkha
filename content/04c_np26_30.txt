## The Story
@ Rājagaha · Veḷuvana, the squirrels' feeding place
At robe-making time **the group-of-six** ==asked for a great deal of yarn==. Even after the robes were made much yarn was left over. “Let's ask for more yarn and **have weavers weave robes**,” they said, and did so. Yarn was still left after weaving, so **a second and a third time** they asked for yarn and had robes woven. People complained, “The Sakyan ascetics **ask for yarn themselves and have weavers weave robes**!” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Sāmaṃ** — having asked himself.
**Suttaṃ** — yarn of the six kinds, or what is comparable:
|! Yarn | Made from
| **Khoma** | flax / linen fibre
| **Kappāsika** | cotton
| **Koseyya** | silk spun from cocoons
| **Kambala** | sheep's wool
| **Sāṇa** | hemp fibre
| **Bhaṅga** | a mixture of the above five (some say a separate bark fibre)
**Viññāpetvā** — having asked for it for the sake of a robe.
**Vāyāpeyya** — has it woven with **an unallowable request**: “Weave me a robe, friend.”
**Tantavāyehi** — by weavers.
## (B) Allowable and unallowable yarn and weavers
**Yarn** — yarn one has asked for oneself is **unallowable**; yarn received through relatives and the like is **allowable**.
**Weaver** — one engaged **by asking**, being unrelated and not having invited, is **unallowable**; a relative, one who invited, or one engaged **for payment** is **allowable**.
If the weaver lacks loom parts and, saying “I'll fetch them from the forest,” sharpens an adze or axe — from then on, ==**for every effort** he makes for tools or for weaving the robe, the monk incurs a dukkaṭa==. When **a span in length and a cubit in width** have been woven — the minimum assignable robe size — it is **nissaggiya pācittiya**. As the robe grows, **the offences grow by this measure**.
|! Yarn | Weaver | Offence
| unallowable (asked for) | unallowable | **nissaggiya pācittiya** for each span × cubit
| allowable | unallowable | **dukkaṭa** by the same measure
| unallowable | allowable | **dukkaṭa** by the same measure
| allowable | allowable (relative / invited / paid) | no offence (for the weaving)
**Mixed yarn** — in a robe woven **in sections like the plots of a field**, where an unallowable-yarn section equals **the minimum robe size**, there is **pācittiya** for it and **dukkaṭa** for the others; if the sections are smaller — **even the size of an eye** — **dukkaṭa** for every section by count. Woven **with alternate threads**, or **allowable lengthwise and unallowable crosswise**, there is **dukkaṭa for every board-length (phalaka)**. When an allowable and an unallowable weaver work in turns, the offences divide the same way; if both **hold the reed together**, it is **pācittiya** for each board-length of unallowable yarn.
## (C) When the ten days begin
If both yarn and weaver are allowable there is no offence for the weaving. But to guard against the **ten-day rule (nissaggiya 1)**, ==as soon as an assignable size has been woven it should be **determined while still on the loom**==.
|! The weaver said | The ten days count from
| “I'll have it woven by such-and-such day” | the day he fixed
| “I'll weave it and send word” | the day **the messenger he sent** informs him (another's report doesn't count)
| “I'll send it by someone” | the day the monk who brought it **hands it over**
While **even a kākaṇika** of the weaving fee remains unpaid, the robe is not yet his, so he is protected.
> Idaṃ me, bhante, cīvaraṃ sāmaṃ suttaṃ viññāpetvā tantavāyehi vāyāpitaṃ nissaggiyaṃ, imāhaṃ saṅghassa nissajjāmi.
## (D) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Rājagaha** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of having robes woven. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (E) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Having asked-for yarn woven by an asked-for weaver — for each effort of the weaver | DK
| When a span by a cubit is woven — knowing it is being woven, in doubt, or perceiving it as not (**triple pācittiya**) | NP
| An asked-for weaver weaving yarn not asked for, or an allowable weaver weaving asked-for yarn | DK
| Mixed yarn — sections smaller than the minimum / alternate threads / lengthwise–crosswise mix | DK
| Not woven, perceived as woven or in doubt | DK
## (F) No offence
# Asking for yarn **to sew robes**.
# For a **back-strap (āyoga)**, **belt**, **shoulder-strap**, **bowl-bag** or **water-strainer**.
# Having allowable yarn woven by **relatives** or **those who invited**.
# **For another**; with **one's own funds**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (G) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Yarn asked for the sake of a robe**.
# **For oneself**.
# Having it woven **by an unallowable weaver through an unallowable request**.
## (H) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Go-between origin** (six origins), **by doing, no release by perception, without intention, an offence by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of consciousness, three feelings**.

**භික්ඛුං පනෙව උද්දිස්ස** — භික්ෂුව සඳහා, භික්ෂුව අරමුණු කොට, ඔහුට සිවුරකින් පිදීමට කැමතිව.
**අඤ්ඤාතකො** — මවගේ හෝ පියාගේ පැත්තෙන් **සත්වන මුත්තා පරම්පරාව** දක්වා සම්බන්ධයක් නැති තැනැත්තා.
**චීවරං** — සය වර්ගයෙන් එකක් — විකප්පනයට සුදුසු අවම ප්‍රමාණය.
**තත්‍ර චෙ සො භික්ඛු** — රෙදි වියන්නන් සිටින **ගමේ හෝ නියම්ගමේ** — යමෙකු උදෙසා සිවුර වියනු ලැබේ ද ඒ භික්ෂුව.
**පුබ්බෙ අප්පවාරිතො** — සිවුරේ හිමිකරුවන් විසින් "**ස්වාමීනි, ඔබට කෙබඳු සිවුරක් ඕනෑ ද? කෙබඳු සිවුරක් වියවන්න ද?**" යි පෙරාතුව නොකියන ලද්දේ.
**තන්තවායෙ උපසඞ්කමිත්වා** — ගෙදරට ගොස් හෝ කොතැනක හෝ හමු වී.
**විකප්පං ආපජ්ජෙය්‍ය** — **විශේෂ සැකසුමක්, අධික විධානයක්** කරයි.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
A man going abroad told his wife, “**Weigh out yarn, give it to such-and-such weaver, have a robe woven and keep it; when I return I'll present Ven. Upananda with a robe.**” A monk on alms round overheard and told Ven. **Upananda**, “You have great merit.”
Upananda went to the weaver and said, “This robe is being woven **for me**. ==**Make it long, wide and dense, well woven, well spread, well scraped and well combed**; perhaps we'll give you something too.==” The weaver went to the woman for more yarn. “Didn't I tell you to weave the robe with that yarn?” “Ven. Upananda told me so.” She gave **as much yarn again** as she had first given.
Hearing the man had returned, Upananda went to his house and sat down. The man gave the robe. Learning afterwards what had happened, he complained, “**These ascetics have great wishes and are not content; it's not easy to give them robes. How can Ven. Upananda, uninvited, go to the weavers and lay down specifications for the robe?**” Asked by the Blessed One, “Is he a relative?” Upananda answered, “Not a relative.” The Blessed One rebuked him — “**An unrelated person does not know what is fitting or unfitting for an unrelated one**” — and laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Bhikkhuṃ paneva uddissa** — for the sake of a monk, with the monk in mind, wishing to clothe him.
**Aññātako** — one not related on the mother's or father's side back **to the seventh generation**.
**Cīvaraṃ** — any of the six kinds — the minimum assignable size.
**Tatra ce so bhikkhu** — the monk for whom the robe is being woven, in **the village or town** where the weavers are.
**Pubbe appavārito** — not told beforehand by the robe's owners, “**Venerable sir, what kind of robe do you need? What kind shall we have woven for you?**”
**Tantavāye upasaṅkamitvā** — going to their house or meeting them anywhere.
**Vikappaṃ āpajjeyya** — **makes special stipulations**, extra arrangements.
## (B) “Vikappa” — having the robe improved
|! Pāli word | Meaning
| **Āyataṃ** | long
| **Vitthataṃ** | wide
| **Appitaṃ** | dense
| **Suvītaṃ** | well woven — even throughout
| **Suppavāyitaṃ** | well spread on the loom — even throughout
| **Suvilekhitaṃ** | well scraped with the scraper
| **Suvitacchitaṃ** | well combed with the comb — well cleaned
**Antamaso piṇḍapātamattampi** — “alms” means **gruel, rice, snacks, a ball of bath powder, a tooth-stick, a thread of fringe** — ==**even speaking a talk on Dhamma**==.
==Merely giving alms does not make it nissaggiya.== If at his word the weavers take yarn from the robe's owners and make it **even slightly longer, wider or denser** — **dukkaṭa** for the monk at their **efforts**, and **nissaggiya on obtaining** the robe.
> Idaṃ me, bhante, cīvaraṃ pubbe appavārito aññātakassa gahapatikassa tantavāye upasaṅkamitvā cīvare vikappaṃ āpannaṃ nissaggiyaṃ, imāhaṃ saṅghassa nissajjāmi.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning Ven. Upananda, on the matter of making stipulations about a robe. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The weavers' **efforts** at his word to lengthen / widen / thicken | DK
| Obtaining the robe — perceiving them as unrelated, in doubt, or as related (**triple pācittiya**) | NP
| Relatives perceived as unrelated, or in doubt | DK
## (E) No offence
# Through the weavers of **relatives** or **those who invited**.
# **For another**.
# With **one's own funds**.
# Having **a cheaper one** woven for those wanting to have **a costly one** woven.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= cattāri — four)
# Going to the weavers of **unrelated people who had not invited** and **making stipulations**.
# The robe being **for oneself**.

## කථා වස්තුව
@ සැවැත් නුවර · ජේතවනාරාමය
**සේනාව සමඟ** ගමනක් යන එක් **මහාමාත්‍යයෙක්** "ස්වාමීන් වහන්සේලා වඩිත්වා; **වස්සාවාසිකයක්** (වස් සිවුරක්) දෙමි" යි භික්ෂූන් වෙත දූතයෙකු එවීය. "වස් වැසූ අයට වස්සාවාසිකය අනුදැන ඇත" යි ==කුකුස් ඇතිව භික්ෂූහු නොගියහ==. මහාමාත්‍යයා "**මම සේනාව සමඟ යමි; ජීවිතය දැනගත නොහැකිය, මරණය දැනගත නොහැකිය**" යි දොස් කීය. එවිට භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ **අච්චේක සිවුරක් පිළිගෙන තැබීමට** අනුදැන්නහ.
පසුව භික්ෂූහු ඒ අනුදැනීම මුවා කොට අච්චේක සිවුරු පිළිගෙන ==**චීවර කාලය ඉක්මවා** තැබූහ==; ඒවා **සිවුරු වංශයේ මිටි බැඳ** තිබිණ. සෙනසුන් චාරිකාවේ ඇවිද්ද **ආනන්ද** තෙරුන් ඒ දැක, "කොපමණ කලක සිට තැබුවා දැ?" යි අසා දොස් කීහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූහ.
## (අ) පද විවරණය
**දසාහානාගතං** — දින දහයකින් නොපැමිණි — එනම් **පවාරණයට දින දහයක් ඇති කල්හි** පටන්.
**කත්තිකතෙමාසිකපුණ්ණමං** — **පළමු කත්තික (පවාරණ) පුර පසළොස්වක** — "පවාරණ කත්තිකා" යි කියනු ලැබේ.
ඉදින් **පවාරණ මාසයේ පුර පස්වක සිට** — ඒ දින දහය තුළ — භික්ෂුවකට අච්චේක සිවුරක් ලැබුණේ නම්, "**මෙය අච්චේක ය**" යි දැනගෙන සියල්ල පිළිගත යුතුය. මෙයින් ඒ දින දහය තුළ ලැබෙන සිවුරේ **තැබිය හැකි කාලය** දැක්වේ. "දසාහපරමං අතිරෙකචීවරං" (නිස්සග්ගිය 1) මගින් ම මෙය සිද්ධ වන නමුත්, **අට්ඨුප්පත්තිය** (සිදුවීම) නිසා අලුත් අර්ථයක් මෙන් දක්වා මේ ශික්ෂාපදය තබන ලදී.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
A **high official** about to set out **with the army** sent a messenger to the monks: “Let the venerables come; I will give **a rains-residence gift**.” Thinking, “The rains-residence gift is allowed for those who have completed the rains,” ==the monks were scrupulous and did not go==. The official complained, “**I am going with the army — life is uncertain, death is uncertain.**” So the Blessed One allowed them **to accept urgent robe-cloth and store it**.
Then, on the strength of this allowance, monks accepted urgent robe-cloth and ==kept it **beyond the robe season**==; it lay **tied in bundles on the robe-pole**. Ven. **Ānanda**, touring the lodgings, saw it, asked how long it had been kept, and criticised them. The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Dasāhānāgataṃ** — not yet reached by ten days, i.e. from the time **when the Pavāraṇā is ten days away**.
**Kattikatemāsikapuṇṇamaṃ** — **the first Kattika (Pavāraṇā) full moon**, called “Pavāraṇā Kattikā.”
If, **from the fifth of the waxing half of the Pavāraṇā month** — within those ten days — urgent robe-cloth comes to a monk, knowing “**this is urgent**,” he should accept it all. This shows **how long** cloth arising in those ten days may be stored. Though this already follows from “dasāhaparamaṃ atirekacīvaraṃ” (nissaggiya 1), the rule was set down because of **the incident**, as if presenting something new.
**Accekacīvaraṃ** — **urgent** robe-cloth —
|! Donor | Circumstance
| one about to go **with the army** | return uncertain
| one about to **go abroad** | likewise
| one who is **sick** | life uncertain
| a **pregnant woman** | likewise
| one in whom **faith** has newly arisen | mere faith
| one in whom **confidence** has newly arisen | strong faith
— the cloth announced by such a person, by messenger or in person, with “**I will give a rains-residence gift**,” is urgent robe-cloth. **Non-urgent** cloth arising from the sixth day, and cloth relinquished and stored, also get this same exemption.
## (B) Marking it and the robe season
**Accekaṃ maññamānena … nikkhipitabbaṃ** — it should be stored with **some mark**: “**idaṃ accekacīvaraṃ**.” Why? If it is **distributed before the Pavāraṇā**, ==the one who took it **must not break his rains**; if he does, the cloth becomes **Saṅgha property**==. The mark makes it easy to identify and give later.
**Cīvarakālasamayo** — **without the kaṭhina**, **the last month of the rains**; **with the kaṭhina**, **five months**.
**Tato ce uttari nikkhipeyya** — without the kaṭhina, passing **the last day of the rains**; with it, passing **the day the kaṭhina ends** — it becomes nissaggiya.
|! Cloth | Exemption
| extra robe-cloth | 10 days
| urgent robe-cloth — no kaṭhina | ==**one month and 11 days**==
| urgent robe-cloth — with kaṭhina | ==**five months and 11 days**==
| unmade rains-cloth material | 5 / 6 / 10 / at most **11** months
| base-cloth kept with expectation | one month
Urgent cloth **not yet distributed** — held for the Saṅgha or **in the storeroom** — entails no offence even beyond the robe season; “perceiving urgent cloth as urgent” etc. refers to **what has been distributed**.
> Idaṃ me, bhante, accekacīvaraṃ cīvarakālasamayaṃ atikkāmitaṃ nissaggiyaṃ, imāhaṃ saṅghassa nissajjāmi.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning a number of monks, on the matter of keeping urgent robe-cloth beyond the robe season. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Keeping urgent cloth beyond the robe season — perceiving it as urgent, in doubt, or as not urgent (**triple pācittiya**) | NP
| Not determined, not assigned, not given, not lost … — perceived as such — kept beyond the season | NP
| Using a nissaggiya robe without forfeiting it | DK
| Non-urgent cloth perceived as urgent, or in doubt | DK
## (E) No offence
Within the season —
# **determined**; **assigned**; **given away**;
# **lost**, **destroyed**, **burnt**;
# **snatched**; **taken on trust**;
# for the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= cattāri — four)
# **Urgent robe-cloth** — one's own, of proper material and size.
# **Distributed** (taken as one's share).
# **Not determined / assigned**.
# **The robe season passing**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Kathina origin, by not doing, no release by perception, without intention, an offence by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of consciousness, three feelings**.

**සප්පටිභයානි** — ආරාමයේ හෝ උපචාරයේ **සොරුන් විසින් මිනිසුන් මරා, කොල්ලකා, පහර දී ඇති බව** දක්නට ලැබෙන — **ළඟ ම ඇති බලවත් බිය** සහිත.
**අන්තරඝරෙ නික්ඛිපෙය්‍ය** — ආරණ්‍ය සෙනසුන වටා **ඕනෑම දිශාවක තමා කැමති ගොචර ගමක** තැබිය යුතුය.
**කොචිදෙව පච්චයො** — යම්කිසි හේතුවක්, කළ යුත්තක්. **විප්පවාසාය** — වෙන් වී විසීමට.
**ඡාරත්තපරමං** — **රාත්‍රී සයක්** උපරිම කොට.

# සෙනසුන **සාසඞ්ක, සප්පටිභය** වීම — බිය නැති සෙනසුනක අනෙක් අංග තිබුණ ද නොලැබේ.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
Monks who had completed the rains were living in **forest lodgings**. **Kattika thieves**, thinking “They've received gains,” harassed them. When this was reported, the Blessed One allowed “**a monk living in a forest lodging to keep one of his three robes inside a village**.” — This was to protect robes, because requisites are hard to come by rightfully (a self-effacing monk cannot ask even his mother); forest dwelling, being fitting for monks, was not forbidden.
Then monks left a robe in a village and ==stayed apart from it **for more than six nights**==. The robes were **lost, destroyed, burnt, eaten by rats**, and the monks were **poorly and roughly clothed**. The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Upavassaṃ** — “upavassa,” **having entered and spent the rains**; from then until **the end of the later Kattika full moon**. (The word-analysis says “**vuṭṭhavassānaṃ**” — those who have completed the rains — to show the person entitled to leave a robe.)
**Kattikapuṇṇamaṃ** — **the Kattika four-monthly** (later Kattika) full moon.
**Āraññakāni senāsanāni** — lodgings at least **five hundred bow-lengths** away, measured **with a drawn teacher's bow**: from the **gate-post** of a walled village, or from where a wall would be for an unwalled one, **along the usual path** — one may not make another path or measure off the path. For an unwalled monastery, measure to the **first lodging, refectory, regular meeting place, Bodhi tree or shrine** nearest the village. (One who blocks paths here and there to make a nearby village qualify is to be known as a “**dhutaṅga thief**.”)
**Sāsaṅkasammatāni** — considered risky: in the monastery or its precincts **places where thieves have camped, eaten, stood, sat or lain** are seen.
**Sappaṭibhayāni** — dangerous: in the monastery or its precincts **people killed, robbed or beaten by thieves** are seen — a **strong danger close at hand**.
**Antaraghare nikkhipeyya** — he may leave it in **any alms village of his choice, in any direction** around the forest lodging.
**Koci deva paccayo** — some reason, some business. **Vippavāsāya** — for staying apart.
**Chārattaparamaṃ** — **six nights** at most.
## (B) The four conditions for leaving the robe
==A robe may be left in the village only when **all four conditions** are met:==
# He **entered the first rains** and **completed the Great Pavāraṇā** — not one who entered the later rains or broke his rains.
# It is **the month of Kattika** — not after it.
# The lodging **meets the minimum of five hundred bow-lengths** — not less, nor more than **a gāvuta**. ==A place from which one can go for alms and return to the monastery **by mealtime**== is what is meant. (Going half a yojana or a yojana on invitation and staying there does not count.)
# The lodging is **risky and dangerous** — in a safe lodging it is not allowed even if the other conditions are met.
## (C) Exceeding six nights
**Tato ce uttari vippavaseyya** — if after six nights **the seventh dawn rises** while he is in that lodging, it becomes nissaggiya.
If he cannot get back to the lodging to meet the seventh dawn (e.g. the lodging is east of the alms village and he has gone west), ==he may **enter the village boundary**, stay in a hall or anywhere, **find out how the robe is**, and leave==. If he cannot, he must **relinquish** it right where he is; it then stands as an extra robe (with the ten-day exemption).
**Aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā** — except with the **authorisation to be apart from the robe** that the Saṅgha grants a sick monk — the **Kosambī authorisation** of **the staying-away rule (nissaggiya 2)**. With it, one may be apart even beyond six nights.
> Idaṃ me, bhante, cīvaraṃ atirekachārattaṃ vippavuṭṭhaṃ aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā nissaggiyaṃ, imāhaṃ saṅghassa nissajjāmi.
## (D) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning a number of monks, on the matter of staying apart from the robe. “Aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā” is a **supplementary rule**. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**.
## (E) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Without authorisation, apart for more than six nights — knowing, in doubt, or perceiving it as fewer (**triple pācittiya**) | NP
| Not relinquished, not given, not lost … — perceived as such — beyond six nights | NP
| Using a nissaggiya robe without forfeiting it | DK
| Fewer than six nights, perceived as more or in doubt | DK
## (F) No offence
# Apart for **six nights** or **fewer**.
# After six nights apart, **re-entering the village boundary, staying**, and leaving.
# Within six nights **relinquished**, **given away**; **lost**, **destroyed**, **burnt**, **snatched**, **taken on trust**.
# With **the monks' authorisation**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (G) Factors (= cattāri — four)
# **One of the three robes** — determined, one's own — left in a village.
# Living in a forest lodging **meeting the four conditions**.
# **No authorisation from the monks**.
# **The seventh dawn rising** after six nights.
## (H) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Kathina origin** (body–speech, body–speech–mind), **by not doing** (not relinquishing), **no release by perception, without intention, an offence by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of consciousness, three feelings**.

## (උ) සමුට්ඨානාදිය
**තිසමුට්ඨානයි** (කාය-චිත්ත, වාචා-චිත්ත, කාය-වාචා-චිත්ත), **කිරියයි, සඤ්ඤාවිමොක්ඛයි, සචිත්තකයි, ලෝකවජ්ජයි, කාය-වචීකම්මයි, අකුසලචිත්තයි, තිවේදනයි**.
#en
## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
At Sāvatthī a certain **guild** (pūga) had prepared **a meal with robes** for the Saṅgha — “We'll feed them and **present them with robes**.” **The group-of-six** went to them and said, “Friends, give us these robes.” “Venerable sirs, we won't; we have arranged **a yearly alms-giving with robes** for the Saṅgha.” “**The Saṅgha has many donors and many meals. We live here depending on you, looking to you. If you don't give to us, who will?**” — ==pressured in this way, the guild gave them the robes== and served the Saṅgha only the meal. Monks who knew of the robe-offering asked, “Friends, is there to be a robe-offering?” and were told what had happened. They criticised the group-of-six, and the Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Jānaṃ** — **knowing it himself**, or **others tell him**, or **the donor tells him**.
**Saṅghikaṃ** — belonging to the Saṅgha. Because it has been **intended** for the Saṅgha it belongs to the Saṅgha in one sense **even before reaching its hands**. (The word-analysis shows saṅghika in the strict sense: “**given and relinquished** to the Saṅgha.”)
**Lābhaṃ** — something to be received — **robes, alms-food, lodgings, medicines and requisites for the sick**; ==even **a ball of bath powder, a tooth-stick, a thread of fringe**==.
**Pariṇataṃ** — **inclined**, leaning, sloping towards the Saṅgha, through **words spoken** — “We will give, we will do” — or **a gesture**.
**Attano pariṇāmeyya** — he inclines it towards himself, saying “**Give this to me**” and the like. **Dukkaṭa in the effort** of diverting; **nissaggiya when it reaches his hands**.
## (B) Given versus intended
|! Situation | Ruling
| **already given** to the Saṅgha (saṅghika) | may not be taken; it **must go to the Saṅgha** — if he eats it with the monastery attendants, the goods are valued and judged accordingly
| **intended** for the Saṅgha | belonging to co-religionists or lay people — ==**even his mother's**== — diverting it to himself with “Give it to me” → **nissaggiya pācittiya**
| intended item diverted **to another individual** (“Give it to this monk”) | **pure pācittiya** (pācittiya 82)
| one to himself, one to another | **both** nissaggiya pācittiya and pure pācittiya
| intended for the Saṅgha, diverted to **another Saṅgha or a shrine** | DK
| intended for a shrine, diverted to **another shrine, the Saṅgha or an individual** | DK
| intended for an individual, diverted to **another individual, the Saṅgha or a shrine** | DK
> Idaṃ me, bhante, jānaṃ saṅghikaṃ lābhaṃ pariṇataṃ attano pariṇāmitaṃ nissaggiyaṃ, imāhaṃ saṅghassa nissajjāmi.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of diverting. It is a **common rule**, and **anāṇattika**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The effort of diverting | DK
| Knowing it was intended, diverting it to himself and **obtaining** it | NP
| In doubt whether it was intended, diverting it | DK
| Not intended, perceived as intended or in doubt | DK
| Diverting what was intended for one Saṅgha / shrine / individual to another | DK
## (E) No offence
# Diverting it **perceiving it as not intended**.
# When asked “**Where shall we give?**”, saying, “**Give where your gift will be used, will be kept in repair, will last long**, or **where your mind is inspired**.”
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# The gain **intended for the Saṅgha**.
# **Knowing this**, diverting it to oneself.
# **Obtaining** it.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Three origins** (body–mind, speech–mind, body–speech–mind), **by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily and verbal action, unwholesome mind, three feelings**.
