## (අ) සේඛිය ශික්ෂාපදවල ස්වභාවය — කඞ්ඛාවිතරණී අනුව

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six** ==wore their lower robes **hanging down in front and behind**==. People complained, “**Just like householders enjoying sensual pleasures!**” The Blessed One laid down the rule: “**I will wear the lower robe evenly all round: a training to be done.**”
## (A) The nature of the sekhiya rules — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
**Sikkhā karaṇīyā** — the training “**I will wear it thus**” must be done **everywhere — in the monastery and among the houses**.
# Since **the duties given in the Vatta-khandhaka** must also be trained in, **they too are sekhiya**. Hence, unlike the pārājikas etc., **no fixed count is given**.
# To show **the Vinaya of conduct**, the Pāli of every rule is framed as “**a training to be done**,” not by naming an offence (“**whoever wears it hanging down: dukkaṭa**”).
# But since **the word analysis says “an offence of dukkaṭa,”** it should be understood that ==**doing it out of disrespect** is **dukkaṭa** in every case==.
## (B) Explanation of terms
**Parimaṇḍalaṃ** — **evenly round all over**.
==A lower robe worn **covering the navel**, and let down **below the kneecaps** to **about eight fingerbreadths** below the top of the shin== is “worn evenly.” Per the Mahāpaccarī, **so that four fingerbreadths below the knee are covered when sitting**.
|! Point | Commentarial ruling
| **size of the lower robe** | **five fist-cubits** long, **two and a half cubits** wide; if not available, **two cubits** wide is allowable (to cover the knees)
| the navel | may also be covered **by the upper robe**
| a **single-layer** lower robe | even worn thus, **it does not stay in place**; a **double-layer** one does
| one with **thin shins** or **heavy calves** | may wear it **more than eight fingerbreadths lower** for a seemly look
## (C) Ways of dressing forbidden in the Khandhaka
Not only hanging it front and back, but also these ways forbidden in **the Cūḷavagga** as “**householders' dress**” are **dukkaṭa**:
|! Style | Description
| **hatthisoṇḍaka** | hanging from the navel **like an elephant's trunk** (as Cola women dress)
| **macchavālaka** | the **fringed end** hanging on one side and the **other end** on the other (**like a fish's tail**)
| **catukkaṇṇaka** | showing **four corners**, two above and two below
| **tālavaṇṭaka** | the cloth hanging **like a bunch of palm leaves**
| **satavalika** | a long cloth **folded many times into pleats**, or showing **continuous pleats** down the left and right sides (one or two pleats showing from the knee is allowable)
| **saṃvalliya** | **tucked between the legs** like **wrestlers and labourers**
==These are **not allowable even for one ill or travelling**==. On the road, **lifting the corners and hanging them over the lower robe**, or wearing one such cloth inside and another outside, is also **not allowable**. **One who is ill** may show the waist-roll of the inner cloth and wear another on top; one **not ill** wearing two must wear them **together as one** (doubled).
## (D) Laying down and command — common to all sekhiyas
|! Point | Ruling
| where laid down | **slurping** at **Kosambī**; **the drinking-water pot with a food-soiled hand** and **bowl-rinsing water**, in **the Bhagga country**; **all the rest at Sāvatthī**, concerning the group-of-six
| amendments | one amendment **on account of illness** in **asking for curry and rice** and in **teaching Dhamma** etc.
| sharing | all are **common rules**
| command | all are **anāṇattika**
| offence | **dukkaṭa in every case**; no other division of offences
## (E) Division of offences
|! Case | Offence
| wearing it hanging down in front or behind **out of disrespect** | DK
| wearing it in **any distorted way** forbidden in the Khandhaka | DK
| whether in the monastery or among the houses | DK
## (F) No offence — and what it means
|! Exemption | Meaning — per the commentaries
| **asañcicca** (unintentionally) | not meaning to wear it hanging, but **trying to wear it evenly and getting it wrong**
| **assatiyā** (unmindfully) | wearing it so **with the mind on something else**
| **ajānanta** (not knowing) | one **who has learnt the dressing duty** but does not notice it **has ridden up or slipped down**. ==**For one who has not learnt the duty there is no release**== — not learning it deliberately is itself **disrespect** (Samantapāsādikā). The Kurundī says “no offence for one who does not know how to wear it evenly,” but **the dressing duty must be learnt**
| **gilāna** (ill) | with **a sore on the shin or foot**, wearing it higher or lower
| **āpadā** (emergencies) | when **wild beasts or thieves** are in pursuit
| **the insane etc.** | the insane, deranged, those in pain, and the first offender
## (G) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Disrespect**.
# **No reason for exemption**.
# **Wearing it unevenly**.
> Per the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī, **every sekhiya** has **three** factors — the first two are common; the third is **doing the opposite of what that rule says**.
## (H) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**First-pārājika origin** (body–mind), **by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily action, unwholesome mind, painful feeling**.
|! Elder's view | Blame / mind / feeling
| Elder **Phussadeva** | **without intention, blameworthy by convention, three feelings**
| Elder **Upatissa** | because it says “**out of disrespect**”: **blameworthy by nature, unwholesome mind, painful feeling**

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six** ==wore their robes **hanging down in front and behind**==. People complained. The Blessed One laid down the rule: “**I will wear the robe evenly all round: a training to be done.**”
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Pārupissāmi** — ==this rule on wearing applies to **both robes that are worn over the body**: **the upper robe (uttarāsaṅga)** and **the double robe (saṅghāṭi)**==. (The lower robe — antaravāsaka — belongs to the first sekhiya.)
**Parimaṇḍalaṃ pārupitabbaṃ** — it must be worn ==**with both corners even**==.
Not wearing it in the **householders' styles** forbidden in the Khandhaka (“**Monks, one should not wear it as householders do**”), one should wear it evenly, **without distortion**.
==Since these two rules (lower and upper robe) are stated **without qualification**, one must wear them evenly **both in the monastery and among the houses**== (Samantapāsādikā).
## (B) “Householders' styles” — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Style | How it is worn
| **setapaṭa** | as **the Aḍḍhapālaka Jains** wear it
| **paribbājaka** | like some wanderers, **leaving the chest bare** and placing the wrap **on both shoulders**
| **ekasāṭaka** | like those with one cloth, **covering the back with one end of the lower cloth**, both corners on the shoulders
| **surāsoṇḍa** | like drunkards, cloth **around the neck**, both ends **hanging on the chest** or **thrown behind**
| **antepurika** | like harem women, **showing only the eyes**, head covered
| **mahājeṭṭha** | like great elders, wearing a long cloth and covering **the whole body with one end of it**
| **kuṭippavesaka** | like **farmers entering a field hut**, wrapping the cloth, **tucking it under the armpit**, covering the body with one end
| **brāhmaṇa** | like brahmins, passing the cloth **between both armpits** and onto the shoulders
| **pāḷikāraka** | worn **over one shoulder**, **baring the left arm**, the robe lifted onto the shoulder
All these and **similar faults** are to be avoided, wearing it evenly.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, **dukkaṭa only**.
## (D) Division of offences
|! Case | Offence
| wearing it hanging down in front or behind **out of disrespect** | DK
| in **any distorted way**, such as householders' styles — **in the monastery or among the houses** | DK
## (E) No offence
# **Unintentionally** (trying to wear it evenly and getting it wrong); **unmindfully**; **not knowing**.
# When **ill** (sores on the body etc.).
# In **emergencies**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Disrespect**.
# **No reason for exemption**.
# **Wearing it unevenly**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**First-pārājika origin** (body–mind), **by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily action, unwholesome mind, painful feeling**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six** ==sat among the houses **looking here and there**==. People complained, and the Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Okkhittacakkhu nisīdissāmi** — ==**when sitting too**, one should sit with eyes lowered, looking at the ground **a yoke's length (about four cubits)** ahead== (Samantapāsādikā).
**Tahaṃ tahaṃ olokento** — sitting and looking at **this direction and that, at houses, at people**.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, **dukkaṭa only**.

# **Disrespect**.
# **No reason for exemption**.
