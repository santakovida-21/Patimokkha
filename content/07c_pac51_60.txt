## The Story
@ Kosambī · Ghositārāma
At the hermitage of the matted-hair ascetics at **Ambatittha** in Ceti, Ven. **Sāgata** subdued **a fierce nāga** by psychic power. When the people of **Kosambī**, delighted, asked, “**What shall we give the venerable?**”, **the group-of-six** said, “**Pigeon-coloured liquor** is hard for monks to get and agreeable; prepare that.” As Sāgata went for alms, he was given liquor **at house after house**, and ==he fell down drunk **at the city gate**==.
The Blessed One had him brought to the monastery. Laid down **with his head towards the Blessed One**, he turned over in his drunkenness so that **his feet** pointed that way. The Blessed One asked, “**Wasn't Sāgata formerly respectful towards the Tathāgata? Could he now subdue even a water-snake?**” and, rebuking: “**Should one drink what makes one lose one's senses?**”, laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Surā** — liquor made from **flour, cakes, cooked rice** etc. (with yeast).
**Meraya** — a **fermented drink** made from **flowers, fruit, honey, molasses** etc.
**Pāne** — drinking it ==**from the start of fermentation**, **even with the tip of a blade of grass**== — a pācittiya **for each act of drinking**.
## (B) Alcohol and what is no offence
|! Thing | Ruling
| **surā, meraya** — even a little | PC — each act
| a non-alcoholic drink perceived as alcoholic, or in doubt | DK
| a drink with the **colour, smell or taste** of alcohol that **does not intoxicate**; **salted sour gruel**, **sutta** | no offence
| broth, meat etc. **cooked with a little alcohol for flavour** (its alcoholic quality gone) | no offence
| **non-intoxicating ariṭṭha** made with **emblic juice** etc. | no offence
**Nature of the offence** — since alcohol is **blameworthy by nature**, there is an offence even if drunk **unknowingly** (without intention), but the mind involved is unwholesome.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Kosambī** concerning Ven. **Sāgata**, on the matter of drinking alcohol. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya** (perceiving it as alcohol, in doubt, or as not).
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Drinking surā / meraya — perceiving it as alcohol, in doubt, or as not (**triple pācittiya**) — each act | PC
| A non-alcoholic drink perceived as alcoholic, or in doubt | DK
## (E) No offence
# Perceiving a non-alcoholic drink as such.
# **Non-intoxicating** drinks with the colour, smell or taste of alcohol; **salted sour gruel**, **sutta**.
# Broth, meat etc. **cooked with a little alcohol for flavour**.
# **Non-alcoholic ariṭṭha** made with **emblic juice** etc.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# **An alcoholic drink**.
# **Drinking** it.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Eḷakaloma origin** (body; body–mind), **by doing, no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily action, unwholesome mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six** ==**tickled** a monk of **the group-of-seventeen** with their fingers and made him laugh==. He **could not catch his breath** and **died**. The monks criticised them, and the Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Aṅgulipatodake** — **poking the armpit** and the like **with the fingers**. Moreover, touching an ordained person ==**with any part of the body**, **intending fun**== is pācittiya.
## (B) Whom, and with what
|! Way of touching / whom | Offence
| **body to body** — an ordained person | PC
| **an unordained person** — in all three perceptions | DK
| here **a nun is unordained for a monk**, and **a monk for a nun** | DK
| with **something attached to the body** (robe, stick etc.) to the body, body to something attached, etc. | DK
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of tickling to make someone laugh. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Intending fun, touching an ordained person's body with the body — perceiving him as ordained, in doubt, or as unordained (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| To an unordained person (triple dukkaṭa); all cases involving things attached to the body | DK
## (E) No offence
# **Without intending fun**.
# Touching **when there is something to be done** (waking someone etc.).
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# **Intending fun**.
# **Touching an ordained person's body with the body**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**First-pārājika origin** (body–mind), **by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily action, unwholesome mind, two feelings** (pleasant / neutral).

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-seventeen** ==were playing in the water of **the Aciravatī river**==. **King Pasenadi of Kosala**, on an upper terrace with **Queen Mallikā**, saw them and said, “**Mallikā, there are your arahants.**” The queen replied, “**Great king, it must be that the Blessed One has not laid down a rule, or they don't know it,**” and suggested a plan. The king sent **a pot of molasses** to the Blessed One by the hands of those very monks, “**to be shared among the Saṅgha**.” Asked, “**Where did the king see you?**”, they told him what had happened, and the Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Udake hasadhamme** — **playing in the water**.
A monk who, **in water above the ankles**, ==**intending play**==, **dives, surfaces or swims** —
|! Act | Offence
| **each movement of hand or foot** in going in to dive etc. | DK
| **diving, surfacing** — **each effort** | PC
| having dived, **each movement of hand or foot** moving **under water** | PC
| **swimming** — each effort with whatever limb he swims | PC
## (B) Play not in deep water
|! Play | Offence
| play in water **below the ankles** | DK
| playing **in a boat** | DK
| striking the water **with hand, foot, stick or potsherd** | DK
| playing at **throwing** water from a vessel, **sour gruel, mud** etc. | DK
| **cutting a letter that conveys meaning** (in water or ground) | no offence — allowable
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-seventeen, on the matter of playing in the water. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| In water above the ankles, intending play, diving / surfacing / swimming — perceiving it as play, in doubt, or as not (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| Not play, perceived as play or in doubt | DK
| Playing in a boat; striking the water; throwing water / sour gruel / mud in play | DK
## (E) No offence
# **Without intending play**.
# Going in and diving, surfacing or swimming **for some purpose**.
# **Crossing over**.
# **In emergencies**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# Water **above the ankles**.
# **Playing with intent to play**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**First-pārājika origin, by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily action, unwholesome mind, two feelings**.

## The Story
@ Kosambī · Ghositārāma
When Ven. **Channa** behaved improperly, the monks told him, “**Friend, don't do that; it is not allowable.**” He ==kept on doing it **out of disrespect**==. The monks criticised him, and the Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Anādariye** — disrespect towards **a person** or towards **the Dhamma**.
|! Disrespect | Meaning
| **towards the person** | when an ordained person admonishes him **with a rule that has been laid down**, being disrespectful **not wishing to do what he says**
| **towards the Dhamma** | **not wishing to train in that Dhamma (rule)** — not learning it, thinking, “How might this be lost or disappear?”
The pācittiya falls on that disrespect.
## (B) Admonished with what has and has not been laid down
|! Who admonishes / with what | Offence
| **an ordained person, with a rule laid down** | PC
| **an unordained person**, with what was laid down — in all three perceptions | DK
| ordained or unordained, with **what was not laid down** — “**This does not lead to effacement, to austerity, it leads to loss of confidence**” etc. | DK
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Kosambī** concerning Ven. **Channa**, on the matter of disrespect. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Disrespect when an ordained person admonishes with a rule laid down — perceiving him as ordained, in doubt, or as unordained (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| Disrespect to an unordained person's admonition (triple dukkaṭa); to an admonition about what was not laid down | DK
## (E) No offence
# Saying, “**This is how our teachers learnt and explained it**,” following **a learning handed down by teachers**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# Words of **an ordained person, based on a rule laid down**.
# **Showing disrespect**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Three origins, by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily and verbal action, unwholesome mind, painful feeling**.

**ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු** ==**සත්තරසවග්ගිය** භික්ෂූන් **බිය ගැන්වූහ**==. බියට පත් ඔවුහු **හඬා වැලපුණහ**. "**කුමක් නිසා හඬන්නහු ද?**" යි ඇසූ භික්ෂූන්ට ඔවුහු සිදු වූ දේ කීහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූහ.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six** ==**frightened** monks of **the group-of-seventeen**==, who **wept** in fear. Asked by the monks, “**Why are you weeping?**”, they told what had happened. The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Bhiṃsāpeyya** — to frighten him, he **presents sights and the like** (frightening sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches), or tells **a frightening story** (“There are yakkhas, wild beasts here”).
==**Whether he is frightened or not**==, the other incurs **a pācittiya**.
## (B) Ways of frightening
|! Means | Examples
| **by sight** | creating or showing forms of **yakkhas, ghosts, snakes**, or taking on a frightening guise near his bed at night
| **by sound** | making **a frightening noise**
| **by smell, taste, touch** | frightening by these
| **by talk** | telling frightening tales: “**There are robbers, fierce yakkhas, goblins** on the road”
It must be done **within his range of sight or hearing**.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of frightening. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Frightening an ordained person — perceiving him as ordained, in doubt, or as unordained (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| Frightening an unordained person — in all three perceptions (**triple dukkaṭa**) | DK
## (E) No offence
# **Without wishing to frighten** — showing sights etc., or speaking of robbers, yakkhas and the like **as a warning**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# **An ordained person**.
# **Making an effort, wishing to frighten him, within his range of sight or hearing**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Three origins, by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily and verbal action, unwholesome mind, painful feeling**.

## කථා වස්තුව
@ භග්ග ජනපදය · සුංසුමාරගිරය, භේසකළාවනය
**ශීත කාලයේ** භික්ෂූහු ==**සිදුරු සහිත විශාල දර කොටයක් ගින්නට දමා** උණුසුම ලබමින් සිටියහ==. ඒ කොටයේ සිටි **කළු සර්පයෙක්** ගින්නෙන් තැවී පිටතට පැන භික්ෂූන් පසුපස ලුහුබැන්දේය; භික්ෂූහු **එහෙ මෙහෙ දිව්වෝ** ය. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූහ.
පසුව **ගිලන් භික්ෂූන්** ද, **පහන් දැල්වීම, ජන්තාඝරය** ආදිය ගැන භික්ෂූන් ද කුකුස් ඇති කරගත් හෙයින් "**අගිලානො**" හා "**අඤ්ඤත්‍ර තථාරූපප්පච්චයා**" යන **අනුප්‍රඥප්ති දෙක** පැනවූහ.

## The Story
@ Bhagga country · Suṃsumāragira, Bhesakaḷā Grove
In **the cold season** monks were ==warming themselves, having **put a large hollow log on a fire**==. A **black snake** in the log, scorched by the fire, came out and chased the monks, who **ran here and there**. The Blessed One laid down the rule.
Later, as **sick monks**, and monks concerned about **lighting lamps, the sauna** etc., became scrupulous, he added **two supplementary rules**: “**agilāno**” and “**aññatra tathārūpappaccayā**.”
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Visibbanāpekkho** — **wishing to warm himself**.
**Samādaheyya** — **kindles**.
**Aññatra tathārūpappaccayā** — **without a reason** such as **lighting a lamp** or making a fire for **firing a bowl**.
## (B) How the offences fall
|! Stage | Offence
| kindling it himself — every effort **from setting the fire-sticks until the flame rises** | DK
| **the flame rising** | PC
| having another kindle it — **giving the order** | DK
| the one ordered kindling it — ordered **once**, even if he kindles much | **one** PC
| lifting a burning brand and **putting it back in place** (still alight) | DK
| rekindling **a fire that has gone out** | PC
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down **among the Bhaggas** concerning a number of monks, on the matter of lighting a fire to warm themselves. “**Agilāno, aññatra tathārūpappaccayā**” are two supplementary rules. It is a **common rule**, **sāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Preliminary efforts until the flame rises; giving the order | DK
| One not ill kindling a fire to warm himself — perceiving himself as not ill, in doubt, or as ill (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| One ill perceiving himself as not ill, or in doubt; lifting and replacing a burning brand | DK
## (E) No offence
# For **the sick** — perceiving himself as sick.
# Warming himself at a fire **lit by another**, or at **embers without smoke or flame**.
# For **such a reason** — **a lamp, a fire (for light), a sauna** etc.
# **In emergencies** (to drive off wild animals or snakes).
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= cattāri — four)
# **Not ill**.
# **No allowed reason**.
# **Wishing to warm himself**.
# **Kindling a fire**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Go-between origin** (six origins), **by doing, no release by perception, without intention, an offence by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of consciousness, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Rājagaha · Veḷuvana, the squirrels' feeding place
Monks ==bathed again and again, without moderation,== at the **Tapodā** hot springs. King **Bimbisāra** came to bathe and waited “**until the venerables have bathed**.” The monks bathed **till late at night**. When the king had bathed and was returning, **the city gates were shut**, and ==he **spent the night outside the city**==. Next day he told the Blessed One, who laid down: “**Whoever bathes at intervals of less than half a month — pācittiya.**”
Later, as monks became scrupulous **in the hot season, when ill, after work, when travelling, and in wind and rain**, he allowed **six occasions**.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Orenaddhamāsaṃ nahāyeyya** — a monk **in the Middle Country** who, **before half a month has passed since he last bathed**, without an occasion, prepares **bath powder or clay** intending to bathe —
|! Stage | Offence
| **every effort** from then on | DK
| **the end of the bath** | PC
## (B) The six occasions
|! Occasion | Meaning
| **Hot season** | **the last month and a half of summer**
| **Fever season** | **the first month of the rains** (with the hot season, **two and a half months**)
| **Illness** | when he is not at ease without bathing
| **Work** | after doing work — **even just sweeping the cell**
| **Travelling** | wishing to go, going or having gone **half a yojana**
| **Wind and rain** | covered by **a dusty wind**, or **two or three drops of rain** have fallen on the body
**In the border countries** (outside the Middle Country, Sri Lanka included) — **no offence for anyone**.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Rājagaha** concerning a number of monks, on the matter of bathing without moderation. “**Aññatra samayā**” — a **sixfold supplementary rule**. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Every effort from preparing powder / clay to bathe | DK
| Finishing a bath within half a month, without an occasion — perceiving it as less, in doubt, or as more (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| More than half a month, perceived as less or in doubt | DK
## (E) No offence
# Perceiving **half a month as passed**.
# **On the six occasions**.
# Bathing **in pits dug in the sand while crossing a river**.
# For everyone **in the border countries**.
# **In emergencies** (diving into water when chased by bees etc.).
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **The Middle Country**.
# **Bathing within half a month**.
# **No occasion, river crossing or emergency**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Eḷakaloma origin, by doing, no release by perception, without intention, an offence by convention, bodily action, three kinds of consciousness, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
A number of monks and wanderers travelling **from Sāketa to Sāvatthī** were robbed on the road by **thieves**. **The king's men** caught the thieves, brought back the goods and said, “**Venerables, recognise your robes and take them.**” ==The monks **could not recognise** their own robes==. People complained, “**How can they not even recognise their own robes?**” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Navaṃcīvaralābhena** — one who has **received a new robe**. (Said without dropping the nasal, for “navacīvaralābhena.”)
**Tiṇṇaṃ dubbaṇṇakaraṇānaṃ aññataraṃ dubbaṇṇakaraṇaṃ ādātabbaṃ** — **one of three** discolouring marks must be applied:
|! Mark | Meaning
| **nīla** | **copper blue-green** or **leaf green**
| **kaddama** | **mud** (from water)
| **kāḷasāma** | any **black** colour
## (B) Applying the kappabindu
Having received a robe **fit for wearing as lower or upper robe** and **finished dyeing** it, **anywhere** on it —
|! Size | Note
| **a peacock's eye** | largest
| **a bedbug's back** | smallest
— a **kappabindu** of any size between these should be applied before use. ==**Using it without one is pācittiya**==.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning a number of monks, on the matter of not recognising their own robes. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Wearing a new robe without the mark — perceiving it as unmarked, in doubt, or as marked (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| Marked, perceived as unmarked or in doubt | DK
## (E) No offence
# Perceiving it **as marked**.
# **The mark is lost**.
# **The marked part has worn away**.
# **Unmarked** cloth sewn to **marked** cloth.
# **Patches, borders or edging added later**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# A robe of the kind described **not marked**.
# **Not a robe whose mark was lost** etc.
# **Wearing it** as lower or upper robe.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Eḷakaloma origin, by doing and not doing** (using; not marking), **no release by perception, without intention, an offence by convention, bodily action, three kinds of consciousness, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
Ven. **Upananda** **assigned a robe in person** to a co-resident pupil of his brother, and ==afterwards used it **without its being relinquished back**==. The monk told the other monks, “**Ven. Upananda is using the robe he assigned to me without asking me.**” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Vikappetvā** — there are two ways of assigning: **in presence** and **in absence**. Knowing whether there are **one or several** robes, **near or far**, one says accordingly “**imaṃ / imāni / etaṃ / etāni cīvara(āni)**.”
|! Assignment | What is said
| **in presence (1)** | “…**tuyhaṃ vikappemi**”
| **in presence (2)** | before that monk, naming any of the five co-religionists he likes — “…**Tissassa bhikkhuno / Tissāya bhikkhuniyā / sikkhamānāya / sāmaṇerassa / sāmaṇeriyā vikappemi**”
| **in absence** | “…**tuyhaṃ vikappanatthāya dammi**” — “**Ko te mitto vā sandiṭṭho vā?**” — “**Tisso bhikkhū…**” — “**Ahaṃ Tissassa bhikkhuno dammi**”
==After assigning it may **only be stored**; **using, giving away or determining** it is not allowed.==
## (B) Relinquishing back
|! Assignment | The other monk relinquishes it back by saying
| in presence (1) | “**Mayhaṃ santakaṃ / santakāni paribhuñja vā vissajjehi vā yathāpaccayaṃ vā karohi**”
| in presence (2), in absence | “**Tissassa bhikkhuno santakaṃ … paribhuñja vā vissajjehi vā yathāpaccayaṃ vā karohi**”
From then on, use and the rest are allowed. **The same method applies to assigning a bowl.**
A robe assigned to one of the five co-religionists by either method, if used —
- **not relinquished back**, or
- **not taken on trust** from the one who did the Vinaya act
— is pācittiya.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning **Upananda**, on the matter of using a robe not relinquished back. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Using an assigned robe not relinquished back — perceiving it as not relinquished, in doubt, or as relinquished (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| **Determining** it or **giving it away** | DK
| Relinquished, perceived as not relinquished or in doubt | DK
## (E) No offence
# Using it **on trust**, perceiving it as relinquished.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# A robe **he himself assigned**, **not relinquished back**.
# A robe **of assignable size**.
# **Using** it.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Kathina origin, by doing and not doing** (using; not having it relinquished), **no release by perception, without intention, an offence by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of consciousness, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-seventeen** left their requisites **carelessly**. **The group-of-six** ==**hid their bowls and robes**==. When the group-of-seventeen said, “**Friends, give us our bowls and robes,**” the group-of-six laughed, and they **wept**. The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Pattaṃ** — a bowl **fit for determination**.
**Cīvaraṃ** — a robe **of assignable size**.
**Nisīdanaṃ** — a sitting cloth **with a border**.
**Sūcigharaṃ** — a needle-case, **with or without needles**.
**Kāyabandhanaṃ** — a belt, **flat (paṭṭikā)** or **“pig's-intestine” (sūkarantaka)**.
**Apanidheyya** — **removes and hides**. **Hasāpekkho** — **for fun**.
## (B) How the offences fall
|! Act | Offence
| hiding it **himself** | PC
| **ordering** another | DK
| when the one ordered hides it — **for the one who ordered** | PC
| a requisite of an ordained person **other than these five** | DK
| a bowl etc. or other requisite **of an unordained person** | DK
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of hiding. It is a **common rule**, **sāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Hiding an ordained person's bowl, robe, sitting cloth, needle-case or belt for fun or to annoy — perceiving him as ordained, in doubt, or as unordained (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| Giving the order | DK
| An unordained person's things (triple dukkaṭa); requisites other than these five | DK
## (E) No offence
# **Putting away for safety** something **carelessly left** (that could be lost).
# Putting it away thinking, “**I'll give it back after a Dhamma talk (teaching him)**.”
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# **Hiding** a bowl etc. **belonging to an ordained person**.
# **Wishing to annoy** or **for fun**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Three origins, by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily and verbal action, unwholesome mind, three feelings**.
