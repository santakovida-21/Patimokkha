## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**King Pasenadi of Kosala** asked the Blessed One, “**Please appoint a monk to teach Dhamma to our women's quarters.**” The Blessed One gave the task to Ven. **Ānanda**, who went from time to time to teach.
One day, as Ānanda was arriving at the palace, the king was **in bed** with **Queen Mallikā**. ==Seeing him from afar, the queen got up suddenly and **her golden-yellow cloth slipped off**==. Ānanda turned back right there and told the monks at the monastery. The Blessed One spoke of **the ten dangers of entering a king's quarters** and laid down the rule.
## (A) The ten dangers of entering a king's quarters — according to the Pāli
|! # | Danger
| 1 | the king sits with the queen and a monk enters; they smile at each other — the king suspects “**something has happened or will happen between them**”
| 2 | a woman the king has forgotten becomes pregnant — suspicion: “**no one comes here but the monk**”
| 3 | **a jewel goes missing** in the quarters — suspicion falls on the monk
| 4 | **secret counsel leaks out** — suspicion falls on the monk
| 5 | a son **wishes his father dead**, or a father his son — suspicion falls on the monk
| 6 | the king **promotes someone of low position** — those displeased suspect the monk
| 7 | the king **demotes someone of high position** — those displeased suspect the monk
| 8 | the king **sends out the army at the wrong time** — those displeased suspect the monk
| 9 | having sent it out at the right time, he **recalls it midway** — those displeased suspect the monk
| 10 | the quarters are **crowded with elephants, horses and chariots**, and have **enticing sights, sounds, smells, tastes and touches** unsuitable for one gone forth
## (B) Explanation of terms
**Khattiyassa** — a noble **well born on both sides**, of pure descent, unchallenged as to birth **back to the seventh generation**.
**Muddhāvasittassa** — **consecrated on the head with the noble's consecration**.
**Anikkhantarājake** — the king **has not left** the bedchamber. **Aniggataratanake** — “**the jewel” is the chief queen**; she has not left, or **both** have not left.
**Pubbe appaṭisaṃvidito** — **without being announced beforehand**.
**Indakhīlo** — **the threshold** of the bedchamber.
**Sayanigharaṃ** — **any place where the king's bed is prepared**, **even one enclosed by a screen wall**.
## (C) How the offences fall
|! Stage | Offence
| unannounced, putting **the first foot** past the threshold | DK
| putting **the second foot** | PC
## (D) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning Ven. Ānanda, on the matter of **entering the king's quarters**. It is a **rule not shared** (for monks only), **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (E) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Not announced — perceiving it as unannounced, in doubt, or as announced (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| Announced, but **perceiving it as unannounced** or **in doubt** | DK
## (F) No offence
# When **announced** (and perceived as such).
# When the king is **not a noble**, or **not consecrated** with the noble's consecration.
# When **the king**, **the queen**, or **both** have **left** the bedchamber.
# Entering **what is not a bedchamber**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (G) Factors (= pañca — five)
# **A noble**.
# **Consecrated**.
# **Neither has left** the bedchamber.
# **Not announced beforehand**.
# **Crossing the threshold**.
## (H) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Kaṭhina origin** (body–speech; body–speech–mind), **by doing and not doing** (entering; not announcing), **no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

> **පුණ්ණපත්තං** — **සියයකට කහවණු පහක්** වූ ත්‍යාගය (අට්ඨකථාව).

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
A monk was bathing in **the Aciravatī river**, and a brahmin who had put down **a purse of five hundred coins** on the bank while bathing **forgot it** and went off. The monk picked it up, thinking, “**Don't let it be lost here.**” When the brahmin remembered and came running, the monk gave it back. The brahmin, ==thinking “**How can I avoid giving this monk a finder's reward?**”, said “**It wasn't five hundred, it was a thousand**,” detained the monk and then let him go==. The Blessed One laid down the rule.
> **Puṇṇapatta** — a reward of **five coins in every hundred** (commentary).
**First amendment — within a monastery:** at a festival in Sāvatthī, **Visākhā, Migāra's mother**, on her way to the park, took off her **mahālatā ornament** (worth ninety million) to visit the Blessed One, tied it in her upper robe and gave it to a slave girl, who **forgot it** when leaving. The Blessed One said, “**Pick it up and put it aside**,” allowing this **within a monastery**.
**Second amendment — within a dwelling:** at **Anāthapiṇḍika's work village** in Kāsi, a servant fed some monks and **forgot his signet ring**. The monks stayed there, thinking, “**If we go, it will be lost.**” The Blessed One allowed it **within a dwelling** as well.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Ratanaṃ** — the ten valuables: **pearl, gem, beryl, conch, crystal, coral, silver, gold, ruby and cat's-eye**.
**Ratanasammataṃ** — anything **people use or enjoy**.
**Ajjhārāme** — **inside the fence** of a fenced monastery; within **two stone-throws** of an unfenced one.
**Ajjhāvasathe** — **inside the fence** of a fenced dwelling; within **a pestle's throw** of an unfenced one (a winnowing-fan's or pestle's throw per the Mahāpaccarī).
**Uggaṇheyya** — **picks it up himself**. **Uggaṇhāpeyya** — **has another pick it up** (sāṇattika).
## (B) The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī's ruling
|! Case | Offence
| **Gold or silver** picked up or had picked up for oneself | NP (nissaggiya 18)
| Gold or silver for **the Saṅgha, a group, an individual, a shrine, building work** | DK
| **Pearls and other valuables** — for anyone at all | DK
| **Anything, allowable or not** — **even one's mother's** — kept **as a storekeeper** (lay property) | PC
| Keeping it after making it one's own | allowable
==When a lay person says “**Keep this**,” it should be refused: “**It is not allowable**.”== If he gets angry and drops it and goes, **it is an impediment** — it should be kept. If carpenters working at the monastery or **royal favourites** say, “**Store these tools / bedding for us**,” it must **not be done, whether out of goodwill or fear**; **showing a safe place** is all that is allowable.
## (C) When a lost item is found — within a monastery or dwelling
# In a place where suspicion might arise that “**monks or novices took it**,” he should **pick it up or have it picked up**, **note its form or marks**, and put it aside.
# He should announce: “**Whoever has lost something, let him come**.”
# Asking the one who comes, “**What is your item like?**”, he should give it **if the marks match**; if not, he should say, “**Keep looking**.”
# On leaving the residence he should leave it in the hands of **suitable monks**, or if there are none, **suitable householders**.
# If the owner never comes, he should have **a permanent lodging, a shrine or a pond** made with it. If the owner comes after a long time, he should show it and say, “**Rejoice in it**.” If he does not, and demands, “**Give me my money**,” it should be **collected and given back**.
## (D) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning a certain monk, on the matter of **picking up a valuable**. There are **two amendments**: “**except within a monastery or dwelling**.” It is a **common rule**, and **sāṇattika**.
## (E) Division of offences
|! Case | Offence
| Outside the permitted places, picking up or having picked up another's valuable or valued item (Pāli analysis) | PC
| Picking it up **in a permitted place** and **disrespectfully** not putting it aside properly | DK
## (F) No offence
# Picking it up **in a permitted place** and putting it aside: “**Whoever it belongs to will take it**.”
# Taking **a touchable valued item on trust** or **temporarily**.
# Taking it **perceiving it as discarded**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (G) Factors (= cattāri — four)
# Doing it **other than as allowed**.
# **Belonging to another**.
# **No perception of trust or of its being discarded**.
# **Picking it up or having it picked up**.
## (H) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Six origins, by doing, no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six** ==went into the village **at the wrong time**, sat in **the meeting hall** and engaged in all kinds of **animal talk** — talk of **kings, thieves, wars, women** and so on==. People complained, “**Just like householders enjoying sensual pleasures!**” The Blessed One laid down, “**Entering a village at the wrong time: pācittiya.**”
**The three amendments:**
|! # | Incident | Added wording
| 1 | monks travelling to Sāvatthī in Kosala reached a village in the evening, did not enter out of scruple, and **were robbed by thieves** | “**without taking leave**”
| 2 | **a monk alone**, with no one to ask, likewise did not enter and **was robbed** | “**of an available monk**”
| 3 | when a monk **was bitten by a snake**, a monk going **to fetch fire** did not enter out of scruple | “**except for an urgent matter of that kind**”
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Santo bhikkhu** — a monk available **so that one can enter after taking leave**. **Asanto** — one not available to ask.
**Vikālo** — **from just after midday until dawn**.
**Gāmaṃ paviseyya** — **crossing the fence** of a fenced village, or **entering the precincts** of an unfenced one —
|! Stage | Offence
| **first foot** | DK
| **second foot** | PC
One should take leave saying, “**I take leave to enter the village at the wrong time**,” or “**I will enter the village**.”
## (B) Rulings on taking leave — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Situation | Ruling
| **several monks** enter together on some business | **all must take leave of one another**
| the business fails in that village, so they go **to another village for the same business** | **no need** to ask again
| giving up the effort and heading back, they wish to enter **another village on the way** | **must** ask
| after a meal at a family house or seating hall, wishing to go **for alms of oil or ghee** | ask a monk nearby if there is one; if not, he may go, noting “**there is none**”
| **having gone down into the street**, he then sees a monk | **no need** to ask
| on **a road through a village**, the thought arises, “**I'll go for oil etc.**” | ask a monk seen nearby; **not leaving the road**, no need; leaving it, pācittiya
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of **entering a village at the wrong time**. There are **three amendments**: “**an available monk**,” “**without taking leave**,” “**except for an urgent matter of that kind**.” It is a **rule not shared**, **anāṇattika**, and a **triple pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| At the wrong time — perceiving it as the wrong time, in doubt, or as the right time — entering without taking leave (**triple pācittiya**) | PC
| At **the right time**, **perceiving it as the wrong time** or **in doubt** | DK
## (E) No offence
# For one **perceiving the right time** (at the right time).
# For **an urgent matter**.
# Entering **having taken leave of an available monk**, or **without taking leave when none is available**.
# Going **to another monastery, a nuns' residence, a sectarians' lodging or a refectory**.
# Going by **a road that passes through a village**.
# In **emergencies**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Not taking leave of an available monk**.
# **No allowed reason**.
# **Entering a village at the wrong time**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Kaṭhina origin**, **by doing and not doing** (entering; not taking leave), **no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Kapilavatthu · Nigrodhārāma
An **ivory-worker** invited the monks: “**Any venerable who needs a needle case, I'll provide one.**” ==The monks asked for **many needle cases** — those with small ones asked for big ones, those with big ones for small ones==. Making needle cases for the monks, he **could not make other goods for sale**, could not support himself, and **his wife and children suffered**. People complained, and the Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Aṭṭhi** — **any bone**. **Danto** — **ivory**. **Visāṇaṃ** — **any horn**.
**Kārāpeyya** — **makes or has made** — **dukkaṭa in the effort**.
**Bhedanakaṃ** — “**to be broken**” — **on obtaining it**, he must **break it** and **confess** the pācittiya.
## (B) The fourfold pācittiya
|! Begun by | Finished by | Offence
| oneself | oneself | PC
| oneself | others | PC
| others | oneself | PC
| others | others | PC
| making / having made **for another** | — | DK
| using **one made by another** after obtaining it | — | DK
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down **in the Sakyan country** concerning a number of monks, on the matter of **asking for many needle cases**. It is a **common rule**, **sāṇattika**, and a **fourfold pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The effort of having a bone, ivory or horn needle case made | DK
| For oneself — begun by oneself or others, finished by oneself or others — **obtaining** it (**fourfold pācittiya**) | PC
| Making / having made for another; using one made by another | DK
## (E) No offence
# Making any of these of bone etc.: **a toggle**, **a fire-drill bow**, **a buckle**, **an ointment box**, **an ointment stick**, **an adze handle**, **a water wiper**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **A needle case**.
# **Made of bone, ivory, horn** etc.
# **Making it for oneself, or having it made and obtaining it**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Six origins, by doing, no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
Ven. **Upananda** slept on **a high bed**. When the Blessed One, **touring the lodgings** with many monks, was seen coming from afar, Upananda said, ==“**Venerable sir, may the Blessed One come and see my bed.**”== The Blessed One turned back right there, saying, “**Monks, a foolish man can be known by his dwelling,**” and laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Navaṃ** — said with reference **to the making**.
**Mañco / pīṭhaṃ** — of **four kinds**:
|! Kind | Description
| **masāraka** | legs **inserted into holes** bored in the frame
| **bundikābaddha** | frame **fitted and bound onto the tops** of the legs
| **kuḷīrapādaka** | legs **curved like a crab's**
| **āhaccapādaka** | legs that are **detachable** (pegged in)
**A bench** is the same, but is **not very long like a bed**, nor **square like a stool**.
**Aṭṭhaṅgulapādakaṃ sugataṅgulena, aññatra heṭṭhimāya aṭaniyā** — it must be made ==with legs of **eight sugata fingerbreadths**, **not counting the lower frame** (from the ground to the frame)==. Making or having it made over that: **dukkaṭa in the effort**.
**Chedanakaṃ** — “**to be cut down**” — **on obtaining it**, he must **cut it to size** and confess the pācittiya.
## (B) If he does not wish to cut it — commentary
|! Method | Description
| **sink it** | bury the legs so that **just the proper size shows** above ground
| **upturn it** | turn it **legs upward**
| **make a platform** | raise it **onto cross-beams** and bind it **as a platform**
All these are **allowable**.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning Upananda, on the matter of **sleeping on a high bed**. It is a **common rule**, **sāṇattika**, and a **fourfold pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The effort of making / having it made over size | DK
| For oneself — begun by oneself or others — finishing and **obtaining** it (**fourfold pācittiya**) | PC
| Making / having made for another; using (uncut) one made by another | DK
## (E) No offence
# Making it **to size** or **smaller**.
# Receiving one made by another **over size** and using it after **cutting it down** so that just the proper size shows, or **sinking**, **upturning** or **binding it as a platform**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# A bed / bench **over the size**.
# **Making it for oneself, or having it made and obtaining it**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Six origins, by doing, no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six** ==had **beds and benches made stuffed with cotton and covered over**==. People touring the monastery saw them and complained, “**Just like householders enjoying sensual pleasures!**” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Mañco / pīṭhaṃ** — of **four kinds**: **masāraka** (legs inserted into holes in the frame), **bundikābaddha** (frame bound onto the tops of the legs), **kuḷīrapādaka** (legs curved like a crab's), **āhaccapādaka** (detachable legs).
**Tūlaṃ** — cotton of **three kinds**:
|! Kind | Meaning
| **rukkhatūla** | cotton **from trees** (silk-cotton etc.)
| **latātūla** | cotton **from creepers**
| **poṭakitūla** | cotton **from grasses**
**Tūlonaddhaṃ** — **spreading a cloth, putting in cotton and covering it over with cloth**.
**Kārāpeyya** — makes or has made — **dukkaṭa in the effort**.
**Uddālanakaṃ** — “**to be torn out**” — **on obtaining it**, he must **remove the stuffing** and confess the pācittiya.
## (B) The head-sized pillow — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
A pillow (**bimbohana**) is allowable only **of head size**:
|! Measure | Size
| width — between two of the three corners | **a span and four fingerbreadths**
| middle | **a cubit with a fist**
| length | **one and a half or two cubits**
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of **having cotton-stuffed beds and benches made**. It is a **common rule**, **sāṇattika**, and a **fourfold pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The effort of making / having made a cotton-stuffed bed or bench | DK
| For oneself — begun by oneself or others — finishing and **obtaining** it (**fourfold pācittiya**) | PC
| Making / having made for another; using (with stuffing) one made by another | DK
## (E) No offence
# Putting cotton into **a strap**, **a waistband**, **a shoulder strap** (for the bowl), **a bowl bag**, **a water strainer**.
# Making **a pillow** (of head size).
# Receiving a cotton-stuffed one made by another and using it after **removing the stuffing**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# **A cotton-stuffed bed or bench**.
# **Making it for oneself, or having it made and obtaining it**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
When the Blessed One had allowed **a sitting cloth**, **the group-of-six** ==used **sitting cloths of no fixed size**, letting them **hang down in front of and behind** beds and benches==. The Blessed One fixed the size — “**two sugata spans long, one and a half wide**” — and laid down the rule.
**The amendment:** Ven. **Udāyī** had **a large body**. Spreading his sitting cloth before the Blessed One, he sat down ==**stretching it out all round**==. Asked, “**Udāyī, why do you stretch your sitting cloth all round like an old leather-worker?**”, he said, “**Venerable sir, because the Blessed One allowed too small a sitting cloth.**” A **border** was then added: “**the border one span**.”
> The sitting cloth was allowed in **the Robe Chapter of the Mahāvagga** — “**for protecting the body, the robes and the lodging**” (commentary).
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Nisīdanaṃ** — said of one **with a border** (sadasaṃ): a requisite **spread like a felted rug**, with **three border strips** made by **slitting it in two places** over **a sugata span** at one end.
**Sugatavidatthi** — **the sugata span**; per the commentary, “**now three spans of a medium-sized man**, **one and a half cubits by the carpenter's cubit**” (Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī, saṅghādisesa 6).
**Chedanakaṃ** — “**to be cut down**” — **on obtaining it**, he must **cut it to size** and confess the pācittiya.
## (B) Cloth sizes in the Ratana chapter
|! Requisite | Length (sugata spans) | Width (sugata spans) | By the commentary's measure (ordinary spans)
| **sitting cloth** (Pāc. 89) | 2 | 1½ (+ border 1) | 6 × 4½ (+ border 3)
| **itch-cover cloth** (Pāc. 90) | 4 | 2 | 12 × 6
| **rains cloth** (Pāc. 91) | 6 | 2½ | 18 × 7½
| **the Sugata's robe** (Pāc. 92) | 9 | 6 | 27 × 18
> **Modern scholars differ** on the actual length of the sugata span; only **the commentarial view** is given here.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of **using sitting cloths of no fixed size**. There is **one amendment**: “**the border one span**.” It is a **rule not shared**, **sāṇattika**, and a **fourfold pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The effort of making / having made one over size | DK
| For oneself — begun by oneself or others — finishing and **obtaining** it (**fourfold pācittiya**) | PC
| Making / having made for another; using (uncut) one made by another | DK
## (E) No offence
# Making it **to size** or **smaller**.
# Receiving one made by another **over size** and using it after **cutting it down**.
# Making **a canopy, floor covering, screen wall, mattress or pillow**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# A sitting cloth **over the size**.
# **Making it for oneself, or having it made and obtaining it**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Six origins, by doing, no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ **කණ්ඩුප්පටිච්ඡාදිය** අනුදැන වදාළ කල්හි, **ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු** ==**ප්‍රමාණ රහිත කණ්ඩුප්පටිච්ඡාදි** දරමින් **ඉදිරියෙන් ද පිටුපසින් ද ඇදගෙන** ඇවිද්දහ==. භික්ෂූහු දොස් කීහ; භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූහ.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
When the Blessed One had allowed **an itch-cover cloth**, **the group-of-six** ==used **itch-cover cloths of no fixed size**, walking about **dragging them in front and behind**==. The monks criticised them, and the Blessed One laid down the rule.
> The itch-cover cloth was allowed in **the Robe Chapter of the Mahāvagga**, in the story of **Ven. Belaṭṭhasīsa** (commentary).
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Kaṇḍuppaṭicchādi** — a robe allowed for **covering** an ailment such as **itching, boils, discharge or severe scabies** ==**below the navel and above the knees**==.
**Size** — **four sugata spans long**, **two sugata spans wide** (12 × 6 ordinary spans by the commentary's measure).
**Chedanakaṃ** — “**to be cut down**” — **on obtaining it**, he must **cut it to size** and confess the pācittiya.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of **using itch-cover cloths of no fixed size**. It is a **common rule**. It is **sāṇattika**, a **fourfold pācittiya**.
## (C) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The effort of making / having made one over size | DK
| For oneself — begun by oneself or others — finishing and **obtaining** it (**fourfold pācittiya**) | PC
| Making / having made for another; using (uncut) one made by another | DK
## (D) No offence
# Making it **to size** or **smaller**.
# Receiving one made by another **over size** and using it after **cutting it down**.
# Making **a canopy, floor covering, screen wall, mattress or pillow**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (E) Factors (= dve — two)
# An itch-cover cloth **over the size**.
# **Making it for oneself, or having it made and obtaining it**.
## (F) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Six origins, by doing, no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## කථා වස්තුව
@ සැවැත් නුවර · ජේතවනාරාමය
භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ **වස්සිකසාටිකාව** අනුදැන වදාළ කල්හි, **ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු** ==**ප්‍රමාණ රහිත වස්සිකසාටිකා** දරමින් **ඉදිරියෙන් ද පිටුපසින් ද ඇදගෙන** ඇවිද්දහ==. භික්ෂූහු දොස් කීහ; භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූහ.

## (අ) පද විවරණය
**වස්සිකසාටිකා** — **වස්සානයේ සාර මාසය** සඳහා (වැසි දිය නෑමට) අනුදැන වදාළ සළුව.

**ඡෙදනකං** — "**කැපිය යුතු**" — **ලැබීමෙන්** පසු **පමණට කපා** පාචිත්තිය දේශනා කළ යුතුය.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
When the Blessed One had allowed **a rains cloth**, **the group-of-six** ==used **rains cloths of no fixed size**, walking about **dragging them in front and behind**==. The monks criticised them, and the Blessed One laid down the rule.
> The rains cloth was allowed in **the Robe Chapter of the Mahāvagga**, in the story of **Visākhā** (commentary).
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Vassikasāṭikā** — a cloth allowed **for the four months of the rains** (for bathing in the rain).
**Size** — **six sugata spans long**, **two and a half wide** (18 × 7½ ordinary spans by the commentary's measure).
**Chedanakaṃ** — “**to be cut down**” — **on obtaining it**, he must **cut it to size** and confess the pācittiya.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six, on the matter of **using rains cloths of no fixed size**. Per the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī, it is a **rule not shared**, **sāṇattika**, a **fourfold pācittiya**.
## (C) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The effort of making / having made one over size | DK
| For oneself — begun by oneself or others — finishing and **obtaining** it (**fourfold pācittiya**) | PC
| Making / having made for another; using (uncut) one made by another | DK
## (D) No offence
# Making it **to size** or **smaller**.
# Receiving one made by another **over size** and using it after **cutting it down**.
# Making **a canopy, floor covering, screen wall, mattress or pillow**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (E) Factors (= dve — two)
# A rains cloth **over the size**.
# **Making it for oneself, or having it made and obtaining it**.
## (F) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Six origins, by doing, no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
Ven. **Nanda**, **son of the Blessed One's maternal aunt**, was **handsome, good-looking and graceful**, **only four fingerbreadths shorter than the Blessed One**. He wore **a robe the size of the Sugata's robe**. ==Seeing him coming from afar, **elder monks rose from their seats, thinking “The Blessed One is coming”**==; recognising him as he drew near, they complained. The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Caturaṅgulomako** — **smaller by four fingerbreadths** (than the Blessed One) (commentary).
**Sugatacīvarappamāṇaṃ** — **nine sugata spans long**, **six sugata spans wide** (27 × 18 ordinary spans by the commentary's measure).
**Atirekaṃ vā** — ==**the size of the Sugata's robe or larger**== — so **that size itself** is an offence.
**Chedanakaṃ** — “**to be cut down**” — **on obtaining it**, he must **cut it smaller** and confess the pācittiya.
## (B) Compared with the other size rules
|! Rule | Size (sugata spans) | Is “making it to size” no offence?
| Pāc. 89 — sitting cloth | 2 × 1½ (+ border 1) | yes
| Pāc. 90 — itch-cover cloth | 4 × 2 | yes
| Pāc. 91 — rains cloth | 6 × 2½ | yes
| **Pāc. 92 — the Sugata's robe** | **9 × 6** | **no — only smaller**
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning Ven. Nanda, on the matter of **wearing a robe the size of the Sugata's robe**. It is a **common rule**. It is **sāṇattika**, a **fourfold pācittiya**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The effort of making / having made one of the Sugata's robe size or larger | DK
| For oneself — begun by oneself or others — finishing and **obtaining** it (**fourfold pācittiya**) | PC
| Making / having made for another; using (uncut) one made by another | DK
## (E) No offence
# Making it **smaller**.
# Receiving one made by another and using it after **cutting it down**.
# Making **a canopy, floor covering, screen wall, mattress or pillow**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= dve — two)
# A robe **of the Sugata's robe size or larger**.
# **Making it for oneself, or having it made and obtaining it**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Six origins, by doing, no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.
