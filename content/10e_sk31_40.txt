## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six** ==**asked for curry and rice for themselves** and ate them==. People complained: ==“**Who doesn’t like what is fine? Who doesn’t enjoy what is tasty?**”== The Blessed One laid down the rule.
**The amendment:** **sick monks** said, “**We used to ask for curry and rice and were comfortable; now, out of scruple, we don’t ask and are uncomfortable.**” The Blessed One added “**not ill**.”
## (A) Explanation of terms and related rules
**Viññāpetvā** — **having asked**.
**Agilāno** — one not ill.
|! What is asked for | Rule | Offence
| the **nine fine foods** such as ghee (not ill) | pācittiya 39 | PC
| **curry or rice** (not ill) | **sekhiya 37** | DK
> Per the Samantapāsādikā, **asking for curry and rice** is **a sekhiya dukkaṭa for both monks and nuns**.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six. It is a **common rule**, **anāṇattika**, **dukkaṭa only**.
## (C) Division of offences
|! Case | Offence
| Asking for curry or rice for oneself when not ill and eating it, **out of disrespect** | DK
## (D) No offence
# **Unintentionally**; **unmindfully**; **not knowing**.
# When **ill**.
# From **relatives** or **those who have invited**.
# **For another**.
# Obtained **with one's own funds**.
# In **emergencies**.
# For the insane and so on, and the first offender.
## (E) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Disrespect**.
# **No reason for exemption**.
# **Asking for curry or rice for oneself when not ill**.
## (F) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Thieves'-caravan origin** (body–mind; body–speech–mind), **by doing, release by perception, with intention, blameworthy by nature, bodily and verbal action, unwholesome mind, painful feeling** (Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī summary).
