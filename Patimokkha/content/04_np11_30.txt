## The Story
@ Āḷavī · Aggāḷava shrine
**The group-of-six monks** went to silk-makers and ==asked, “Friends, cook many silkworms and give us the silk; we too want to make a silk-mixed felt rug.”== They complained, “The Sakyan recluses come to us unbidden and make us kill living beings.” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Kosiyamissakaṃ** — mixed with **even a single silk fibre**, even one **blown in by the wind** where it is being made.
**Santhataṃ** — made by **spreading the fibres layer upon layer** on level ground, sprinkling them with rice-water and the like and beating them into **felt** — this is the defining mark of how it is made. (It is not woven cloth.)
**Kārāpeyya nissaggiyaṃ** — dukkaṭa in the efforts of making or having it made; **nissaggiya on receiving it**.
Forfeiture formula: “Venerable sirs, this silk-mixed felt rug that I had made is to be forfeited…” — **all the following felt-rug rules** follow this wording.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Āḷavī** concerning the group-of-six monks, on the matter of having silk-mixed felt rugs made. It is **not a common rule**. Since one may have it made for oneself, it is **sāṇattika**.
## (C) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| In the efforts of making or having it made | DK
| On receiving a rug made, or had made, for himself | NP
| **Fourfold pācittiya** — begun and finished by himself; begun by himself and finished by another; begun by another and finished by himself; begun by another and finished by another — in all four | NP
| Making or having it made **for another** | DK
| Using one made by another, after receiving it | DK
## (D) No offence
# Making **a canopy, floor-covering, screen, ground-spread, pillow, cushion** and the like.
# For the insane and so on, and for the first offenders.
## (E) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Mixed with silk**.
# Making or having made **for himself** a felt rug.
# **Receiving** it.
## (F) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
The go-between origin; by doing; not freed by perception; without intention; blameworthy by rule; bodily and verbal action; three kinds of mind; three feelings.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**A number of monks** ==had new felt rugs made every year==. Pestering people again and again for wool, they made them complain, “Our houses are filled with wool for rugs.” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
Later at Kosambī, **a sick monk** invited by his relatives could not carry his heavy rug, so “except with the authorisation of the monks” was added.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Navaṃ pana bhikkhunā santhataṃ kārāpetvā chabbassāni dhāretabbaṃ** — having had a new rug made, he is to keep it **for six years**.
**Orena ce channaṃ vassānaṃ** — **within** six years.
**Aññatra bhikkhusammutiyā** — except for the **rug authorisation** that the Saṅgha gives **a sick monk**, one without it who makes another rug within six years has a nissaggiya.
**One with the authorisation** may make one **every year, wherever he goes**, as long as the illness has not subsided, or has subsided and flared up again.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning a number of monks, on the matter of having rugs made every year. “Except with the authorisation of the monks” is the **supplementary rule**. It is **not a common rule**, and **sāṇattika**.
## (C) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī

## (D) No offence

# With **the monks' authorisation**.
# Making it **for another**; using one **made by another**.
# **Canopies, floor-coverings** and the like.
# For the insane and so on, and for the first offenders.
## (E) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Six years not complete**, and no authorisation.
# Making or having made **for himself** a new rug.

**Matchmaking (go-between) origin** (six origins — body; speech; body–speech; body–mind; speech–mind; body–speech–mind), **by doing and not doing**, **no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
When the Blessed One announced he would spend three months in seclusion, hearing that only those keeping the austere practices might visit, **a number of monks** ==discarded their old rugs== and took up the wilderness, almsfood and rag-robe practices. Walking through the dwellings, the Blessed One saw ==rugs thrown about here and there==, asked the reason, and laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Nisīdanasanthataṃ** — a felt rug for sitting.
**Purāṇasanthataṃ** — a rug sat or lain on **even once**.
**Samantā sugatavidatthi** — the piece cut from one side of the old rug, **round or square**, should measure **one sugata span**.
**Dubbaṇṇakaraṇāya** — to dull the colour of the new rug. When spreading, it may be laid **in one place**, or **teased apart and mixed in** — that way it is stronger.
**Anādā ce** — not taking it **when there is** an old rug. **If there is none**, it is allowed without taking any.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning a number of monks, on the matter of discarding rugs. It is **not a common rule**, and **sāṇattika**.
## (C) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| In the efforts of making or having it made | DK
| On receiving a sitting rug made for himself without taking a sugata span from an existing old rug (fourfold pācittiya) | NP
## (D) No offence
# Not taking any when **there is no old rug**.
# Making it **taking** a sugata span; or taking **less** (Pāḷi).
# Making it **for another**; using one **made by another**.
# **Canopies, floor-coverings** and the like.
# For the insane and so on, and for the first offenders.
## (E) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Not taking a sugata span** though there is an old rug.
# Making or having made **for himself** a sitting rug.
# **Receiving** it.
## (F) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Matchmaking (go-between) origin** (six origins — body; speech; body–speech; body–mind; speech–mind; body–speech–mind), **by doing and not doing**, **no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**A certain monk**, travelling from Kosala to Sāvatthī, was given goat's wool on the way. As he ==carried it tied up in his robe, people mocked him: “Venerable sir, how much did you pay for it? What profit will you make?”== He was ashamed. The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Addhānamaggappaṭipannassa** — for one on a long road. This merely **states the occasion**; there is no fault in receiving it lawfully **anywhere**.
**Tiyojanaparamaṃ** — up to **three yojanas** **from the place where it was received**.
**Sahatthā** — to be carried **by himself**.
**Asante hārake** — when there is **no one else** to carry it; if there is, he may have him carry it.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning a certain monk, on the matter of exceeding three yojanas. It is **not a common rule**, and **anāṇattika**.
## (C) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Carrying wool — even some **put untied in the ear-hole as a remedy for wind illness** — and taking **the first step** beyond three yojanas | DK

## The Story
@ Sakyan country · Nigrodhārāma at Kapilavatthu
**The group-of-six monks** ==had nuns wash, dye and card goat's wool==. The nuns' hands were stained with dye, and they had no time for **recitation, questioning, higher virtue, higher mind and higher wisdom**. When **Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī** told the Blessed One, he laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Dhovāpeyya** — has it washed. **Rajāpeyya** — has it dyed. **Vijaṭāpeyya** — has it teased apart and carded.
The analysis covers — the unrelated nun, the ways of commanding, cloth given to a trainee or laywoman who washes it after ordination, and so on.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down in **the Sakyan country** concerning the group-of-six monks, on the matter of having wool washed. It is **not a common rule**, and **sāṇattika**.
## (C) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| In the efforts of the nun so commanded | DK
| The moment it is washed, dyed or carded (**triple pācittiya**) | NP
| Having all three or two done — one nissaggiya, the rest dukkaṭa | NP + DK
| A related nun perceived as unrelated / in doubt; one ordained on one side; having another's wool washed | DK
## (D) No offence
# A **related nun** washes it; her companion washes it **unasked**.
# Having wool **already made into an article** washed.
# A **trainee nun or novice nun** washes it.
# For the insane and so on, and for the first offenders.
## (E) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Goat's wool not made into an article**.
# **Commanding an unrelated nun**.
# Her **washing and so on**.
## (F) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Matchmaking (go-between) origin** (six origins — body; speech; body–speech; body–mind; speech–mind; body–speech–mind), **by doing**, **no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## The Story
@ Rājagaha · Veḷuvana
A family at Rājagaha that supported Ven. **Upananda** had set aside **a portion of meat** for him. At night a child of the family cried, and they gave it to him; in the morning, when Upananda came, they said, “Venerable sir, the meat is gone; what shall we bring you for a kahāpaṇa?” He ==said, “Give me the kahāpaṇa,” and accepted it==. People complained, “The Sakyan recluses accept gold and silver just as we do.” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) The meaning of “jātarūparajataṃ”
**Jātarūpaṃ** — **gold**. **Rajataṃ** — **silver**; and also **kahāpaṇas**, and **copper, wooden and lac māsakas** and all that **pass as currency**, are here included under “silver.”
## (B) “Uggaṇheyya”, “uggaṇhāpeyya”, “sādiyeyya”
|! Term | Meaning
| Uggaṇheyya | **Taking it himself** — something given to him, or unowned and lying anywhere.
| Uggaṇhāpeyya | **Having another take** it.
| Upanikkhittaṃ vā sādiyeyya | When something is relinquished to him — face to face, “Let this be the master's,” or in his absence, “My gold is in such a place; let it be yours,” by word or gesture — **consenting to it in mind without refusing it by body or speech**.
If, though wanting it in his mind, he refuses it by body or speech — “This is not allowable” — or, without refusing by body or speech, **with a pure mind** does not consent, thinking “This is not allowable for us,” it is **allowed**.
**Nissaggiyaṃ** — for one who does any of these, a nissaggiya pācittiya **for each item**, if the items are **not fastened together**.
## (C) Forfeiture and the money-disposer
It must be forfeited **in the midst of the Saṅgha**: “Venerable sirs, I accepted money; this is to be forfeited by me; I forfeit it to the Saṅgha.”
|! Situation | What to do
| If a layman comes there | He should be told, “Know this.”
| If he asks, “What shall I bring with it?” | Without saying “this,” he should be told only what is **allowable**: “Ghee and the like are allowable for monks.”
| If he brings something | All share and use it **except the monk who accepted the money**. He may not use anything arising from it — **not even the shade of a tree grown from it**.
| If he does not want to bring anything | He should be told, “Throw it away”; wherever he puts it, or if he takes it away, he should not be stopped.
| If he will not throw it away | A monk with **five qualities** should be appointed **money-disposer** — he should throw it away **without noting the place, like excrement**; if he notes it, a dukkaṭa.
**The five qualities of a money-disposer** — not going wrong through desire, hatred, delusion or fear, and **knowing what has and has not been thrown away**.
## (D) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Rājagaha** concerning Ven. Upananda, on the matter of accepting money. It is a **common rule**. Because of “has another take it,” it is **sāṇattika**.
## (E) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Taking, having taken, or consenting to gold or money for himself — by the number of items (**triple pācittiya**) | NP
| Something not money perceived as money, or in doubt | DK
| Taking it **for the Saṅgha, a shrine** and so on | DK
| Accepting **pearls, gems** and the like (dukkaṭa objects) | DK
| The money-disposer throwing it away while noting the place | DK
## (F) No offence
# Picking up what has fallen within a monastery or rest-house **to keep it for the owner**.
# For the insane and so on, and for the first offender.
## (G) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# **Gold or silver** (money).
# **For himself**.
# One of **taking, having it taken, or consenting**.
## (H) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Matchmaking (go-between) origin** (six origins — body; speech; body–speech; body–mind; speech–mind; body–speech–mind), **by doing or by not doing** (falling into it by taking is doing; by not refusing, not doing), **no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

**ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූහු** ==නොයෙක් ආකාරයෙන් රූපිය හුවමාරු කළහ — රන්වලින් රිදී, රිදීවලින් රන් ආදී වශයෙන්==. මිනිස්සු "ශාක්‍යපුත්‍රීය ශ්‍රමණයෝ කාමභෝගී ගිහියන් මෙන් රූපිය ගනුදෙනු කරති" යි දොස් කීහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූහ.
## (අ) පද විවරණය
**නානප්පකාරකං** — **කළ** (ආභරණාදී ලෙස කළ), **නොකළ**, **කළ-නොකළ** ආදී වශයෙන් නොයෙක් ආකාර.
**රූපියසංවොහාරං** — **රන් රිදී (මුදල්) හුවමාරු කිරීම**.
පෙර ශික්ෂාපදයෙන් නිස්සග්ගිය වස්තු හා දුක්කට වස්තු **පිළිගැනීම** වළක්වන ලදී; මෙයින් **හුවමාරු කිරීම** වළක්වයි.

එය **සංඝ මධ්‍යයේ** නිස්සජ්ජනය කළ යුතුය. ගිහියෙක් පැමිණියොත් "**මෙය දැනගන්න**" යි කිව යුතුය; "මෙයින් කුමක් ගෙනෙන්න ද?" යි ඇසුවොත් **ගිතෙල් ආදී කැප දෙය පමණක්** කිව යුතුය. ඔහු ගෙනාවොත් **හුවමාරු කළ භික්ෂුව හැර** සියල්ලන් පරිභෝග කළ යුතුය; ඔහු ඉවත නොදමයි නම් **අංග පහකින් යුත් රූපිය ඡට්ටකයෙකු** සම්මත කොට **සලකුණක් නොතබා** ඉවත දැමවිය යුතුය.
## (ඇ) ප්‍රඥප්තිය හා ආණත්තිය
**සැවැත් නුවරදී** ෂඩ්වර්ගීය භික්ෂූන් අරබයා රූපිය හුවමාරුවේ වස්තුවෙහි පැනවිණි. **සාධාරණ ප්‍රඥප්තියකි**. **අනාණත්තිකයි**.

# උමතු ආදීන්ට හා ආදිකම්මිකයන්ට පමණි.

# **හුවමාරු කිරීම**.
## (ඊ) සමුට්ඨානාදිය
**සඤ්චරිත්ත සමුට්ඨානයි** (ඡසමුට්ඨාන — කාය, වාචා, කාය-වාචා, කාය-චිත්ත, වාචා-චිත්ත, කාය-වාචා-චිත්ත), **කිරියයි**, **නෝසඤ්ඤාවිමොක්ඛයි, අචිත්තකයි, පණ්ණත්තිවජ්ජයි, කාය-වචීකම්මයි, තිචිත්තයි, තිවේදනයි**.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
**The group-of-six monks** ==engaged in various kinds of money transactions — silver for gold, gold for silver and so on==. People complained, “The Sakyan recluses deal in money like householders who enjoy sensual pleasures.” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Nānappakārakaṃ** — of various kinds: **worked** (into ornaments and the like), **unworked**, **partly worked** and so on.
**Rūpiyasaṃvohāraṃ** — **exchanging gold and silver (money)**.
The previous rule forbade **accepting** objects entailing forfeiture or dukkaṭa; this one forbids **exchanging** them.
## (B) Offences in exchange
|! Gives | Gets | Offence
| A dukkaṭa object (pearls, gems etc.) | A dukkaṭa object or an allowable thing | DK
| An allowable thing | A dukkaṭa object | DK
| An object entailing forfeiture (gold, silver, money) | An object entailing forfeiture, a dukkaṭa object or an allowable thing | NP
| A dukkaṭa object or an allowable thing | An object entailing forfeiture | NP
It is to be forfeited **in the midst of the Saṅgha**. If a layman comes, he should be told, “**Know this**”; if he asks what to bring with it, only **allowable things such as ghee** should be named. If he brings something, all may use it **except the monk who made the exchange**; if he will not throw it away, **a money-disposer with five qualities** is appointed, who throws it away **without noting the place**.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning the group-of-six monks, on the matter of trading with money. It is a **common rule**, and **anāṇattika**.
## (D) No offence
# Only for the insane and so on, and for the first offenders.
## (E) Factors (= dve — two)
# What he exchanges with his own funds, **or** the funds he exchanges, **being money**.
# **Exchanging**.
## (F) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Matchmaking (go-between) origin** (six origins — body; speech; body–speech; body–mind; speech–mind; body–speech–mind), **by doing**, **no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.

## කථා වස්තුව
@ සැවැත් නුවර · ජේතවනාරාමය
**උපනන්ද** තෙරුන් දක්ෂ ලෙස මසා මනාව පඬු පෙවූ සිවුරක් දුටු පරිබ්‍රාජකයෙක් "මේ සිවුර මට දෙන්න, මම ඔබට මගේ සළුව දෙමි" යි කීවේය. "දැනගන්න" යි ==මිල කථා කොට හුවමාරුව කළ තෙරුන්==, පසුව මිතුරන්ගේ වචනයෙන් පසුතැවිලි වූ පරිබ්‍රාජකයා සළුව ආපසු ඉල්ලූ කල්හි, ==ආපසු දීම ප්‍රතික්ෂේප කළහ==. පරිබ්‍රාජකයා "පසුතැවිලි වූ ගිහියෙකුට ගිහියෝ පවා ආපසු දෙති; පැවිද්දෙකු පැවිද්දෙකුට නොදෙන්නේ ද?" යි දොස් කීවේය. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූහ.
## (අ) පද විවරණය
**නානප්පකාරකං** — සිවුරු ආදී **කැප භාණ්ඩ** වශයෙන් නොයෙක් ආකාර.
**කයවික්කයං** — **මිලට ගැනීම (කය) හා විකිණීම (වික්කය)**. "මෙයින් මෙය දෙන්න, මෙය ගෙනෙන්න, හුවමාරු කරන්න, මිලට ගන්න" ආදී ලෙස **අනුන්ගේ කැප භාණ්ඩය ගන්නා විට කය** ද, **තමාගේ කැප භාණ්ඩය දෙන විට වික්කය** ද වේ.
එබැවින් **සහධාර්මිකයන් පස් දෙනා හැර**, එසේ තමාගේ කැප භාණ්ඩය දී **මවගේ සන්තක** කැප භාණ්ඩයක් වුව ද ගන්නේ නම් නිස්සග්ගිය වේ.
## (ආ) නිස්සජ්ජනය
නිස්සග්ගිය වචනයෙන් **සංඝයාට, ගණයකට හෝ පුද්ගලයෙකුට** නිස්සජ්ජනය කළ යුතුය. "මෙය වළඳා හෝ ගෙන මෙය ගෙනෙන්න, කරන්න" යි පඬු ආදිය ගෙන්වා, හෝ **ධමකරණය** (පෙරහන) ආදී පිරිකර, **බිම පිරිසිදු කිරීම** ආදී නව කර්ම කරවා ගත්තේ නම් — වස්තුව ඇත්නම් නිස්සජ්ජනය කළ යුතුය; **නැත්නම් පාචිත්තිය පමණක්** දෙසිය යුතුය.

## The Story
@ Sāvatthī · Jetavana monastery
A wanderer saw a robe that Ven. **Upananda** had skilfully sewn and well dyed, and said, “Give me this robe and I'll give you my cloak.” Saying “You decide,” Upananda ==bargained and made the exchange==. Later the wanderer, persuaded by his friends, regretted it and asked for his cloak back, but Upananda ==refused to return it==. The wanderer complained, “Even householders give back to a householder who regrets a deal; won't one gone forth give back to one gone forth?” The Blessed One laid down the rule.
## (A) Explanation of terms
**Nānappakārakaṃ** — of various kinds, by way of **allowable goods** such as robes.
**Kayavikkayaṃ** — **buying (kaya) and selling (vikkaya)**. Saying, “Give this for that, bring this, exchange, buy,” one **buys** when taking another's allowable goods and **sells** when giving one's own.
So, **except with the five co-religionists**, if he gives his own allowable goods and takes allowable goods in this way — **even his mother's** — it is nissaggiya.
## (B) Forfeiture
It is to be forfeited **to a Saṅgha, a group or an individual** with the forfeiture formula. If he has had dye and the like brought — “Eat this, or take this, and bring or do that” — or had requisites such as **a water-strainer** made, or new work such as **clearing ground** done: if the object remains it is to be forfeited; **if not, only the pācittiya** is to be confessed.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Sāvatthī** concerning Ven. Upananda, on the matter of buying and selling. It is a **common rule**, and **anāṇattika**.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Getting allowable goods with his own allowable goods from one who is not a co-religionist (**triple pācittiya**) | NP
| Perceiving a non-trade as trade, or in doubt | DK
| When the object no longer exists (eaten or used up) | PC
## (E) No offence
# Asking the price: **“What does this cost?”**
# Making **someone other** than the seller — **even his own son or brother** — a steward and telling him, “Get this with that and give it to me.”
# Saying, **“We have this; we need this and this,”** and taking what is obtained with his own goods.
# Exchanging **with co-religionists**.
# For the insane and so on, and for the first offender.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# What he exchanges and what he gets **being allowable things**.
# The other party **not a co-religionist**.
# **Engaging in buying and selling**.
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
**Matchmaking (go-between) origin** (six origins — body; speech; body–speech; body–mind; speech–mind; body–speech–mind), **by doing**, **no release by perception, without intention, blameworthy by convention, bodily and verbal action, three kinds of mind, three feelings**.
