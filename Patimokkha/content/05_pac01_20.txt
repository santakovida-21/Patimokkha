## The Story
@ Kosambī · Ghositārāma
**Ven. Channa**, again and again, had the finished dwelling that had been built for him **roofed and plastered**, so that through the excess weight it collapsed; gathering grass and wood, he also damaged a brahmin's barley field. The Blessed One laid down the rule:
> “Mahallakaṃ pana bhikkhunā vihāraṃ kārayamānena yāvadvārakosā aggaḷaṭṭhapanāya ālokasandhiparikammāya dvatticchadanassa pariyāyaṃ appaharite ṭhitena adhiṭṭhātabbaṃ. Tato ce uttariṃ appaharitepi ṭhito adhiṭṭhaheyya, pācittiyaṃ.”
## (A) The meaning of “mahallakaṃ”, “yāva dvārakosā”, “aggaḷaṭṭhapanāya”, “ālokasandhiparikammāya”
# **Mahallaka** — having an owner (one that a householder is building for him); **vihāra** — plastered inside, outside, or both.
# **Yāva dvārakosā** — the area of two and a half cubits (*aḍḍhateyya hattha*) around the door frame.
# **Aggaḷaṭṭhapanāya** — so that the door panel is fixed firmly. Because the door panel strikes the wall when opened and the frame when closed, the wall shakes and the clay loosens and falls; therefore one may plaster again and again up to the door frame.
# **Ālokasandhiparikammāya** — for finishing the window panels; because they strike when opened, one may plaster a space the width of a panel in every direction.
## (B) The meaning of “dvatticchadanassa pariyāyaṃ”
Pariyāya = a layer (of roofing); two or three layers may be supervised. **Appaharite ṭhitena** — standing in a place without crops; crops are the first and later grains (*pubbaṇṇa-aparaṇṇa*); even those not yet ripe, which will ripen with rain, count as crops; if one supervises standing among crops, there is a dukkaṭa.
The limit for standing where there are no crops: one must stand in a place where a man sitting at the side of the ridge-pole or the pinnacle of a peaked roof, looking from the edge of the eaves, can see the monk; even if it is without crops inside that, it is not allowed.
**Tato ce uttari** — going beyond three courses when building in courses; beyond three layers when building in layers; there is a pācittiya according to the number of bricks, stones or lumps of plaster; with grass and leaves, according to the number of leaves or bundles of grass.
## (C) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Kosambī** concerning Ven. Channa. It is a **common rule**; it is **anāṇattika**; it is a **tika-pācittiya**; for one who perceives more than two or three layers when it is less, or is in doubt, there is a dukkaṭa.
## (D) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| Supervising beyond two or three layers (in all three perceptions: more, doubt, less) — by the number of bricks, stones, lumps of plaster, bundles of grass or leaves | PC
| When it is less than two or three layers, perceiving it as more, or being in doubt | DK
| Supervising while standing among crops | DK
| When it is less than two or three layers, perceiving it as less | N
## (E) No offence
# In whitewashing and so on;
# Within two or three layers, or less than that;
# In caves, grottoes, grass huts and so on;
# For one who builds for others, or with his own wealth;
# For one who supervises anywhere other than the living quarters;
# For the insane and so on.
## (F) Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# It is a **large dwelling**.
# It is **one's own living quarters**.
# **Supervising beyond** [the limit].
## (G) Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
As stated in the **Sañcaritta rule**.

## The Story
@ Āḷavī · Aggāḷava shrine
The **Āḷavaka monks**, doing building work, ==knowing that the water contained living beings==, wetted grass and clay with that water, and had it wetted. The Blessed One laid down the rule:
> “Yo pana bhikkhu jānaṃ sappāṇakaṃ udakaṃ tiṇaṃ vā mattikaṃ vā siñceyya vā siñcāpeyya vā, pācittiyaṃ.”
## (A) The meaning of “jānaṃ sappāṇakaṃ”, “siñceyya”, “siñcāpeyya”
**Jānaṃ sappāṇakaṃ** — knowing, by having seen or heard, “This has living beings in it.” **Siñceyya** — pours it himself; **siñcāpeyya** — commands another and has it poured.
For one who pours without breaking the stream, there is one offence for one pot; for one who pours breaking it, according to the number of efforts. For one who makes a channel flow, there is one offence even if it flows the whole day; for one who blocks it here and there and leads it elsewhere, according to the number of efforts. For one who throws in many grasses, leaves and branches with one effort, there is one offence; for one who throws them in one by one, according to the number of efforts.
This was said about water that becomes less, gets stirred up and in which the beings die when things are thrown in; not about a great body of water. In having it poured, there is a dukkaṭa for the command; even if much is poured on one command, the one who commands has one pācittiya.
## (B) The laying down of the rule and the command
It was laid down at **Āḷavī** concerning the Āḷavaka monks, on the matter of sprinkling. It is a **common rule**; it is **sāṇattika** (the offence also arises by getting another person to do it).
## (C) Division of offences — according to the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī
|! Case | Offence
| The water has beings in it, with the perception that it has beings, knowing “the beings will die by pouring”, pouring it on grass or clay or having it poured | PC
| Being in doubt about water with beings, pouring it or having it poured | DK
| Water without beings, perceiving it as having beings or being in doubt, pouring it or having it poured | DK
| Water with beings, perceiving it as without beings | N
| Water without beings, perceiving it as without beings | N
## (D) No offence
# For one who thinks there are no beings;
# For one who pours without intending to, or without mindfulness;
# For one who does not know;
# For the insane and so on.
## (E) Factors (= cattāri — four)
# The water **contains beings**.
# Knowing, “**the beings will die** by pouring.”
# The water **is in fact like that**.
# Pouring grass and so on, or having it poured, for some purpose **without the intention to kill**.

As in the **Adinnādāna rule**; the difference is that this is **paṇṇattivajja**, **ticitta** and **tivedana**.
