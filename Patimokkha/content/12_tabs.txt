## {story} කථා වස්තුව
### රුක්ඛඡින්දන වස්තුව
@ ආළවිය · අග්ගාළව චෛත්‍යය
:::short
ආළවියෙහි නවකර්ම කරන ආළවක භික්ෂූහු ==ගස් කැපූහ, කප්පවූහ==. එක් භික්ෂුවක් ගසක් කපන විට, එහි වූ දේවතාවිය තම භවනය නොකපන ලෙස ඉල්ලුවා ය; ඔහු එය නොසලකා කපා, ඇගේ දරුවාගේ බාහුවට ද පහර දුන්නේ ය. ඇය ක්‍රෝධය නවතා භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට සැල කළා ය; උන්වහන්සේ භික්ෂුව ජීවිතයෙන් තොර නොකිරීම ගැන ඇයට ප්‍රශංසා කොට, හිස් (**විවිත්ත**) ගසකට යැවූ සේක. ගසෙහි ජීවයක් ඇතැයි සලකන මිනිස්සු “ශ්‍රමණයෝ **ඒකේන්ද්‍රිය ජීවයක්** පෙළති” යි දොස් කීහ. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ භික්ෂූන්ට ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක.

:::long
එක් සමයෙක බුදුරජාණන් වහන්සේ ආළවියෙහි අග්ගාළව චෛත්‍යයෙහි වැඩ වසන සේක. එකල ආළවක භික්ෂූහු නවකර්ම කරමින් ==ගස් කපති, කප්පවති==. එක් ආළවක භික්ෂුවක් ද ගසක් කපයි. ඒ ගසෙහි අධිවාසය කළ දේවතාවිය ඒ භික්ෂුවට “ස්වාමීනි, තමන්ගේ භවනය කරනු කැමැත්තෙන් මාගේ භවනය නොකපනු මැනවි” යි කීවා ය. ඒ භික්ෂුව එය නොසලකා කැපුවේ ම ය; ඒ දේවතාවියගේ දරුවාගේ බාහුවට ද පහර දුන්නේ ය.
එවිට ඒ දේවතාවියට “මම මේ භික්ෂුව මෙහිදී ම ජීවිතයෙන් තොර කරන්නෙම් නම් මැනවි” යි සිතිණ. නැවත “මා මේ භික්ෂුව ජීවිතයෙන් තොර කිරීම සුදුසු නොවේ; මේ කරුණ භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට සැල කරන්නෙම් නම් මැනවි” යි සිතා, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ වෙත එළඹ ඒ කරුණ සැල කළා ය.
භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “දේවතාවිය, යහපති, යහපති! ඔබ ඒ භික්ෂුව ජීවිතයෙන් තොර නොකළේ යහපති. ඉදින් අද ඔබ ඒ භික්ෂුව ජීවිතයෙන් තොර කළා නම් බොහෝ පව් උපදවා ගන්නෙහි ය. දේවතාවිය, යන්න; අසවල් තැන හිස් (**විවිත්ත**) ගසක් ඇත; එයට එළඹෙන්න” යි වදාළ සේක.
මිනිස්සු “ශාක්‍යපුත්‍රීය ශ්‍රමණයෝ කෙසේ නම් ගස් කපන්නාහු ද, කප්පවන්නාහු ද? ශාක්‍යපුත්‍රීය ශ්‍රමණයෝ **ඒකේන්ද්‍රිය ජීවයක්** පෙළන්නාහු ය” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ. ඒ නින්දාව ඇසූ අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු ද “ආළවක භික්ෂූහු කෙසේ නම් ගස් කපන්නාහු ද, කප්පවන්නාහු ද?” යි දොස් කියා භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට සැල කළහ.
භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “මහණෙනි, ඔබ ගස් කපන බවත් කප්පවන බවත් සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් ගස් කපන්නාහු ද, කප්පවන්නාහු ද? මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, ==මිනිස්සු ගසෙහි ජීවයක් ඇතැයි සලකන්නෝ ය==. මෝඝපුරුෂයෙනි, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා ශික්ෂාපදය පැනවූ සේක.

## {story} අනාපත්ති
# “මෙය දැනගන්න, මෙය දෙන්න, මෙය ගෙනෙන්න, මෙයින් ප්‍රයෝජන ඇත, මෙය කප්පිය කරන්න” යි කියයි නම්;
# නොසිතා (**අසඤ්චිච්ච**) විනාශ වූ කල්හි;
# සිහි නොමැතිව (**අස්සතියා**) විනාශ වූ කල්හි;
# නොදන්නාහට (**අජානන්තස්ස**);
# උමතු වූවහුට (**උම්මත්තකස්ස**);
# ආදිකම්මිකයාට (**ආදිකම්මිකස්ස**).
## {att} කථා වස්තුවට අදාළ විශේෂ කරුණු
- **දරුවාගේ අත කැපුණු ආකාරය** — එසවූ පොරොව නවතා ගත නොහැකිව, මිනිස් ඇසට නොපෙනෙන, මහරජුන් වෙතින් ලද **රුක්ඛට්ඨක** දිව්‍ය විමානයෙහි සැතපී සිටි දරුවාගේ අත ළය මුලින් ම (**ථනමූලෙ**) කැපිණ.
- **“රුක්ඛධම්ම”** — හිමවතෙහි පක්ෂ දිනවල දේවතා සමාගමක් වේ. එහිදී “ඔබ රුක්ඛධම්මයෙහි පිහිටියෙහි ද?” යි විමසති. ගස කපන කල ඒ ගසෙහි දේවතාවා සිත නොදූෂ්‍ය කර ගැනීම **රුක්ඛධම්ම** යි. එහි නොපිහිටි දේවතාවට ඒ සමාගමට ඇතුළු වීමට නොලැබේ. මේ ආදීනවය ද, **ඡද්දන්ත** ආදී කාලයෙහි තථාගතයන්ගේ පූර්ව චරිතය ද සිහි කළ හෙයින් ඇය භික්ෂුව නොමැරුවා ය.
- **භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට සැල කිරීමට හේතුව** — “මේ භික්ෂුව පියෙකු ඇති පුතෙකි (ශාස්තෘවරයකු ඇත්තෙකි); භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ මේ වරද අසා සීමාවක් බඳිනු ඇත, ශික්ෂාපදයක් පනවනු ඇත” යි සලකා ය.
- **දේශනාව හා ඵලය** — භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ඇය සනසමින් නැගුණු ක්‍රෝධය නවතා ගන්නා තැනැත්තා රියැදුරෙකු යැයි දක්වන ගාථාව (ධම්මපද 222) වදාළ සේක; ගාථාව අවසානයේ ඇය **සෝවාන් ඵලයෙහි** පිහිටියා ය. ඉන්පසු පිරිසට ක්‍රෝධය දුරු කරන භික්ෂුව සර්පයා දිරූ සැවය හරින්නාක් මෙන් මෙලොව පරලොව හරින බව දක්වන ගාථාව (සුත්තනිපාත 1) වදාළ සේක.
- **හිස් ගස** — දේවතාවියට දුන් ගස ආළවියෙහි නොව **ජේතවනයේ වැට ඇතුළත** විය; එහි හිමිකරු වූ දෙවියා චුත වූ හෙයින් “හිස්” (**විවිත්ත**) යැයි කියන ලදී. එතැන් පටන් ඇය බුද්ධෝපස්ථායිකාවක් විය; මහේශාක්‍ය දෙවියන් පැමිණෙන කල අල්පේශාක්‍ය දෙවියන් ඉවත්ව යතත්, ඇයට තම වාසස්ථානයෙහි ම හිඳ බණ ඇසීමට ලැබිණ. පළමු යාමයෙහි භික්ෂූන් ද, මැදියම් යාමයෙහි දෙවියන් ද අසන ප්‍රශ්න සියල්ල ඇය එහි ම හිඳ අසයි; සතරවරම් රජවරු ද භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේට උපස්ථානයට පැමිණ යන විට ඇය දැක ම යති.
## {att} “බීජජාත” හා පාළියේ නැති අමතර උදාහරණ
පැරණි අටුවාවල කී විවරණය පාළියේ “යානි වා පනඤ්ඤානිපි අත්ථි මූලෙ ජායන්ති” ආදිය සමඟ නොගැළපෙන හෙයින් සමන්තපාසාදිකාව මෙසේ විවරණය කරයි. **“බීජජාත”** යන්නට අර්ථ දෙකකි:

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of Cutting Trees (Rukkhachindana-vatthu)
@ Āḷavī · Aggāḷava shrine
:::short
At Āḷavī the Āḷavaka monks, doing building work, ==cut trees and had them cut==. When one monk cut a tree, the deity living in it asked him not to destroy her dwelling; he ignored her and struck her child’s arm. Restraining her anger, she told the Blessed One, who praised her for not taking the monk’s life and sent her to a vacant (**vivitta**) tree. People, who regard trees as having life, criticised the monks for harming **a being with one faculty**. The Blessed One rebuked the monks and laid down the training rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Āḷavī, at the Aggāḷava shrine. At that time the Āḷavaka monks, doing building work, ==cut trees and had them cut==. One Āḷavaka monk was cutting a tree. The deity who lived in that tree said to the monk: “Venerable sir, wishing to build a dwelling for yourself, do not cut down my dwelling.” Paying no attention, the monk cut it anyway, and struck the arm of the deity’s child.
Then the deity thought: “What if I were to take this monk’s life right here?” Then she thought: “It would not be proper for me to take this monk’s life. What if I were to tell this matter to the Blessed One?” So she approached the Blessed One and told him the matter.
“Good, good, deity! It is good that you did not take that monk’s life. Had you taken that monk’s life today, you would have produced much demerit. Go, deity; in such-and-such a place there is a vacant tree. Go there.”
People complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can the Sakyan recluses cut trees and have them cut? The Sakyan recluses are harming **a being with one faculty**!” Hearing the people complaining, the monks of few wishes also complained: “How can the Āḷavaka monks cut trees and have them cut?” — and they told the Blessed One.
The Blessed One asked: “Is it true, monks, that you cut trees and have them cut?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked them: “Foolish men, how can you cut trees and have them cut? Foolish men, ==people regard a tree as having life==. Foolish men, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” — and he laid down the training rule.
:::

## {story} Word analysis — bhūtagāma (the five kinds of seed)
**Bhūtagāma** (plant life) means the five kinds of seed: root-seed (**mūlabīja**), trunk-seed (**khandhabīja**), joint-seed (**phaḷubīja**), tip-seed (**aggabīja**), and fifth, seed-seed (**bījabīja**).
|! Kind of seed | Examples given in the Pāli | Where it grows from
| **Mūlabīja** | Haliddi (turmeric), siṅgivera (ginger), vacā, vacatta, ativisā, kaṭukarohiṇī, usīra, bhaddamuttaka | Grows from the root, arises from the root
| **Khandhabīja** | Assattha, nigrodha (banyan), pilakkha, udumbara (cluster fig), kacchaka, kapitthana | Grows from the trunk, arises from the trunk
| **Phaḷubīja** | Ucchu (sugarcane), veḷu (bamboo), naḷa (reed) | Grows from a joint, arises from a joint
| **Aggabīja** | Ajjuka, phaṇijjaka, hirivera | Grows from the tip, arises from the tip
| **Bījabīja** | Pubbaṇṇa, aparaṇṇa (kinds of grain) | Grows from the seed, arises from the seed
Besides those named in the Pāli, whatever else grows from a root, trunk, joint, tip or seed also belongs to that same kind of seed.

# When it is destroyed unintentionally (**asañcicca**);

## {att} Special points about the origin story
- **How the child’s arm was cut** — unable to stop the raised axe, the monk cut off, right at the base of the breast (**thanamūle**), the arm of the child lying in the tree-dwelling divine mansion that the deity had received from the Great King and that is beyond human sight.
- **“Rukkhadhamma”** (the duty of a tree deity) — on the fortnightly days there is an assembly of deities in the Himalaya, where they ask, “Do you stand in rukkhadhamma?” **Rukkhadhamma** means that, when a tree is being cut, its deity does not let her mind become corrupted with anger. A deity who does not stand in it may not enter that assembly. Seeing this danger, and recalling the Tathāgata’s past conduct (as in the **Chaddanta** birth), she did not kill the monk.
- **Why she told the Blessed One** — she reflected: “This monk is a son who has a father (a teacher); hearing of this misconduct, the Blessed One will set a boundary and lay down a training rule.”
- **The teaching and its fruit** — consoling her, the Blessed One spoke the verse that calls the one who checks arisen anger a true charioteer (Dhammapada 222); at its end she was established in the **fruit of stream-entry**. Then, teaching the assembly, he spoke the verse on the monk who removes anger as a snake sheds its old skin (Suttanipāta 1).
- **The vacant tree** — the tree given to the deity was not at Āḷavī but **inside the enclosure of Jetavana**; its owner, a young deva, had passed away, so it was called “vacant” (**vivitta**). From then on she became an attendant of the Buddha; when powerful deities arrived and lesser ones withdrew, she could stay seated in her own dwelling and hear the Dhamma. The questions the monks asked in the first watch and the deities in the middle watch — she heard them all seated there; and the Four Great Kings, coming to attend on the Blessed One, saw her before they left.
## {att} “Bījajāta” and extra examples not in the Pāli
Because the older commentaries’ explanation does not fit the Pāli “whatever else grows from a root” and so on, the Samantapāsādikā explains it this way. **“Bījajāta”** is explained in two ways:
# What is born from seeds — a name for trees and so on.
# Seeds that have sprouted, producing leaves and roots — this includes ginger and the like placed in moist sand that have put out leaves and roots.
“Whatever else grows from a root” means: **the root** in which shrubs, creepers, trees and so on grow, together with the turmeric and so on named in the Pāli — all of this is called **mūlabīja**. The same method applies to trunk-seed and the rest.
|! Kind of seed | Examples added by the commentary
| **Mūlabīja** | Āluva, kaseru, kamala, uppala, puṇḍarīka, kuvalaya, kanda, pāṭali root and so on
| **Khandhabīja** | Ambāṭaka, indasāla, nuhī, pāḷibhaddaka, kaṇikāra and so on
| **Phaḷubīja** | Amūlavalli, caturassavalli, kaṇavīra and so on
| **Aggabīja** | Makaci, sumana, jayasumana and so on
| **Bījabīja** | Mango, rose-apple and jackfruit seeds and so on
## {att} “Bīje bījasaññī” — what does “seed” mean here?
Just as rice made from sāli grain is called “rice of sāli”, here **the plant life arisen from seed** is itself called “seed”. Or else (**atha vā**), read together with the first word of the rule’s analysis, “**bhūtagāmo nāma**”, the passage means:
%% Object | Regarding a seed that is plant life,
%% Perception | one who perceives it as seed (**bījasaññī**)
%% Cutting | and, taking a knife or the like, cuts it himself or has another cut it,
%% Breaking | or, taking a stone or the like, breaks it himself or has another break it,
%% Cooking | or, bringing fire to it, cooks it himself or has another cook it,
%% Result | falls into a **pācittiya**.
- A seed kept after being released from plant life (**bījagāma**) is **an object of dukkaṭa**. So one should not, taking the words literally, say that cutting a seed separated from plant life is a pācittiya.
- **In short:** destroying plant life → pācittiya; destroying any of the five kinds of seed-life released from plant life → dukkaṭa.
## {att} Plant life growing in water
All kinds of moss — mustard-sized, sesame-seed-sized and so on, with leaves or without — down to **udakapappaṭaka** (water scum: hard and rough-coloured above, soft and blue below) are to be regarded as plant life.
|! Case | Act | Offence
| Moss whose root is fixed in the ground (its place is the ground) | Destroying it anywhere, or pulling it up and moving it to another place | PC
| Moss floating in water (its place is the whole water) | Destroying it | PC
| Moss floating in water | Pushing it this way and that with the hands while bathing (not a change of place) | N
| Moss floating in water | Deliberately lifting it out of the water without water | Not allowed
| Moss floating in water | Lifting it up together with water and putting it back in the water | N
| Water with moss that passes through the strainer | Use it only after having it made allowable | —
| Water creepers and grasses such as water-lilies and lotuses | Pulling them out of the water, or destroying them right there | PC
| Water-lilies, lotuses etc. pulled out by others (they count as seed-life) | Destroying them | DK
| Sesame-seed or mustard-sized moss lifted out of water and not withered (counts as tip-seed) | Destroying it | DK
!src: The Mahāpaccarī and other commentaries call such things objects of dukkaṭa, and the Andhaka commentary says “dukkaṭa, because it is not complete plant life”; the Samantapāsādikā does not accept these: a third class, “incomplete plant life”, is found neither in the Pāli nor in the commentaries, and it is a principle of the Vinaya that “between the heavier and the lighter, one should stand by the heavier”. To say “it belongs to seed-life” does not fit either, since such seed-life does not have plant life as its root.
## {att} On land — stumps, banana, bamboo and planted stakes
|! Object | Condition | Counts as | Offence
| The green stump left of a cut tree — kakudha, karañja, piyaṅgu, jackfruit and others that grow upward | It grows upward | Plant life | PC
| Stumps of palmyra, coconut and the like | It does not grow upward | Seed-life | DK
| Stump of a banana plant that has not fruited | — | Plant life | PC
| Stump of a banana plant that has fruited | — | Seed-life | DK
| A banana plant or bamboo that has fruited | As long as the leaves are green | Plant life | PC
| A fruited banana or bamboo | Once it starts drying from the top (nothing grows from it) | Joint-seed — seed-life | DK
| Indasāla and other stakes cut and piled up | Even if branches a cubit long come out | Seed-life | DK
| Stakes planted in the ground for a pavilion, a fence or for creepers | When both roots and leaves have come out | Plant life again | PC
| Such planted stakes | When only roots or only leaves have come out | Seed-life | DK
## {att} Sprouting seeds — when do they become plant life?
Seeds kept on the ground and watered, or put in pots with moist soil, are still seeds even if only the root or only a leaf has come out. Even if roots and a shoot above have come out, they are seeds as long as the shoot is not green.
|! Seed | Counts as seed-life while | Becomes plant life when
| Mung beans etc. | The leaves have not risen | The leaves have risen
| Rice etc. | The shoot is not green | The shoot has turned blue-green like a leaf
| Palmyra seed | Even with a root like a boar’s tusk, as long as no leaf-stalk has come out above | The leaf-stalk comes out
| Coconut | Even with a shoot like a tooth-pick breaking through the husk, as long as there is no blue leaf-stalk like a deer’s horn | Such a leaf-stalk forms — even without a root (“rootless plant life”)
| Mango stones etc. | Judged in the same way as rice | As with rice

|! Object | Act / condition | Offence

| A rootless creeper winding round shrub stems like a finger-ring | Destroying it or removing it | PC

| Applying an oily coat over plaster laid by an unordained person | — | N
| Moss that has dried up in the hot season | Scraping it off with a broom etc. | N

| Moss on the **inside** of water pots etc. | Not reckoned (**abbohārika**) | N
| Mould (**kaṇṇaka**) on tooth-sticks, cakes etc. | Not reckoned (**abbohārika**) | N

## {att} Cutting, breaking, cooking — special cases
%% Chindati vā chedāpeti vā | Even sweeping the ground with a broom-stick with the intention “I will cut the grass”, he cuts it himself or has another cut it — an offence.
%% Bhindati vā bhedāpeti vā | Even while doing walking meditation, deliberately trampling, thinking “let what breaks break; I will show a walking path”, he breaks grass, creepers and the like himself or has another break them — an offence. If a grass or creeper breaks when knotted, one should not even tie a knot.
%% Pacati vā pacāpeti vā | Even wanting to bake a bowl, deliberately lighting a fire on grass and the like, he cooks it himself or has another cook it — an offence.
- **Against thieves** — fixing wooden spikes (**dārumakkaṭaka**) on palm trees or tying thorns on them so thieves cannot climb is not allowed for a monk; merely placing them so they touch without pressing the tree is allowed.
|! Saying | Ruling
| “Cut a tree, cut a creeper, dig up a tuber or root” | Allowed — not specified
| Naming it: “Cut / break / dig up a mango tree, the caturassa creeper, the āluva tuber, muñja grass, the bark of such-and-such tree” | Allowed — this too is unspecified
| “Cut **this** mango tree” and so on | Not allowed — this is specified
| “Cook mung beans, cook black gram” | Allowed
| “Cook **these** mung beans, cook **this** black gram” | Not allowed
## {att} With novices; branches and ginger
- One may bend down a branch for novices picking flowers; but one should not scent drinking water with those flowers. One who wishes to scent water should lift a novice up and have the flowers picked.
- One should not bend a fruit branch wishing to eat the fruit oneself; lift a novice up and have the fruit taken.
- One should not grab hold and pull together with novices who are uprooting a shrub or creeper; to encourage them, one may hold the tip, showing as if pulling without pulling.
- From trees whose branches take root when planted, if a branch taken for a fly-whisk etc. without being made allowable is scratched on its bark or leaf, even with a fingernail, it is **dukkaṭa**. The same for fresh ginger and the like.
- If ginger etc. made allowable and kept in a cool place sprouts roots, one may cut the upper part; if it sprouts a shoot, one may cut the lower part; if both roots and a green shoot have sprouted, it is not allowed.
## {att} Making allowable — further details
Making allowable is to be understood by this discourse: “Monks, I allow fruit to be eaten when made allowable by the five recluse’s allowances.”
|! Recluse’s allowance | Meaning
| **Aggiparijita** | Overcome by fire — burnt, touched by fire
| **Satthaparijita** | Overcome by a blade — cut or pierced
| **Nakhaparijita** | Overcome by a nail
| **Abīja** | Seedless — allowable by itself
| **Nibbaṭṭabīja** | With the seed removed — allowable by itself
- **Fire** — any fire, of wood, of cow-dung and so on, even a heated piece of iron.
- **Blade** — it should be a metal blade.
- **Nails** — it can be done with the sharp nails of humans, of lions, tigers, leopards, monkeys and the like, and of birds. The hooves of horses, buffaloes, pigs, deer, cattle and the like are not sharp, so they are not allowed; done with them, it is not done. Elephants’ nails are not hooves, so they are allowed. Nails still on the body or removed may both be used.
- When sugarcane and firewood are tied together, piercing the **rope** or creeper that binds them is not allowed.
- When a basket filled with sugarcane pieces is brought, making one piece allowable makes all of it allowable.
## {att} Explanation of the no-offence terms
|! Term | The commentary’s illustration
| “**Imaṃ jāna**” (“know this”) etc. | “Know this root medicine; give this root or leaf; bring this tree or creeper; this flower, fruit or leaf is needed; make this tree, creeper or fruit allowable” — this much effects **release from plant life**; when using it, one must have it made allowable again for **release from seed-life**.
| **Asañcicca** (unintentionally) | When rolling stones or logs, dragging a branch, or walking while striking the ground with a staff, grass gets cut — since he did not intend to cut it, no offence.
| **Assatiyā** (without mindfulness) | Absent-minded, talking with someone, he stands cutting grass or a creeper with his toe or hand — no offence.
| **Ajānantassa** (for one not knowing) | Not knowing that there is seed-life or plant life inside, nor that he is cutting, he puts a chisel or digging stick (**nikhādana**, **khaṇitti**) or a hoe (**kudāla**) into a fence or a straw heap for safe keeping; or, his hand being burnt, he drops a fire — if grass is cut or burnt there, no offence.
## {att} A special ruling — a monk trapped under a tree
- All the commentaries (in the explanation of the third pārājika) say: if a monk is pinned under a tree or has fallen into a pit, even if he could get out by cutting the tree or digging the ground, **he must not do it himself, even to save his life**. **Another monk** may dig the ground, cut the tree, or cut a stick from a living tree, roll the tree away and get him out — no offence.
- The Samantapāsādikā sees no reason for this. The only discourse found is: “Monks, when a forest fire is burning, I allow you to set a counter-fire and to make a firebreak” (Cūḷavagga). From this, the distinction “not allowed for oneself, allowed for another” cannot be derived.
- The reason “one doing it for himself acts from self-love with an unwholesome mind, while the other acts out of compassion” is not acceptable either, since this offence is incurred even with a wholesome mind (it is ticitta).
- But since it is stated in all the commentaries, it cannot be rejected. The reasoning should be sought, or it should be accepted out of faith in the teachers of the commentaries.
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> භූතගාමපාතබ්‍යතාය පාචිත්තියං.
This is **the first rule of the Bhūtagāma chapter** (**bhūtagāmavaggassa paṭhame**). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī begins by explaining the words of the rule:
|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Bhūta** | “They come to be and have come to be” — things that arise and grow, have arisen and have grown.
| **Gāma** | A mass, a heap.
| **Bhūtagāma** | A mass of bhūtas, or the bhūtas themselves as a mass — a name for **green grass, trees and so on that are rooted in place**.
| **Pātabyatā** | Being something to be used as one likes, by cutting, breaking and so on.
| **Pātabyatāya** | A locative in the sense of cause (**nimittatthe bhummavacanaṃ**) — “a pācittiya because of cutting and so on of plant life”.
## {kkv} The full meaning
%% Where it grows | A monk who, regarding plant life growing anywhere — on the ground, in water, on walls and so on —
%% Condition | that is not dried up (**asukkha**),
%% Size | ==even a very fine grass, or moss the size of a mustard seed==,
%% Act | destroys it by pulling it out, cutting it, piercing it and so on, or has another destroy it in the way stated in the **earth-digging rule** (by specifying “this place / this tree”),
%% Result | falls into a **pācittiya**.
## {kkv} The laying down of the rule and the command
%% Place | Laid down at Āḷavī,
%% Person | concerning the Āḷavaka monks,
%% Matter | **on the matter of cutting trees**, this rule
%% Kind of rule | is a **common rule** (**sādhāraṇa-paññatti**) — shared with the nuns;
%% Command | and it is **sāṇattika** — the offence also arises by getting another to do it.
## {kkv} Division of offences
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| Plant life (**bhūtagāma**) | Perceiving it as plant life, he destroys it by cutting, breaking, piercing, pulling out and so on, or has it destroyed | PC
| Seed-life (**bījagāma**) separated from plant life (root-, trunk-, joint-, tip- or seed-seed — in a container, in a heap, or planted in the ground) — when only the root or only the shoot has come out (even if a leaf-stalk a span long has come out), or, whether or not the root has come out, the shoot is not yet green | He destroys it or has it destroyed | DK
| Plant life or seed-life | He destroys it while in doubt | DK
| What is neither plant life nor seed-life | He destroys it perceiving it as plant life or seed-life | DK
| What is neither plant life nor seed-life | He destroys it while in doubt | DK
| Plant life or seed-life | He destroys it perceiving it as not so (**atathā-saññā**) | N
## {kkv} No offence
# For one who, regarding plant life or seed-life, wrongly perceives it as not so;
# For one who destroys it unintentionally, without mindfulness, or without knowing;
# For one who says “cut a tree, cut a creeper” and so on, without specifying “this tree”;
# For one who says “know this flower or fruit, give this, bring this, I need this, make this allowable”;
# For the insane and so on.
## {kkv} Making allowable (kappiya karaṇa)
Seed-kinds separated from plant life by an allowable word should also be used only after having them made allowable again by saying **“kappiyaṃ karohi”**; done in this way, it is also freed from being seed-life.
# Making allowable should be done **with fire, with a nail, or with a blade**.
# **With fire** — with any fire, touching it on one part and saying “kappiya”.
# **With a blade** — with any sharp blade, even a needle or nail-cutter, showing a piercing or a cut with its point or edge, and saying “kappiya”.
# **With a nail** — except the hooves of cattle, buffaloes and so on, with any human or animal nail that is not rotten (even one cut off and brought), in the same way as said for the blade.
# Even if there is a mountain-sized heap of seeds, a thousand trees cut and bound together, or a great load of sugarcane tied up, when one seed, one branch or one cane is made allowable, all is made allowable. Piercing a stick bound together with the sugarcane is fine; but piercing the creeper with which the load is tied is not.
# In rice mixed with peppers and so on, piercing even one grain of rice is fine; the same with sesame and rice grains. Things put into gruel are not held together, so each one must be pierced.
# In wood-apple (kapiṭṭha) fruits and the like, if the inner pulp moves free of the shell, have it broken and then make it allowable; if it is attached, it may be done on the shell.
# For a young fruit with no seed, or a fruit to be eaten after removing the seed, making allowable is **not needed**.
## {kkv} Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# It is plant life;
# Having the perception of plant life;
# Destroying it or having it destroyed.
## {kkv} Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
The origin and the rest are the same as in the earth-digging rule (**Pathavīkhaṇana-sikkhāpada**).

එක් සමයෙක බුදුරජාණන් වහන්සේ කොසඹෑ නුවර ඝෝසිතාරාමයෙහි වැඩ වසන සේක. එකල ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයෝ අනාචාරයක් හැසිර, සංඝ මධ්‍යයෙහි ඇවතින් විමසනු ලබන්නාහු ==අනෙකකින් අනෙකක් වසති== (**අඤ්ඤෙනඤ්ඤං පටිචරති**): “කවුරු (ඇවතට) පැමිණියේ ද? කුමකට පැමිණියේ ද? කුමක් කෙරෙහි පැමිණියේ ද? කෙසේ පැමිණියේ ද? කවරෙකු ගැන කියන්නහු ද? කුමක් කියන්නහු ද?” යනුවෙනි.
යම් ඒ අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු වූවාහු ද, ඔවුහු “ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයෝ සංඝ මධ්‍යයෙහි ඇවතින් විමසනු ලබන්නාහු කෙසේ නම් ‘කවුරු පැමිණියේ ද … කුමක් කියන්නහු ද’ යි අනෙකකින් අනෙකක් වසන්නාහු ද?” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ.
භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “ඡන්නය, ඔබ සංඝ මධ්‍යයෙහි ඇවතින් විමසනු ලබන්නේ ‘කවුරු පැමිණියේ ද … කුමක් කියන්නහු ද’ යි අනෙකකින් අනෙකක් වසන බව සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂය, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් සංඝ මධ්‍යයෙහි ඇවතින් විමසනු ලබන්නේ අනෙකකින් අනෙකක් වසන්නෙහි ද? මෝඝපුරුෂය, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා, දැහැමි කථාවක් කොට භික්ෂූන් ඇමතූ සේක: “එසේ නම් මහණෙනි, සංඝයා ඡන්න භික්ෂුවට ==**අඤ්ඤවාදක** (කර්මය) රෝපණය කෙරේවා==” යි වදාළ සේක. ඒ අනුව දක්ෂ, සමර්ථ භික්ෂුවක් **ඤත්තිදුතිය කර්මවාචාවකින්** සංඝයාට දැනුම් දී, සංඝයා ඡන්න භික්ෂුවට **අඤ්ඤවාදක කර්මය** රෝපණය කළේ ය.
ඉක්බිති භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයන්ට නොයෙක් අයුරින් ගරහා, පෝෂණයට අපහසු බවේ (**දුබ්භරතා**) අගුණ කියා … “මහණෙනි, මේ ශික්ෂාපදය මෙසේ උදෙසිය යුතුය” යි වදාළ සේක:
:::
!pn: මුල් පැනවීම
> අඤ්ඤවාදකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
~ සඟ මැද විචාළ දෙය හැර අනෙකක් කීමෙහි පචිති වේ.
### විහෙසක වස්තුව
:::short
පසුව “වසන්නේ නම් ඇවතට පැමිණෙමි” යි සිතා ඡන්නයෝ ==නිශ්ශබ්ද වී සංඝයා වෙහෙසූහ==. භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ නැවත ගරහා, **විහෙසක කර්මය** ද කරවා ශික්ෂාපදය සම්පූර්ණ කළ සේක.
:::
:::long
එකල ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයෝ සංඝ මධ්‍යයෙහි ඇවතින් විමසනු ලබන්නාහු, “අනෙකකින් අනෙකක් වසන්නේ නම් ඇවතට පැමිණෙන්නෙමි” යි (සලකා) ==නිශ්ශබ්ද වී සංඝයා වෙහෙසති== (**තුණ්හීභූතො සඞ්ඝං විහෙසෙති**).
අල්පේච්ඡ භික්ෂූහු “ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයෝ සංඝ මධ්‍යයෙහි ඇවතින් විමසනු ලබන්නාහු කෙසේ නම් නිශ්ශබ්ද වී සංඝයා වෙහෙසන්නාහු ද?” යි අවමන් කළහ, දොස් කීහ, නින්දා කළහ.
භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ “ඡන්නය, ඔබ සංඝ මධ්‍යයෙහි ඇවතින් විමසනු ලබන්නේ නිශ්ශබ්ද වී සංඝයා වෙහෙසන බව සැබෑ ද?” යි විචාළ සේක. “සැබෑ ය, භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්ස” යි කී කල්හි, “මෝඝපුරුෂය, ඔබ කෙසේ නම් සංඝ මධ්‍යයෙහි ඇවතින් විමසනු ලබන්නේ නිශ්ශබ්ද වී සංඝයා වෙහෙසන්නෙහි ද? මෝඝපුරුෂය, මෙය නොපැහැදුණවුන්ගේ පැහැදීම පිණිස නොවේ …” යි ගරහා, දැහැමි කථාවක් කොට භික්ෂූන් ඇමතූ සේක: “එසේ නම් මහණෙනි, සංඝයා ඡන්න භික්ෂුවට ==**විහෙසක** (කර්මය) රෝපණය කෙරේවා==” යි වදාළ සේක. ඒ අනුව දක්ෂ, සමර්ථ භික්ෂුවක් **ඤත්තිදුතිය කර්මවාචාවකින්** සංඝයාට දැනුම් දී, සංඝයා ඡන්න භික්ෂුවට **විහෙසක කර්මය** රෝපණය කළේ ය.
ඉක්බිති භාග්‍යවතුන් වහන්සේ ආයුෂ්මත් ඡන්නයන්ට නොයෙක් අයුරින් ගරහා, පෝෂණයට අපහසු බවේ (**දුබ්භරතා**) අගුණ කියා … “මහණෙනි, මේ ශික්ෂාපදය මෙසේ උදෙසිය යුතුය” යි වදාළ සේක:
:::
!pn: අනුපඤ්ඤත්තිය — අවසන් පැනවීම (සම්පූර්ණ ශික්ෂාපදය)
> අඤ්ඤවාදකෙ විහෙසකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
~ සඟ මැද විචාළ දෙය හැර අනෙකක් කීමෙහි ද, නිහඬව හිඳීමෙන් සංඝයා වෙහෙස කරවීමෙහි ද පචිති වේ.

## {story} The Origin Story
### The Matter of Evasive Speech (Aññavādaka-vatthu)
@ Kosambī · Ghositārāma
:::short
Ven. Channa, having misbehaved and being questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha, ==covered one thing with another==: “Who has fallen into it? What? In what? How? …” When the monks of few wishes reported it, the Blessed One rebuked him, had the Saṅgha impose the **act of evasive speech** on him, and laid down the rule.
:::
:::long
At one time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Kosambī, in the Ghositārāma. At that time Ven. Channa, having misbehaved, was being questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha, and ==he covered one thing with another== (**aññenaññaṃ paṭicarati**): “Who has fallen into it? What has he fallen into? In what has he fallen? How has he fallen? Whom are you speaking of? What are you saying?”
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can Ven. Channa, when questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha, cover one thing with another — ‘Who has fallen into it? … What are you saying?’”
The Blessed One asked: “Is it true, Channa, that when questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha you cover one thing with another — ‘Who has fallen into it? … What are you saying?’” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked him: “Foolish man, how can you, when questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha, cover one thing with another? Foolish man, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” Having given a Dhamma talk, he addressed the monks: “Well then, monks, ==let the Saṅgha impose **evasive speech** (aññavādaka) on the monk Channa==.” Accordingly, a competent and capable monk informed the Saṅgha by a **formal act of one motion and one announcement** (**ñattidutiya-kammavācā**), and the Saṅgha imposed the **act of evasive speech** on the monk Channa.
Then the Blessed One, having rebuked Ven. Channa in many ways, spoke against being hard to support (**dubbharatā**) … and said: “Monks, you should recite this training rule like this:”
:::
!pn: The original rule
> අඤ්ඤවාදකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
~ Evasive speech: Pācittiya.
### The Matter of Causing Frustration (Vihesaka-vatthu)
:::short
Later Channa, thinking, “If I evade I will fall into an offence,” ==stayed silent and frustrated the Saṅgha==. The Blessed One rebuked him again, had the **act of causing frustration** imposed as well, and completed the rule.
:::
:::long
At that time Ven. Channa, when questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha, thinking, “If I cover one thing with another I will fall into an offence,” ==stayed silent and frustrated the Saṅgha== (**tuṇhībhūto saṅghaṃ viheseti**).
The monks of few wishes complained, criticised and spread it about: “How can Ven. Channa, when questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha, stay silent and frustrate the Saṅgha?”
The Blessed One asked: “Is it true, Channa, that when questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha you stay silent and frustrate the Saṅgha?” “It is true, Blessed One.” He rebuked him: “Foolish man, how can you, when questioned about an offence in the midst of the Saṅgha, stay silent and frustrate the Saṅgha? Foolish man, this does not inspire faith in those without faith …” Having given a Dhamma talk, he addressed the monks: “Well then, monks, ==let the Saṅgha impose **causing frustration** (vihesaka) on the monk Channa==.” Accordingly, a competent and capable monk informed the Saṅgha by a **formal act of one motion and one announcement** (**ñattidutiya-kammavācā**), and the Saṅgha imposed the **act of causing frustration** on the monk Channa.
Then the Blessed One, having rebuked Ven. Channa in many ways, spoke against being hard to support (**dubbharatā**) … and said: “Monks, you should recite this training rule like this:”
:::
!pn: Supplementary rule — the final rule (complete training rule)
> අඤ්ඤවාදකෙ විහෙසකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
~ Evasive speech and harassment: Pācittiya.
## {story} Word analysis — aññavādaka and vihesaka
### Aññavādaka (evasive speaker)
%% Place | One who, in the midst of the Saṅgha,
%% Questioning | being questioned about a matter or about an offence,
%% Intention | not wishing to tell it, not wishing to reveal it (**ugghāṭetu**),
%% Act | covers one thing with another — “Who has fallen into it? What has he fallen into? In what has he fallen? How has he fallen? Whom are you speaking of? What are you saying?” —
%% Name | is called **aññavādaka** (an evasive speaker).
### Vihesaka (one who causes frustration)
%% Place | One who, in the midst of the Saṅgha,
%% Questioning | being questioned about a matter or about an offence,
%% Intention | not wishing to tell it, not wishing to reveal it,
%% Act | stays silent (**tuṇhībhūto**) and frustrates the Saṅgha,
%% Name | is called **vihesaka** (one who causes frustration).
## {story} Offences — whether the act has been imposed or not
|! Act of the Saṅgha | What he does (questioned in the Saṅgha about a matter or offence, not wishing to tell or reveal it) | Offence
| Evasive speech **not** yet imposed | Covers one thing with another | DK
| Causing frustration **not** yet imposed | Stays silent and frustrates the Saṅgha | DK
| Evasive speech **has been** imposed | Covers one thing with another | PC
| Causing frustration **has been** imposed | Stays silent and frustrates the Saṅgha | PC
!src: Textual note: in this Pāli edition the dukkaṭa clauses are printed “**āropite**”. But the Pācittiya commentary reads the word as “**aropite** — not imposed by the formal announcement (kammavācāya anāropite)”, and the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī says “**anāropite**”; so the meaning is “when it has not been imposed”.
## {story} Offences — by the act and by perception
|! Act | Perception and act | Offence
| A legitimate act (**dhammakamma**) | Perceiving it as legitimate, he speaks evasively / causes frustration | PC
| A legitimate act | Doing so while in doubt | PC
| A legitimate act | Doing so perceiving it as illegitimate | PC
| An illegitimate act (**adhammakamma**) | Doing so perceiving it as legitimate | DK
| An illegitimate act | Doing so while in doubt | DK
| An illegitimate act | Doing so perceiving it as illegitimate | DK
## {story} No offence
# He asks because he does not know (**ajānanto pucchati**);
# Being ill, he does not speak (**gilāno vā na katheti**);
# He does not speak, thinking, “There will be a dispute, a quarrel, a contention or an argument in the Saṅgha”;
# He does not speak, thinking, “There will be a schism or a rift in the Saṅgha”;
# He does not speak, thinking, “(The Saṅgha) will carry out an act unlawfully, or by a faction, or against one who does not deserve an act”;
# For one who is insane (**ummattakassa**);
# For the first offender (**ādikammikassa**).
## {att} Points on words in the origin story
|! Word | Samantapāsādikā explanation
| **Anācāraṃ ācaritvā** (having misbehaved) | Having done what should not be done — that is, having fallen into an offence through the doors of body and speech.
| **Aññenaññaṃ paṭicarati** | He covers one word with another word, conceals it, overwhelms it (**ajjhottharati**). Showing the way he covered it, “Who has fallen into it?” and so on is said.
| **Aññavādakaṃ ropetu** | Let it impose evasive speech — that is, let it establish it (**patiṭṭhāpetu**). The same method applies to “**vihesakaṃ ropetu**”.
## {att} The sequence of six questions — how Channa turned the talk aside
When some transgression of his had been seen and he was questioned in the midst of the Saṅgha, the commentary connects each reply by which he turned the talk aside:
|! What the monks said | Channa’s reply
| “Friend, you have fallen into an offence.” | “**Who** has fallen into it?”
| “You, then.” | “**What** have I fallen into?”
| “A pācittiya or a dukkaṭa.” | (asking about the matter) “**In what** have I fallen?”
| “In such-and-such a matter.” | “**How** have I fallen? Doing what did I fall?”
| “You fell doing this.” | “**Whom** are you speaking of?”
| “We are speaking of you.” | “**What** are you saying?”
## {att} Ways of evading not given in the Pāli (pāḷimuttaka)
It should be understood that one also covers one thing with another in ways like these, not stated in the Pāli:
|! The monks’ charge | How he covers it
| “A kahāpaṇa coin was seen in your purse (**sipāṭikā**); why do you do something so unsuitable?” | “Well seen, venerable sir — but it is not a kahāpaṇa; it is a tin disc (**tipumaṇḍala**).”
| “You were seen drinking alcohol; why do you do this?” | “Well seen, venerable sir — but it is not alcohol; it is **ariṭṭha** prepared as medicine.”
| “You were seen sitting with a woman on a concealed seat; why do you do something so unsuitable?” | “Whoever saw it saw it well — but there was a wise second person there; why was he not seen?”
| Asked, “Did you see anything like this?” | He turns his ear, saying, “I can’t hear.”
| To those asking through the ear-door (**sotadvāra**) | He turns his eye towards them.
## {att} “Aropite” — the reading and its meaning
%% Aropite aññavādake | “Aropite aññavādake” means: when evasive speech has not been imposed (**anāropite**) by the formal announcement (**kammavācāya**);
%% Aropite vihesake | the same method applies to causing frustration.
!src: Though this Pāli edition prints “āropite”, the commentary’s word is “**aropite**” and it explains it as “**anāropite**”; so the dukkaṭa applies when the act has not been imposed.
## {att} “Dhammakamme dhammakammasaññī” — the meaning
%% Act | If the act imposing evasive speech and causing frustration that was carried out is a legitimate act,
%% Perception | and that monk, perceiving it as legitimate (**dhammakammasaññī**),
%% Act | speaks evasively and causes frustration,
%% Result | then for that evasive speech and causing frustration he incurs a **pācittiya** — the remaining terms should be understood in the same way.
## {att} Explanation of the no-offence terms
|! Term | The commentary’s explanation
| **Ajānanto pucchati** | Not knowing the offence, or not knowing that he has fallen into it, he asks, “What are you saying? I do not know.”
| **Gilāno vā na katheti** | He has such an illness in his mouth that he is unable to speak.
| **Saṅghassa bhaṇḍanaṃ vā** etc. | He does not speak, thinking, “If it is told in the midst of the Saṅgha, because of it there will be a dispute, a quarrel or an argument in the Saṅgha; may that not happen.” The rest is clear.
## {att} Sometimes by doing, sometimes by not doing — why
- For one who covers one thing with another (**aññavādaka**), the offence arises by doing (**kiriyā**).
- For one who frustrates by silence (**vihesaka**), the offence arises by not doing (**akiriyā**).
## {kkv} The rule and the meaning of its words
> අඤ්ඤවාදකෙ, විහෙසකෙ පාචිත්තියං.
This is **the second rule of the Bhūtagāma chapter** (**dutiye**). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī begins by explaining the words of the rule:
|! Word | Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explanation
| **Aññavādaka** | Whatever matter the Vinaya expert asks about in the midst of the Saṅgha, he says something other than that — hence aññavādaka; a name for covering one thing with another (**aññenaññaṃ paṭicaraṇa**).
| **Vihesaka** | He frustrates — hence vihesaka; a name for silence (**tuṇhībhāva**).
| **Aññavādake vihesake** | In that (**tasmiṃ**) evasive speech and in that causing frustration.
| **Pācittiyaṃ** | ==Two pācittiyas for the two matters== (**vatthudvaye pācittiyadvayaṃ**) are stated.
## {kkv} The full meaning
%% Offence | A monk who has fallen into ==a curable offence (**sāvasesa**)== — that is, one other than a pārājika —
%% Questioning | and, being questioned in the midst of the Saṅgha,
%% Intention | not wishing to tell it,
%% Aññavādaka | covers one thing with other words and turns the talk aside this way and that (**vikkhipati**),
%% Vihesaka | or who frustrates by silence —
%% The act | when the Saṅgha has carried out against them the **act of evasive speech** and the **act of causing frustration** allowed by the Blessed One,
%% Result | there is a **pācittiya** for those who do so again.
## {kkv} The laying down of the rule and the command
%% Place | Laid down at Kosambī,
%% Person | concerning the Elder Channa,
%% Matter | **on the matter of covering one thing with another** (**aññenaññaṃ paṭicaraṇa-vatthu**), this rule
%% Kind of rule | is a **common rule** (**sādhāraṇa-paññatti**) — shared with the nuns;
%% Command | and it is **anāṇattika** — no offence arises by commanding another.
## {kkv} Division of offences
|! Object | Perception and act | Offence
| Evasive speech / causing frustration imposed by a legitimate act | Doing so perceiving it as legitimate, in doubt, or perceiving it as illegitimate (the triad) | PC
| Evasive speech / causing frustration imposed by an illegitimate act | Doing so perceiving it as legitimate, in doubt, or perceiving it as illegitimate (the triad) | DK
| Evasive speech or causing frustration not imposed (**anāropita**) | Covering one thing with another, or frustrating by silence | DK
## {kkv} No offence
# For one who, not knowing the offence or that he has fallen into it, asks, “What are you saying?”;
# For one who does not speak because of illness;
# For one who does not speak with the thought, “There will be disputes and so on in the Saṅgha”;
# For one who does not speak with the thought, “They will carry out an act unlawfully, by a faction, or against one who does not deserve an act”;
# For the insane and so on.
## {kkv} Factors (= tīṇi — three)
# The act has been imposed by a legitimate act;
# Being questioned about an offence or about a matter;
# Wishing to hide it, covering one thing with another or staying silent.
## {kkv} Origin and the rest (samuṭṭhāna)
The origin and the rest are the same as in the rule on taking what is not given (**Adinnādāna-sikkhāpada**). But this one arises **sometimes by doing, sometimes by not doing**, and its feeling is **painful** (**dukkha-vedanā**).
